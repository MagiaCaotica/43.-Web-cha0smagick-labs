Celtic Cross Tarot Spread: All 10 Positions Explained

Ten cards, one centre axis and a staff of four. The Celtic Cross is the most-used deep reading in tarot, and it is also the most frequently misremembered. Here is every position, what it actually answers, and how to read the pairs.

The ten positions

Shuffle, cut, and lay the cards in this order. Positions 1 to 3 form the horizontal arm, positions 1 to 6 the vertical staff.

  1. Present. The heart of the matter, the core of the situation right now. If one card could answer the question, this is it.
  2. Crossing. What cuts across the present: the obstacle, the tension, the thing working against you. Often internal.
  3. Foundation. The root, below the conscious surface. What is actually generating the situation.
  4. Recent past. The energy that is leaving, usually fading within days or weeks.
  5. Crown. The conscious goal, the conscious intention, what you are aiming at on purpose.
  6. Near future. The next development, arriving soon.
  7. Self. Your own attitude and role in the situation, as you are currently holding it.
  8. Environment. Other people, external influences, what everyone else brings to it.
  9. Hopes and fears. Often the same card, or a pair that contradicts itself. This position is where you are most likely to be honest with yourself.
  10. Outcome. Where this is heading if nothing changes. Conditional, not fixed.

How to read the pairs, and why it matters

Most bad Celtic Cross readings read ten cards in isolation. The spread is designed to be read in pairs, and that is where the structure lives.

  • 1 and 2 (Present and Crossing) is the central conflict. Present against obstacle. The tension of the situation in one line.
  • 3 and 4 (Foundation and Past) is the cause. What is buried under what just happened. Read 3 first, then 4 as its consequence.
  • 5 and 6 (Crown and Future) is the trajectory. Conscious intention meeting probable arrival.
  • 1 and 10 (Present and Outcome) is the whole reading in two cards: where you are versus where it ends. If these two are the same card, the situation is already resolved, in one direction or the other.
  • 7, 8 and 9 are the moving part: your attitude, the outside influence, and the hidden motive. Read as a conversation between three people.

The staff of four on the right is the staff in many traditional cartomancy layouts. It is read from the bottom up, 4 to 1, which is why it is sometimes called the ascending staff.

Before you start: the four questions that ruin the reading

  1. Asking a yes/no question and then reading all ten cards. A yes/no reading is one card. If you want depth, ask an open question.
  2. Laying it out with no question. The cards answer something. If you do not know what, the reading will feel unfocused and you will invent meaning to compensate.
  3. Reversals without a decision. Decide whether you are reading reversals before you shuffle, not while reading card seven.
  4. Interpreting position 10 as fate. It is a conditional outcome, not a sentence. It describes the path that is open while the present holds.

Celtic Cross questions

What does the Celtic Cross spread have 10 positions for?

Ten cards answer more than one question: the present situation, its obstacle, its root, its direction, your own role, outside forces, hidden motives and the likely outcome, all in one layout.

Which cards are read first in a Celtic Cross?

Read the pairs, not the cards in isolation. Positions one and two for the central conflict, three and four for the cause, five and six for the trajectory, and one against ten for the whole arc.

Do I need a physical deck to do a Celtic Cross?

No. The free spread builder on this site sets out the layout and keeps your notes next to each position, and there are free card reference tools for meanings.

What are the three cards in the middle of the Celtic Cross?

Position seven is the Self, position eight is the Environment and position nine is Hopes and Fears. Read them as a conversation between three parties.

Should I read reversed cards in a Celtic Cross?

Either way, but decide before you shuffle. Reversals add a layer of tension and are more useful for question-shaped readings than for yes/no ones.

Is the staff on the right read separately?

Yes. It is the staff of four from traditional cartomancy and is read from the bottom up, position four to position one.

How long should a Celtic Cross reading take?

Twenty to thirty minutes unhurried is enough for the pairs. The mistake is reading all ten cards in isolation, which doubles the length and loses the structure.
