The I Ching is three thousand years old and works with three coins. Some of the best tools for it are free and run in a browser. This is what each option is actually for, and when paying for one is worth it.

What an I Ching app is supposed to do

Strip away the ornament and an I Ching tool has exactly four jobs: cast, interpret, track changing lines and remember what you read before. Everything else in the category is decoration.

Casting is trivial arithmetic: three coins, six tosses, six lines, one hexagram. Any competent program does it correctly. The parts that actually distinguish a good tool from a bad one are the interpretation text and the history, because those are the two things you cannot do in your head at 2am.

How to cast the three coins correctly

Quick reference, because the method is simple and easy to get subtly wrong.

  1. Hold three coins of one denomination. Heads is 3, tails is 2.
  2. Toss all three. The total is 6, 7, 8 or 9. Six and nine are yin lines, seven and eight are yang lines. A 6 is a changing yin line and a 9 is a changing yang line.
  3. Repeat six times. The first toss is the bottom line, the sixth is the top line.
  4. Build the lower trigram from lines 1 to 3 and the upper trigram from lines 4 to 6. That is your primary hexagram.
  5. If any line was 6 or 9, flip those lines to their opposite and that is your changing into hexagram. The changing lines are where the reading lives.
  6. Read from the bottom line upward, as written. Read the changing lines first if they exist.

The I Ching does not need an app. It needs three coins and somewhere to write down what you saw.
