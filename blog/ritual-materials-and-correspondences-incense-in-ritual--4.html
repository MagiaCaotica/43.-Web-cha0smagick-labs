Ritual Materials and Correspondences

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 27, 2026 ⬢ 11 min read

The tables get a bad reputation they did not earn. They are treated as either a code for talking to angels or a list of arbitrary assignments, and both readings mistake them for a claim when they are closer to a filing system with a consistency rule attached. What they are is an organising principle: planets linked to metals, colours, numbers, days, and hours, so that a set of instruments can be made to agree with itself. That is a design constraint, not a mechanism, and the confusion between those two readings is what makes people either over-credit the tables or dismiss them. There is one part of this material with a documented mechanism underneath it, and it is not the planetary part. It is the smell. Olfactory memory is unusually durable and largely independent of verbal memory, which means a distinctive scent is a far better anchor than a distinctive word, and every practical text in this tradition that includes a burned substance knew something about that long before anybody had a name for it. The rest of the tables are worth learning and worth using as bookkeeping. The legal position on the plant you keep hearing about is a real constraint and not a symbolic one.

What the tables are actually for

You need to choose an hour, a day, a colour, a metal, a number, and a substance, and the tables tell you which of those go together. That is the whole job. The reason this is worth having is that the choices are not independent, and a set chosen at random from six dimensions will be incoherent in ways that are hard to notice and easy to feel. A set chosen by the tables is coherent by construction.

The failure mode is reading the coherence as causation. The tables say that this colour and this substance and this hour are in a relationship. They do not say the relationship does anything, and no version of the tables has ever been tested for that. A table that says copper belongs with the sun and the hour of the sun is a description of a group. Whether the group has any power is a separate question, and the separate question is the interesting one and is not answered by the table.

Where they come from

The organising principle runs through the ceremonial tradition of western Europe, and it reached its most systematic printed form in the third book of a three-volume synthesis published in 1533, which set out planetary, angelic, and demonic hierarchies together in a way that had not been done in print before. That volume remains the primary source for most of the tables in circulation, which means most of what you are consulting is five centuries old and was compiled before anybody had a way to check it.

That age is worth holding in mind in a particular way. These are early modern tables, produced by people with strong interests, no experimental method, and a great deal of time. They are a document of a way of organising, and their value to a modern practitioner is as a vocabulary with a consistent grammar. It is not evidence about the world. The five-hundred-year gap is a feature rather than a bug, because a grammar that has stayed usable for five centuries has proved more durable than any claim attached to it.

The organising idea, in one sentence

Everything in this material is linked to a planet, and the planets are linked to days and hours, and the days and hours give you a sequence you can locate yourself inside. That is the sentence, and every table in the tradition is an instance of it. Metals, colours, numbers, stones, plants, and substances are assigned to planets. Days and hours are assigned to planets. The result is a system in which a practitioner can work out what the current hour is doing and choose accordingly.

This is worth understanding as a coordination problem rather than a cosmology. Given six or seven dimensions of choice, you will pick inconsistently unless you have a rule. The tables are the rule. Their historical form grew out of a genuinely cosmological worldview, in which those links were supposed to be real, and modern practitioners retain the rule while being agnostic or sceptical about the claim. That combination is slightly odd to state aloud and it is the position the material actually occupies.

Why consistency is the point

A coherent set is easier to hold in mind, and holding it in mind is most of what the material claims to do. When the colour, the metal, the number, and the hour all agree, the act of assembling them is not difficult, and a difficult act requires attention that would otherwise be spent on whatever you actually came to do. The consistency is doing ergonomic work.

The second reason is less flattering and more useful. An incoherent set produces a feeling of something having gone wrong, and practitioners use that feeling as evidence. The information content of the feeling is near zero, because incoherence is detected much more easily than a working's effect, and the detection threshold is set by how much you want a result. If your set is coherent you have removed one whole category of false signal, which is worth considerably more than the coherence is likely to cost you.

What the tables do not claim

They do not claim that the planetary assignments are discoveries about the physical world. They do not claim that a particular colour produces a particular result. And they certainly do not license the large number of readings in circulation in which a table is treated as a chart of a person's psychology, which is a modern addition that would have been unrecognisable to the people who compiled the originals.

The one thing they do claim, in the only form in which the claim is coherent, is that the groupings are traditional and that the tradition has a logic. That is a much smaller claim and it is entirely supportable. The interesting move for a modern practitioner is to keep the traditional groupings on their own terms, use them as a coordination system, and decline the modern additions. You lose nothing that the source material contained and you avoid a set of claims that were never made by anyone who could be held to them.

The one part with a documented mechanism

Olfactory memory. It is unusually durable, it is largely independent of verbal memory, and it is one of the few memory systems that is not primarily visual. The practical consequence for this material is direct: a distinctive, repeated, unusual smell is a better anchor for a state or a sequence than a distinctive word, and the traditions that include a burned substance were using an effective technology before anybody had a name for it.

The mechanism is not mysterious and it is not mystical. An unusual olfactory input in a repeated context becomes a discriminative cue, and cues acquire their power from being consistent rather than from being powerful. The same is true of a word, a gesture, and a time, and the difference is that the smell is a channel with fewer competing uses in most people's lives. You can find a phrase elsewhere in your day. You are less likely to find that particular smell elsewhere in your day, which is why the anchor holds.

Why a distinctive smell works as an anchor

Two properties, both mundane and both sufficient. The first is selectivity: a blend that nobody in your building uses is discriminative in a way that a favourite piece of music is not, because music is shared with other parts of the day. The second is temporal pairing: if the smell appears in one context repeatedly and nowhere else, it becomes a reliable predictor of that context, and reliable predictors acquire a hold that a pleasant stimulus alone will not.

The operational advice that follows is narrow and I would follow it exactly. One substance, or one blend, used for one class of activity. Not varied. If you find yourself choosing a different substance for a different working, you are making an unnecessary decision at the moment you least want one, and you are weakening a cue that took weeks to build. The tradition's specificity here is not occultism, it is good experimental design, and it costs nothing to copy.

Building a set that agrees with itself

Pick an hour from a current table, take everything the table assigns to that hour's planet, and use those. Colour, metal, number, and substance all come from the same row, which takes the coordination problem out of your hands entirely. Then add one thing the tables do not mention, which is whatever you are actually there to do, and treat it as the only part of the set that requires judgement.

This produces a working that is coherent by construction, costs almost nothing to assemble, and takes about ten minutes. It is repeatable, which matters more than people expect, because a repeatable working can be run several times and compared, and a working assembled fresh each time cannot. Everything in this tradition that claims to have found something, and has any credibility, did so by running the same procedure repeatedly with a record. The set is not the interesting variable. The repetition is.

The column you should not trust

The powers, or the effects, or whatever column in your version lists what each planet does for you. These are the most quoted part of the tables and the least supported, and they are where the modern material has added the most. A list of what a planet governs is descriptive of a cultural vocabulary and often obviously so: everything gets a row, including the things we would explain any other way.

What I would trust is the columns that describe groupings rather than effects. Which metals went with which planet is a historical fact about a table, verifiable, and stable. What a planet does for your love life is a modern reading, added by somebody with an interest in your love life. The distinction is available to anybody who looks at a table and asks whether a column is describing the source or describing the reader, and the answer is usually clear. Using the first kind and skipping the second costs nothing and removes most of what makes this material sound like nonsense.

The legal question, which is real

The plant that appears in this material has documented use across several religious and magical traditions, including Hindu, Rastafarian, and folk-Christian settings. It is genuinely part of the historical record and not a modern invention, and a practitioner reading history will encounter it as an ordinary item in an ordinary list.

The complication is that its legal status varies sharply between jurisdictions and changes. That is not a symbolic constraint, it is an operational one, and it is worth resolving before a practice rather than after. The practical approach is to know your own position clearly and to build a set that does not depend on the resolution, since the tables assign far more substances than the one in question and several of them are unregulated, unavailable, or simply unpleasant. A practice that depends on a contested substance is a practice with a second, non-magical failure mode.

Where correspondences mislead

Three specific places. The first is when the table is used as a chart of a person, which is a modern addition and a psychological reading of a cosmological list. The second is when the coherence of a set is read as evidence that the working was effective, which is the ergonomic value of consistency being mistaken for a result.

The third and worst is when a correspondence is used to support a claim about the physical world. The tables say copper and the sun are related in a particular scheme. They do not say anything about copper, and the schemes were not designed to. The discipline that prevents all three is the same one: ask whether the column is describing the source or describing the reader, and if the answer is the reader, put it down. The tables are a filing system. A filing system that starts answering questions about the world has been misused, and the misuse is easy to spot in retrospect.

The sheet worth having

One page. The planetary groupings for the seven classical bodies across the dimensions you actually use, which is probably hour, day, colour, metal, and number. A note next to each row saying which column is descriptive and which is an effect claim, so you know what you are reading. And one line naming the substance you will use, fixed, with the date you chose it.

That last line is the one I would not have put there two years ago, and it is the one that does the most work. Every tradition in this field that includes a burned substance specified it, and the specificity reads as occult until you notice what it is doing, which is fixing a cue so that the sequence can be learned. The tables give you the coherence. The fixed substance gives you the anchor. Between them you have a repeatable working that costs nothing, and you did not need a single claim about planetary power to build it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do ritual correspondences actually work?

The groupings are historical facts about a body of tables, verifiable and stable, and using them makes a set of instruments agree with itself, which is a real and practical benefit. The effects columns, which say what each planet does for you, are descriptive of a cultural vocabulary and often modern additions, and nothing in the tables has been tested as a causal claim. A reasonable position is to use the groupings on their own terms as a coordination system and decline the effects claims, which costs nothing and removes most of what makes the material sound like nonsense.

Why does a fixed incense work better than a varied one?

Because a cue acquires its power from consistency rather than from pleasantness, and an unusual smell has fewer competing uses in a day than almost any other stimulus. One substance for one class of activity, repeated, becomes a reliable predictor of that context, and reliable predictors hold. If you switch substances between workings you are making a decision at the moment you least want one and weakening a cue that took weeks to build. The tradition's specificity here is good experimental design rather than occultism.

Is it illegal to use the plant in these workings?

Its legal status varies sharply between jurisdictions and changes, which makes it an operational constraint rather than a symbolic one. Resolve your own position before building a practice around it, and then build a set that does not depend on the resolution, since the tables assign many substances and several are unregulated or simply unpleasant. A practice with a contested substance in it has a second failure mode that has nothing to do with whether the practice works.

Do I need all the dimensions, or can I start small?

Start with hour, colour, and number, which is enough to have a coherent set, and add the rest only if you want to. The coordination problem is solved as soon as everything you use agrees with everything else you use, and three dimensions agree as well as seven. What matters is that the set is fixed across repeats, because a repeatable procedure is what makes a comparison possible, and a working assembled fresh each time cannot be compared to anything.

Why is Agrippa still the reference?

Because the 1533 synthesis was the first major printed western compilation of planetary, angelic, and demonic hierarchies together, and most of the tables in circulation descend from it. That makes it a primary source for the tables rather than an authority about the world. Its age is useful rather than disqualifying: a grammar that has stayed usable for five centuries has proved more durable than any claim attached to it, which is not true of most systems of belief with the same half-life.
