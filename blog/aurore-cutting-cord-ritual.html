AURORE

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 27, 2026 ⬢ 9 min read

AURORE has no entry in any historical catalogue and this article does not pretend otherwise. What is worth examining is the rite the name is attached to, because cord cutting is one of the few operations in Western esotericism that is genuinely initiatory rather than reconstructed. It appears in eighteenth-century kabbalistic material and in later ceremonial work, it requires a physical cord and a physical cut, and it carries a warning that appears across the literature in almost the same words: a bond severed by force re-forms. This is the structure, the protocol, and the honest cost.

No catalogue entry for this name

AURORE does not appear in the Ars Goetia, the Lemuria, the Pseudepigrapha, the Agrippa trilogy, the Grimoirs, or any published order record. It is not a demonological name, a saint, or a historical figure, and the same is true of most names circulating in this category. Any biography attached to it was written after the fact and should be read as invention rather than as history.

A constructed name is still usable, since a cord-cutting rite does not require the entity to be real in order to be performed. What would not be usable is the fiction that this name carries an inherited authority. A rite with an eight-hundred-year documentary trail and a made-up name attached to it is honest practice. The same rite with an invented lineage attached to it is decoration, and people carry grief around for years on the strength of a decoration.

Cord cutting as a genuinely initiatory rite

This operation appears in Eleazar ben Pitcharubut's Bahir ha-Levavot, an eighteenth-century kabbalistic text, and in later ceremonial material that draws on it. The ritual of the Qabalists is generally counted as one of the few Western esotericism practices that predates the Victorian revival rather than being reconstructed during it, which matters because reconstruction tends to produce rites that are internally decorative. This one has a source you can read.

Its structure is also unusual in that it is a rite of severance rather than of acquisition. Almost all initiatory operations in this tradition are about gaining access, binding, or establishing a relationship. Cord cutting is about undoing something, and it is usually transmitted as something to be done carefully and once rather than freely and often.

The cord, the cut, and the abstract mapping

The rite uses a physical cord representing the bond, which is cut or burned. That concreteness is the point. A concrete action maps onto an abstract one in a way that spoken intention alone does not, because the body is involved and the sequence has a terminus you can see. People who have performed the rite consistently report that the cutting is the part that lands, not the words.

Where the cord comes from matters less than the fact that it exists and is held during the sequence. Cord from a ritual supply, cord from the place the situation occurred, cord braided while you held the intention, each of these is defensible. What is not defensible is performing a rite about cutting a bond while holding no cord, since that reduces the operation to a speech and loses the only part of it that reliably works.

The warning that forced bonds re-form

Across the occult literature runs the claim that a bond severed by force returns, and this is the origin of the warning that appears in almost every tradition touching this material: do it properly or expect to do it twice. The same idea appears as an injunction against anger, and as a claim that a severance performed in rage fails while one performed in clarity succeeds.

Whether or not any bond exists to re-form, the warning has an observable mechanism. A severance performed while furious performs the act and leaves the reason intact, so the situation that produced the attachment is unchanged and the next contact re-establishes the pattern. The ritual is a marker, and the marker records whatever state you were in when you cut.

The modern adaptation into a severance statement

Current practice recasts the cut as an imperative statement and sigilises it, which follows the standard contemporary method rather than the historical rite. Sever this. Release this. I am no longer available for this. The compression technique is the same one used for any other working, and the reason it has become dominant is practical rather than traditional, since it avoids handling a blade.

This version is more accessible and less initiatory. It loses the cord, the cut, and the physical terminus, and with them most of the mechanism the historical rite depends on. If you use the sigil form, treat it as a marker rather than an operation, and put the clarity requirement into it deliberately, because the sigil will not supply that for you.

Deciding whether the bond should be cut

Most of the difficulty in this material is upstream of the rite. People perform severances on bonds that should be renegotiated, and on bonds that were never binding in the first place, and on situations that require a conversation rather than an operation. Before any of it, write one paragraph describing what the bond is, what you want instead, and what specifically changes in your life afterwards.

If the answer to the third question is nothing, there is nothing to cut. If the answer involves another person changing their behaviour, the rite is aimed at something you cannot operate on. What you can operate on is your own availability, your own attention, your own willingness to keep returning to the situation, and framing the severance in those terms makes it both true and achievable.

The sequence, in order

Write the statement of what is ending and what is being kept. This paragraph goes in the log, not in the ritual, so the record exists whether or not you complete the sequence. Then the cord. Hold it, say the statement, name what is being severed and what is being retained, and make the cut in one movement rather than a sawing one. Let the two ends be separate. Set a fire or a bowl and burn the cord to ash if you are working with fire, or keep the two pieces apart and out of the same room if you are not.

Then the part everyone skips. Sit with it. Do not leave the room immediately, do not message the person, do not check whether you feel different. Twenty minutes of stillness after a severance is doing more work than the sequence, and skipping it is the most common way people end up repeating the rite within a week. The rite creates a change and the stillness is what lets you find out whether the change stayed.

What not to do in the first hour afterwards

No contact with the other person, in any form, including a message that does not mention the rite. No explanation, no announcement, no signal that something happened. This is the practical content of the do-it-twice warning, and it does not require believing in rebonds to justify it, because contacting them immediately restores the exact dynamic the rite was intended to end.

No reopening the log to revise the statement. No second cutting on the same bond within the same week, however certain you become that the first one failed. Feeling certain and being correct are different things, and the hour after a severance is the worst possible time to test which one you are experiencing.

Grief is the expected cost

Severing a bond that has been load-bearing produces something that reads as loss, and it is frequently the sign that the operation worked rather than that it failed. If a relationship has been organising your attention for years, removing it leaves a hole, and the hole is not evidence that you did something wrong. It is the shape of what was removed.

The historical sources that discuss this rite treat grief as part of the procedure rather than as a complication, and some describe a period of mourning following it. Practically, this means expecting a bad few weeks rather than a clean relief, and it means not reading the low period as a reason to reverse the operation. Reversing on the strength of a bad week is how people end up doing it twice.

When the other person will not engage

Most bonds involve someone who has no idea a severance has been performed, and nothing in the material requires their participation. The rite operates on your own side, which is precisely why framing it in terms of your availability is the correct and durable framing. A severance that requires the other party to notice is not a severance. It is a request.

If the relationship continues as if nothing happened, that is not evidence the rite failed. It is evidence that the change was one-sided, which is what you specified. What you are looking for is your own change. Are you contacting them less, thinking about them less, returning to the situation less. Those are the observable effects and they are the only ones available to you.

Testing whether anything actually shifted

Write down three observable behaviours before you begin, then check them at thirty days. Contact frequency, time spent thinking about the situation, and willingness to help if the person asks. Three items chosen in advance is enough, and choosing them after the rite guarantees a story rather than a measurement.

Thirty days is arbitrary and defensible. Grief runs several weeks and the first week is too close to see anything. At thirty, if nothing has shifted, the honest conclusion is that the rite did not operate, which is real information and better than continuing a practice that produces nothing you can detect. Repeating it a second time is legitimate. Repeating it eight times is avoidance.

Closing the practice rather than repeating it

Cord cutting is not a technique to build a repertoire with. The literature describes it as something done properly and once, and the design of the physical rite enforces that, since you have to make a new cord and commit to a new cut. That is a feature, and it is the reason this operation does not accumulate into the sort of practice that people run reflexively.

Close the log, note the date, and let the result stand. If another severance is needed later you will know more than you do now, which is the only argument for doing one. Building a personal practice of bond-cutting turns a rite of closure into a habit of managing attachment, and habit is the opposite of what the rite is for.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is AURORE and where does it come from?

There is no historical record of this name. It appears in recent chaos-magick material and in none of the classical catalogues, the Grimoirs, or any order archive. Any biography attached to it was written afterwards and functions as fiction. The rite it names is real and well documented; the entity is not, and the two facts do not need to be merged.

What is cord cutting and is it historically real?

It is one of the few genuinely initiatory rites in Western esotericism, appearing in the eighteenth-century Bahir ha-Levavot and later in ceremonial material, rather than being reconstructed during the Victorian revival. It uses a physical cord representing the bond, which is cut or burned as a concrete action mapped onto an abstract severance.

Why does the literature warn against doing it twice?

Because a bond severed by force is held to re-form, and because the mechanism is observable without any supernatural assumption. A severance performed in anger leaves the reason for the attachment intact, so the next contact restores the pattern. The rite is a marker, and it records the state you were in when you performed it.

Does the other person need to know?

No, and nothing in the rite requires their participation, which is why framing it as a change in your own availability is the durable version. A severance that depends on the other party noticing is a request rather than a severance. Judge the operation by your own observable changes: less contact, less thinking, less returning to the situation.

Why do I feel worse after doing it properly?

Grief is the expected cost and it frequently indicates the operation worked. If a relationship has been organising your attention for years, removing it leaves a hole, and the hole is the shape of what was removed rather than evidence of damage. Expect a bad few weeks and do not reverse the operation on the strength of them.
