The Secrets of Money in Chaos Magick: An Audit of the Claims

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 10 min read

The money cluster of chaos magick has a genre problem. A ritual is described, a promise is attached to it, and the promise is that something is being withheld from you. That structure is useful to the person selling access and expensive for the person buying it, because a withheld thing cannot be evaluated, compared, or returned. So this is an audit rather than a revelation. The standard claims in circulation get sorted into four groups, and three of the four are ordinary technique wearing a disguise. The fourth is genuinely a secret in the sense that nobody has worked out what it is, which is a different thing entirely.

Why the secrecy framing exists

Withholding is the only part of a money ritual that cannot be commoditised. Drawing a sigil takes five minutes and any generator will do it. Charging a sigil takes twenty minutes and needs nothing. Timing a charge takes an afternoon of reading and then no effort at all. Every one of those steps is available to anyone who decides to publish them, and they have all been published many times.

What has resisted publication is the promise that a particular sequence works reliably on a particular person. That is the asset being protected. The ritual is the packaging, the secret is the product, and the packaging is deliberately designed to look like the product.

The practical cost to a reader is that secrecy makes comparison impossible. You cannot evaluate an offer whose value is defined as the part you were not shown. You also cannot tell whether a specific failure was a bad ritual or a bad fit, because the person who sold it has an explanation ready for every outcome.

Group one: the sigil, which is a specification

Most of the money ritual is a decision that has been compressed into a handle. You start with a sentence, you reduce it to a mark, and the mark is fixed in a way the sentence is not. Words stay negotiable. A mark does not.

This is genuinely useful and it is not magic. The honest name for it is a specification. What it does is remove the daily renegotiation. A practitioner who has drawn a sigil for a named invoice is not deciding each morning whether to chase that invoice, because the decision was made once and the object of attention survives the day.

The claim to strip away is that the mark carries power. The function to keep is the compression. In practice the compression can be achieved by writing the intention on a card, and many people find the card works better because it is legible, which is a real advantage when you need to remember what you were trying to do.

Group two: the charge, which is attention with a deadline attached

The charge is where the money rituals spend the most ceremony and the money magicians in the channel are unusually honest here. The traditional instructions cover emotional intensity, visualisation, breath, posture, and duration, and the traditional material is genuinely silent on numbers. No source says thirty seconds rather than three minutes. That silence is informative: the part that matters is not the quantity but the fact of the undivided interval.

Why the deadline matters more than the emotion is easy to demonstrate. A ritual with a fixed date and a defined outcome produces a person who follows through on the ordinary actions that produce money, which is invoicing, following up, asking for the referral, and keeping the pipeline warm. A ritual with an open-ended date produces someone who feels charged and then behaves identically.

The honest version of the charge is therefore not a mystical state. It is a short interval of undivided attention on a specific object, followed by a date. Everything else is optional and most of it is atmosphere. If your charge changes nothing about what you do on the following Tuesday, the charge did nothing.

Group three: the entity, which is a job description

This is where most of the claimed secrets actually live, and reading the primary material closely dissolves most of the mystique without killing the practice. The goetic entries are short. They list a rank, a shape, a number of legions, a set of granted powers, and a set of explicit refusals. They do not list money.

The money association on most of these spirits is a later cultural layer. A spirit of arts and sciences, read in a commercial culture, becomes a spirit of commerce. A spirit of hidden things becomes a spirit of hidden money. A spirit of usury, which is the actual traditional home of the money question, gets confused with the rest. The result is a pantheon of craftsmen being described as financiers, which is not what the sources say.

Read as specifications rather than as biographies, the entries become more useful, not less. A spirit of hidden things is a spirit of discovery, and discovery is the part of making money that is genuinely hard. A spirit who inspires boldness is a spirit who acts on a decision you have been avoiding. The refusals are the most useful line in any entry, because they tell you what the thing will not do for you, and almost nobody reads that clause.

Group four: the part nobody has worked out

There is one claim in this cluster that survives every deflationary reading available, and it is not the exciting one. Whether an intervention occurs, and if so what it does, is not known. That is not a gap that has been filled by sceptics or by believers. It is an open question that the tradition has never been able to run a good experiment on, because the charge is the point of the practice and the point of the practice cannot be placed in a control group.

The honest position is agnostic on this specific link and firm on the other eight. The intention gets decided before the ritual. Sigilisation compresses it. The charge marks a boundary. The date pre-commits. The entity's intervention is unknown. A door opens or it does not. The deadline applies pressure to an avoided decision. And money arrives through systems that have throughput, which is why the traditional instruction to do the boring pipeline work is correct for reasons that have nothing to do with anything supernatural.

Saying this plainly is more useful than either triumph or dismissal, because it tells you where to spend attention. You can improve your specification, your charge discipline, your dates, and your pipeline. You cannot improve the unknown link, and a practitioner who thinks they can is selling something.

The catalogue of claims, sorted

Group by group, here is what the money material actually contains. In the first group are the sigil as specification, the charge as a marked interval, and the entity as a job description with explicit limits. These three are the whole of the traditional technique, they are all available in public, and they are worth doing.

In the second group are the pre-commitment mechanisms: the review date, the pre-written conclusion, the line item instead of a total, one charge instead of three. These are the most reliably effective parts of the practice by a wide margin, and they are the part almost nobody foregrounds, because they are unglamorous and they look like project management.

In the third group are the unverifiable additions: guaranteed amounts, a demon's involvement, transformation of circumstances, the removal of other people's agency. These cannot be tested from inside the practice, which is exactly why they sell well.

In the fourth group is the one open question, which should be stated as a question and not answered in either direction.

What is worth taking from the genre

Plenty, and none of it requires the secrecy. Take the specification habit: name the amount, the date, and the action, and compress them once. Take the marked interval: fifteen minutes of undivided attention on the specific object, with the date already written. Take the refusal clause from the source, because almost nobody reads it and it is the line that tells you what you should not expect.

Take the line item instead of the total. Ask for a specific figure from a specific person by a specific date rather than sending out a general request for money, and the difference in outcome is larger than any difference between sigil methods, because a total has no addressee and a line item does.

Take one charge instead of three. If the first produced nothing, repeating it does not change the conditions, it only adds two more opportunities to count the result you wanted. The correct response to an unproductive charge is to change one element of the specification, not to add volume.

And take the review date seriously enough to write the conclusion in advance. The line that has to be written before the working is not the one you will want to write afterwards.

Where the field is genuinely misled

The most common distortion is the inflation of the usury layer. The one spirit in this material whose traditional domain is actually money is the spirit of lending and interest, and that association is medieval and scriptural rather than grimoire-based. Modern material tends to spread that money association across the whole pantheon, which flattens entries that are far more specific and therefore more useful.

The second distortion is the numbering. Printed editions of the goetic lists do not agree on the order, and a great deal of popular material cites a number as if it were an identifier. The number is not the identity. The description and the rank are the identity, and a text that tells you to summon number forty-one without telling you what number forty-one does is not giving you a reference.

The third is the list genre itself. Top-ten-grimoire content is almost entirely a function of what a given author found interesting, and the entries are usually described by what is in them rather than what they are for. A grimoire you cannot use is worse than no grimoire, because it consumes the time you would have spent doing something.

What a beginner should actually buy

If the budget is going somewhere, spend it on something that makes the practice reviewable rather than something that makes it sound larger. The things that make it reviewable are a notebook you can keep a dated record in, a source of genuine randomness you cannot predict, and a book that tells you what a practice is for.

Free tools cover most of the mechanical work. A generator produces the mark. A calendar produces the date. Neither of those is where the value is, and paying for either is paying for a five-minute task.

On books, the useful filter is whether the book tells you what a practice is for. A book that describes a system in enough detail that you could follow it is worth more than a book that describes a system in enough detail that you could admire it. The most common disappointment in this field is buying a beautiful volume of evocative prose and discovering it contains no procedure at all.

The expensive failure mode is the accumulation of monthly subscriptions. A practice that costs a small amount once and a large amount repeatedly is a different kind of purchase, and the recurring cost is the part that changes the relationship to the practice. Most people who drift away from a working ritual did not lose interest, they ran out of reasons to open it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are there real secrets to making money with chaos magick?

Not in the sense the genre implies. The traditional material contains a specification, a marked interval of attention, a pre-committed date, and a set of entity descriptions with explicit limits, and all of it has been public for a long time. What has not been established either way is whether any intervention occurs, and that is a genuine open question rather than a hidden technique being withheld from you.

Which part of a money ritual is most likely to actually help?

The pre-commitment mechanisms, by a wide margin. A review date fixed in advance, a conclusion written before the working, a specific line item instead of a general request, and a single charge rather than repeated ones. These are the least impressive parts of any ritual and they are where the reliable effect sits, because they change what you do on the following Tuesday rather than how you feel during the working.

Should I do a money ritual for a specific amount or for abundance in general?

For a specific amount. A general intention has no addressee, no date, and no observable, which makes it impossible to review and therefore impossible to learn from. A specific figure from a specific person by a specific date can be tracked, and tracking it is the only part of the practice that produces information.

Is it worth paying for an expensive grimoire or course?

Only if it gives you a procedure rather than a description. The filter is whether you could follow the method from the text alone, with no further purchase required. If the interesting part is the atmosphere, the book will disappoint you after about forty pages. If the interesting part is what the method is for and what it will not do, a short inexpensive book will serve.

Does calling a spirit for money actually do anything?

Nobody has established that, in either direction. The useful approach is to treat the entry as a job description rather than a biography, which means you work with what it says it does, such as discovery or boldness, and respect what it says it will not do. Reading the refusal clause is the single most skipped step in this material and it is the one that tells you whether the request you are about to make is one this thing has ever been claimed to handle.
