LUX

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 27, 2026 ⬢ 9 min read

LUX is one of the best-documented names in the Goetia, and the reason is not mysterious. It is short, it is phonetically forceful, and the office attached to it is a category rather than a task, which makes it easy to remember and easy to misuse. This article is about the structure of the catalogue itself: what rank, form, office and power are actually telling you when you read an entry, why the format is shaped the way it is, and what it costs to use a framework you have not checked. Four ideas carry the whole argument. Evocation asks for appearance and invocation asks for influence, and the distinction is the one most modern books flatten. The Ars Goetia assigns each of the seventy-two, a rank, a visible form, an office, together with a listed set of powers, and that four-column discipline is what makes it, by common consent the most orderly entity catalogue in Western grimoire literature. Modern chaos practice treats those entities as independent pattern fragments rather than beings. And Carroll held they are aspects of the magician's own mind, which is the position that lets you use all of this without believing any of it.

Where a four-letter name gets its authority

LUX carries authority the way a well-known name in any profession carries authority: through repetition, not through derivation. It has been cited in grimoires for four centuries, printed in lists, illustrated, translated, and used as a brand by applications that have no relationship to the text at all. Every one of those uses adds a little to the sense that the name is substantial.

This is worth naming because it is a general feature of how names acquire power in this field, and it is not fraud. A name that has been used consistently for four hundred years has been shaped by four hundred years of people deciding what it should mean. That is a real process, and it produces something that is not a claim about the world and not a claim about you either. It is a claim about a shared body of usage.

What rank, form, office and power actually tell you

The catalogue format gives four fields per spirit, and each does a different job. Rank tells you where the spirit sits in a hierarchy. Form tells you what it appears as. Office tells you what it is for. Powers tell you what it is supposedly able to do. Three of those are descriptive and one is functional, and the functional one is the only one you can act on.

Read the office field as a job description rather than an identity. A spirit of luxury who grants riches is not a god of wealth; it is a catalogue entry with a stated function, in a document written for a court. Reading the office as a personality is the first step toward inflation, because a personality is something you can have a relationship with and a function is something you can invoke.

The catalogue's grammar and its limits

The Goetia is structured as a ranked administrative list. Spirits are grouped by rank, in the classical division of presidents, dukes and kings, and each entry is terse: a name, a title, a shape, a task, a set of abilities. It reads like a register because that is roughly what it is, assembled from earlier demonological material for a specific textual purpose.

Knowing that changes how you use it. A register answers the question what does this do, and it answers it well. It does not answer what does this mean, who wrote it, or why that particular set of abilities was attached to that name. When a source is structured for lookup, treating it as a source of significance is a category error, and it is the most common way people over-read these entries.

Why the Goetia is still the reference

Later grimoires offer more elaborate personalities, more dramatic scene descriptions, and less clarity. The Goetia remains the reference because the terseness is an advantage in practice. You can hold an office in mind as a single sentence, which is exactly what an invocation needs, and you can compare two entries without translating either of them first.

The cost is that the catalogue says almost nothing about how any of it was obtained or why the descriptions have the form they do. Treat the Goetia as a working vocabulary rather than a source of history, and you will use it well. Treat it as a source of history and you will spend a long time reading around a document that was not trying to provide one.

Carroll's redefinition and what it buys

Peter Carroll held that entities are aspects of the magician's own mind, which reduces the whole question of risk without requiring scepticism. If the entity is a facet of the practitioner, then no external being is being deceived, no relationship is being violated, and the only real question is whether the practice does anything you want it to do.

That is a strong position to have available and it is not a metaphysical claim most people need to sign up for. The useful part is procedural: it lets you work with a full-bodied catalogue entry, complete with rank and dramatic form, without treating yourself as a supplicant. You are borrowing a structure, not reporting to someone. Keeping that framing intact is the single best defence against the version of this practice where you end up apologising to a piece of typography.

Reading an office as a description of a function

The test for whether an office is usable is whether it can generate obligations. Take the entry and rewrite the office as something this spirit would be expected to do, then list what that requires of you. A function that requires nothing of you is a description, and you will invoke it and see nothing happen.

The rewriting also exposes inflation early. If the rewrite produces something you would be embarrassed to explain, you have found the version of the working that will not survive contact with other people, and it is better to find it here than in month three. The office should be describable at a dinner table in one sentence without prompting and without the word chosen.

The pattern-fragment licence, in plain terms

The modern chaos position is that an entity is a fragment of pattern, independent, usable, and not a being with a biography. On this view you may define, invoke, charge and retire any name, including invented ones, and there is no ritual danger in doing so. The failure mode shifts from spiritual to technical: a pattern too vague to be noticed simply does not register, and a pattern too specific to be repeated does not survive contact with a bad week.

In practice this makes the technique much easier to learn and much easier to abandon. You can run ten names in a year without any of them accumulating into a relationship, and that is a real loss as well as a real gain. If the catalogue entries are doing something for you, it may be worth deciding whether you want pattern or presence, because the two pull in different directions and mixing them produces a practice that is neither.

Drafting a working from a borrowed structure

Take the entry's office and narrow it until it fits in a sentence you could complete. Not wealth, but the specific action that produces an outcome you would notice. Not clarity, but the moment of deciding between two things you have already ranked. The narrowing is the whole craft, and it is the step the catalogue cannot do for you because the catalogue was written about categories and you need it applied to one life.

Then write what would count as it having functioned, in terms you could check without a good mood. One line, dated, written before the charge. This is unglamorous, and it separates a working you can evaluate and one you will remember fondly, which are not the same activity and only one of which teaches you anything.

What inflation looks like on paper

Inflation is the standard failure and it has recognisable written forms. Specialness language: this spirit chose me, I was ready, this is rare. Escalation: the office grows a clause each time you revise it, so that by month three the spirit does considerably more than the catalogue says. Dependency: the practice becomes framed as necessary rather than chosen, and a skipped session produces anxiety rather than a note.

Check for these in your own notebook rather than in your memory, because the notebook is where inflation shows up first as grammar. Look for the passive constructions that put you in the role of recipient, and the superlatives that arrive without dates attached. Revising an office downward is normal maintenance. Revising it upward, without a reason you could defend, is the thing to watch for.

A structure you can audit

An auditable structure is one where somebody else, given your notebook, could tell what you were doing and roughly what you got. That requires four things and none of them are difficult: the name as written, the office as a single sentence, the date and count of the charge, and a line on what you tried to observe. It does not require interpretation, which is fortunate, because interpretation is the part nobody else can audit.

Keep the interpretation in a separate column or a separate page. This is a small discipline with an unusually large effect: separating what happened from what it meant means you can change your mind about the meaning without having to pretend the events changed too, which is the main reason practitioners end up defending conclusions they no longer hold.

Using a framework without believing in it

There is a version of this practice where you are a supplicant, another where you are an engineer, and the second is available to anyone. The catalogue gives you a grammar, the grammar gives you an office, the office gives you a sentence, the sentence compresses into a sigil, and the sigil holds a decision in place. Every step is a procedure. None of them requires you to hold a belief about what is on the other end of the procedure.

This is not a denial of the tradition, it is a narrowing of what you are relying on. Someone who finds the framework genuinely inert can still use the compression technique and the commitment mechanism, and they will do most of what the working is for. Someone who finds it alive and treats it as a device will miss the failure mode entirely, because they will attribute every absence of result to their own state rather than to the sentence.

When the framework is the problem

Sometimes the office is too broad to be noticed, and no amount of additional charge fixes it. The signs are consistent: the practice gets more elaborate rather than more specific, you can describe the working in a dozen different ways, and you cannot name a single observable you were looking for. In that state the framework is generating structure without generating a target.

The move is subtraction, not addition. Drop back to the shortest sentence that still means something, and run that for a fixed number of sessions with nothing else. If a one-sentence office produces no observable change over a month, the framework is not the limiting factor and adding tools will not help. What will help is changing what you are trying to do, which is a different project and often the more honest one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does it mean that entities are aspects of your own mind?

That the entity is a facet of the practitioner rather than an independent being, which removes the question of an external party being deceived or angered. Practically it lets you work with elaborate catalogue entries without supplicating, and it relocates every failure into ordinary technique: a vague office, an uncharged sigil, a working you simply did not repeat.

Why is the Ars Goetia still the main reference?

Because its terse format is an operational advantage. Rank, form, office and powers give you an office you can hold as one sentence, which is what an invocation needs, and they are comparable across entries. More elaborate later sources supply dramatic personality and less clarity, which is the wrong trade for a practice that needs a checkable target.

Does using a full catalogue entry mean believing in the spirit?

No. The entry is a structure you are borrowing, and the same procedure works on an invented name. Carroll's position is that an entity is a piece of the magician's own mind, was written to make that borrowing possible. Keeping the framing as a user rather than a supplicant is the main practical protection against the version of this practice where you end up apologising to a piece of typography.

What is inflation and how do I know if it is happening?

Inflation is when the entity becomes something you have a relationship with rather than a function you invoked, and it shows up in writing as specialness claims, an office that gains a clause each revision, and framing the practice as necessary rather than chosen. Check the notebook rather than your memory, because the grammar shifts before you notice the belief has.

How do I stop an office from being too vague to notice?

Rewrite it as a function that generates obligations, then narrow it to a single sentence naming an action you personally take. A useful office requires something of you and can be checked from outside your mood. If you cannot name a single observable you were looking for, the office is too broad and additional charge will not fix it.
