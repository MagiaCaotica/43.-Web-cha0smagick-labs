Phase 1: Calibration Protocol — Baseline Establishment

Every session begins with calibration. Without calibration, you have no baseline to distinguish signal from noise. Calibration takes 3 minutes and establishes your session's discrimination baseline.

The 3-Point Calibration (Mandatory, Every Session)

  1. "Show me YES" → Record: speed, pressure, trajectory, pattern, time to first letter.
  2. "Show me NO" → Record: speed, pressure, trajectory, pattern, time to first letter.
  3. "Show me HELLO" → Record: speed, pressure, trajectory, pattern. This = entity introduction/confirmation.

The Ideomotor Baseline Test

Before communication, establish YOUR ideomotor baseline:

  1. Blindfold yourself (or close eyes).
  2. Ask a question ONLY YOU know the answer to (e.g., "Is my name [Name]?").
  3. Record the movement: speed, pressure, trajectory, pattern.
  4. This = YOUR ideomotor signature. Any movement matching this signature during session = operator ideomotor, not external.

Environmental Baseline (Mandatory)

  1. Place planchette on board. NO HANDS.
  2. Wait 60 seconds. Record ANY movement.
  3. Speed, direction, pattern = environmental baseline (drafts, vibration, board warp, uneven surface).
  4. Any movement during session matching this baseline = environmental artifact.

Real-Time Discrimination Protocol

During session, classify each significant movement:

  1. Observe: Speed, pressure, trajectory shift.
  2. Compare to Calibration: Does it match YES/NO calibration? Ideomotor baseline? Environmental baseline?
  3. Classify: Entity / Ideomotor / Environmental / Interference / Multiple.
  4. Log: Timestamp, classification, confidence (1-10).

The Information Test (Gold Standard)

The only way to distinguish external entity from sophisticated ideomotor:

  1. Ask questions with answers UNKNOWN to operator but VERIFIABLE.
  2. Examples: "What is the object in my pocket?" "What did I eat for breakfast 3 days ago?" "What is the license plate of the car outside?"
  3. If planchette answers correctly consistently → external source probable.
  4. If answers match operator's guesses or are wrong → ideomotor.
INFORMATION TEST PROTOCOL: ========================== PRE-SESSION: Write 5 verifiable questions only entity could know. DURING SESSION: Ask each. Record answer. POST-SESSION: Verify each. ACCURACY RATE: Correct / Total = Entity Probability. THRESHOLD: >70% accuracy on unknown verifiables = External probable. <70% = Ideomotor probable.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I distinguish between ideomotor effect and genuine communication?

Calibration is key. 1) Establish YES/NO baseline. 2) Ask unknown verifiable questions (only entity could know). 3) Compare movement quality: ideomotor = smooth, predictable, follows expectation. Genuine = unexpected speed/pressure/pattern, information not in operator's knowledge. 4) PSI GYM calibration: clean channel = reliable discrimination.

What do different planchette speeds mean?

Slow/hesitant = resistance, deliberation, weak connection, or entity uncertainty. Medium/smooth = clear communication, strong link. Fast/jerky = high energy, urgency, strong emotion, or interference. Circling = testing connection, confusion, or energy buildup.

How do I calibrate my planchette sensitivity?

1) 3-point calibration (YES/NO/HELLO) every session. 2) Blind test with known answers. 3) Environmental baseline (no hands, 60s). 3) PSI GYM weekly (>28% hit rate = clean channel). 4) Log every session: speed, pressure, pattern, accuracy. Calibration = your discrimination baseline.

Is the ideomotor effect the only explanation for planchette movement?

Ideomotor effect explains unconscious muscular movement. But it doesn't explain: 1) Information unknown to all participants. 2) Movement patterns exceeding human micro-muscular capability. 3) Consistent entity personalities across sessions/operators. 4) PSI GYM correlation: clean channel = higher accuracy. The board is an interface; what drives it is the question.
