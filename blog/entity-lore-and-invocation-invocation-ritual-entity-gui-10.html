Entity Lore and Invocation

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 27, 2026 ⬢ 9 min read

There is one aim in this whole domain that instruments can check, and it is a frequency. A pitch has a number attached to it, a tuner will say whether the note is there, and an application on your phone will hold the reading while you sit with it. That changes what kind of working this is. Almost every other aim in entity work is reported: someone felt something, someone saw something, someone noticed a coincidence. Reported experience is precisely the material a determined practitioner can talk themselves into. A frequency arrives already attached to a claim, that it is a tuning the world was meant to hold, and that claim is recent, modern, and unattested anywhere in the older material. So the working is worth doing, not for the reason usually given. The mechanism that does anything is the one a servitor has always used: a conditional wired to a cue. Play the note, then do the thing. The pitch is a door handle. Everything you would have done anyway now happens because a sound made you begin, and the entity apparatus contributes a vocabulary and a filing system that let you write the conditional down in a shape you can audit a month later. What follows is the sheet I use, which is mostly a definition table and a record template.

A frequency you can check with an instrument

Start with the readout, because it changes what counts as a result. A phone application that reports a frequency reading has a noise floor. It will show a number when the room is silent and the phone is lying on a table, and that number is not a signal. Before you do anything else, spend ten minutes recording what the app claims when you are not playing anything. Write the figures down. That baseline is the only thing that will let you tell a real reading from a plausible one, and it costs you a coffee's worth of time.

The cheap tuner is a slightly better instrument and behaves the same way. It is accurate enough to tell you that a note is roughly right and not accurate enough to tell you anything about intention. That is fine. The point of the instrument is not to detect magic. The point is to separate the claim you can check from the claim you cannot, so that the rest of the practice can be arranged around the part where evidence is possible.

Where the sacred-pitch claim comes from

A specific number offered as a correction to the modern world is a twentieth and twenty-first century framing. The older ceremonial literature assigns pitches to planets and to vowels in a system that was never expressed in hertz, because the measurement did not exist when the tables were compiled. When the idea is restated as a number, it is being translated, and the translation adds an authority the original table did not claim.

None of that makes the number useless. It makes it borrowed. If you borrow it, mark it as borrowed in the same place you record everything else, and do not build a charge on the assumption that the universe was tuned. What survives the demotion is small and usable: a sound, played at a particular moment, is a good cue, because a cue that is not a thought is very hard to argue with when you try to skip the action.

The four fields, and which one is doing the work

The catalogue format gives you four slots: a rank, a visible form, an office, and a set of powers. For a working built on a frequency, three of those are decoration. A rank is a claim about status and status has no measurable consequence. A visible form is a drawing, and the drawing is not what you are playing. A list of powers is the field most likely to be copy-pasted from another entry and therefore the field most likely to be wrong.

The office is the only slot that does anything. An office is a description of a function, and a function can be stated so that you will know whether you did it. I write mine in the form a technician would accept: a condition, an action, and a number of repetitions. Nothing in that sentence requires a spirit, and everything in it can be checked on a Sunday.

A trigger is a conditional, not a spirit

This is the whole of it, and it is worth saying plainly. A servitor is a conditional with a cue attached: when this sound plays, I do this thing. That is a mechanism. It has no hidden component. The apparent hidden component arrives later, when the cue fires and you cannot be certain whether the sound caused the action or the action made you reach for the sound, and you resolve that uncertainty by attributing it to something you built deliberately.

The consequence for practice is that you can strengthen the conditional without touching the metaphysics. Keep the cue short. Make it a single note rather than a track. Attach it to one action only. Repeat it enough times that it stops being a decision. A conditional with three actions attached to it is a wish, and a wish does not fire.

The office, written as something a tuner would accept

Here is a shape that works. On the night, for ten minutes, I hold the tuning fork at the agreed number and I do the one administrative task I have been avoiding. The office, in catalogue language, is the warding of a threshold against procrastination, which sounds grander than it is. In working language it is: on this note, I open the file I closed yesterday and add four lines.

The reason for the tuner rather than the app is not accuracy. It is that a physical object you strike has no settings, no recommendations, no playlist logic, and no way of being updated without your consent. It is an instrument that does one thing, which is the only kind of instrument a conditional should hang from.

What the pitch can and cannot do

It cannot carry intention. A frequency has no opinion about what you want and no way of finding out. If you believe the note is doing something to the world, that belief is in you, not in the air, and it will operate identically if you replace the note with a doorbell.

It can do three things that are not mystical. It can mark a boundary in time, so the working starts and stops at a signal rather than at your willingness. It can occupy a slot you would otherwise fill with something worse, which is most evenings. And it can survive the loss of motivation, which motivation cannot. If the note is what starts the ten minutes, then on the evening when you do not want to do anything, the note is what starts it.

Building the working in one evening

Four steps, in this order, and the order is the working. First, write the office as a conditional with one action. Second, choose the cue and commit to one physical object. Third, set the repetitions to a number you have never once failed to reach in a sitting, which for most people is four and not twenty. Fourth, write the record page before you charge anything, and date it.

The fourth step is the one that makes the rest honest. A page with today's date, a blank column for the reading, a column for whether the four lines got written, and a third column for what you would have done anyway, is a page that cannot flatter you. Write it first, while you are still capable of imagining a disappointing result.

The sheet, and why the sheet is the working

The sheet has five rows and it is a definitions table, not a plan. Row one: the cue, defined as the object you will strike. Row two: the office, defined as the conditional. Row three: the repetitions. Row four: the date the working began, because a working with no start date has no end date. Row five: the condition under which you will retire it.

Rows four and five are the ones people skip and they are the ones that matter. A servitor with no retirement condition does not retire, it accumulates, and each active conditional takes a small cut of attention that you will not notice until you count. Counting is the whole of the maintenance.

The first week, with the readout open

Seven nights, four repetitions, and the app recording the whole time. The interesting column is not the frequency. It is the column marked whether the four lines got written, because that is the only thing the practice can actually do. On my own record, the first two evenings were the hardest and the fifth was automatic, which is the ordinary shape of any conditional and requires no explanation.

Write the third column honestly. On several of those nights I would have opened the file anyway, because the deadline was close, and saying so costs the practice nothing. The nights where I would not have opened it are the ones the note was doing something on. That is a small number and it is a real one, and it is a larger number than the baseline of doing nothing at all.

What a change in the readout would mean

Almost nothing, and this is worth being blunt about. A shift in a phone reading between two sessions is within the range you would get from battery level, phone orientation, a nearby appliance, or the temperature of the room. It is a change in a number produced by a device that has no access to your intention.

The number is worth recording anyway, for one reason: it gives you a column that is independent of your mood. When you are having a strange fortnight and the reading has not moved, you have one piece of evidence that the instrument is not responding either. That is worth more than a warm feeling, and it costs nothing to keep.

Listening without hunting for confirmation

The failure mode here is not superstition, it is tuning. You will hear a harmonic that was not there last week and you will want it to mean something, because a harmonic is a small gift and gifts are pleasant. The way out is to decide in advance what would count as a result, write it down, and refuse to renegotiate when the gift arrives.

Decide it like this. The working succeeded if the office was performed on the stated number of nights. That is the criterion, it is checkable, and it was written before the first night. If the practice is only worth continuing when something unusual also happens, you have not built a conditional. You have built a superstition with a tuning fork attached to it.

The row worth keeping

If you keep one row, keep the office, and keep it in the imperative. Not a request, not an aspiration, not a statement about the kind of person you are becoming. An instruction, with a verb, an object, and a count. The imperative is what makes the glyph legible later and what makes the record falsifiable, and it is also the only part of the entity apparatus that any of this needs.

Everything else on the sheet is scaffolding for a habit you could have built with a calendar reminder and a phrase written on a card. That is not a criticism of the tradition. It is an accurate description of where the load sits, and knowing where the load sits is what lets you carry the rest of it lightly, which is how it was meant to be carried.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 432 hz a meaningful frequency in any historical magical text?

No. Ceremonial tables from earlier centuries assign musical notes to planets and vowels, but they are not expressed in hertz because the unit did not exist when those tables were written. Restating an old assignment as a modern number is a translation, and the translation lends the number an authority the original table never claimed. You can still use the number. Just record that you borrowed it rather than inherited it.

Do I need to believe in the spirit for the working to function?

The part that functions is a conditional attached to a cue, and a conditional does not evaluate your belief. What belief does is supply explanations for the evenings when the cue fired and you could not tell whether the sound started the action or the action made you reach for the sound. You can keep the explanation or drop it. The part worth protecting is the repetition count, because that is the part that survives a bad week.

What should I write in the record?

Four things per night: the frequency reading if you took one, whether the office was performed, how many repetitions you completed, and what you would have done anyway without the cue. That last column is the one that stops the practice from becoming a story about yourself. Write the page on the first evening, dated, before you have any results at all.

Can I attach several tasks to the same cue?

Not without weakening it. A cue with one action attached is a conditional, and a conditional fires reliably. A cue with three actions attached is a wish, and a wish does not fire. If you want more than one task on the same signal, use two different signals. The number of cues you can hold is small, and finding out what it is costs you a few weeks.

When should I retire this working?

Write the condition down when you build it, before the first night, when you are still able to imagine being disappointed. A common condition is that the task is finished, or that the count of active cues has reached whatever limit you set. A working with no retirement date does not retire quietly. It takes a small cut of attention forever, and you will only notice when you count.
