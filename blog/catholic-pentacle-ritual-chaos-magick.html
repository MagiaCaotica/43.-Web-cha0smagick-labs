A Pentacle Working Inside a Catholic Frame

By Frater Alek0s | October 5, 2026 | 11 min read

This is a working, not a theory. It is written out so you can do it, and it is written out with its constraints attached, because a ritual stripped of its constraints is just a sequence of gestures.

It assumes three things: that you have a private space you can use without interruption, that you have done Carroll's Liber O exercises enough times to hold a state deliberately, and that you are honest with yourself about which parts of the frame you have authority to speak from.

Where this working sits in the library

The pentacle, and what it is not

The pentacle is a five-pointed figure used across a wide range of European material. It is not a Catholic sacramental symbol. It appears in folk practice, in some architecture, and extensively in the esoteric literature of the last few centuries. Using it does not make a working Catholic, and it is better to say so than to let the shape do persuading.

What the pentacle does supply is a bounded five-part structure: a defined interior, four cardinal directions plus a centre, and a way of placing five different materials or five different acts at fixed points. That is a useful scaffold for a small operation.

What the frame is for

Every serious rite has a frame, and the frame is not ornament. The Roman Rite begins with a preparatory blessing because the work is not to be entered casually. Carroll's practice begins with a circle because the operation is not to be performed from an ordinary mental state. Both are doing the same thing: establishing a container so that attention is not spent on boundary management while you are trying to hold an intention.

The working

Preparation, before anything else

Declare what you are doing, in writing, in plain language, before you begin. Not the sigil form — the plain statement. "I am doing this to make a specific request of a specific person." This is the single most important step and the one most often skipped, because a vague intention produces a vague result and the temptation afterwards is to reinterpret it as a success.

Marking the boundary

Walk the space. Name it aloud as a bounded place for a single stated purpose. Then draw the boundary at the threshold. If you are working inside a Catholic frame, take the form from the Ephpheta: sign the cross at the entrance, bless the space with water, and state the intention. If you are not, use the circle and state it again. Do not do both, and do not mix them.

Holding the state

Sit at the centre. Hold a light before the closed eyes until it is steady, then a darkness, then the light again. This is Carroll's exercise and Spare's, and it is the part that does the actual training. Ten minutes is enough. Anything less and the operation is running on mood rather than intention.

Building the sigil

Write the plain statement in capitals. Reduce it by removing consecutive pairs of repeated characters. Reduce each remaining character to a single stroke. Draw it. Write the full statement, the reduction steps, the date and the material on the reverse, and sign the reverse with something you use for that purpose only.

Committing

This is where the chaotic element belongs, and only here. Fix the sigil to the material you chose at the start, and act as if the statement is already true. Not as a metaphor — as the instruction the method gives. Carroll is explicit that this is a performance of conviction, and that the practitioner's work is to mean it rather than to hope.

Then close the boundary the way you opened it, in reverse order.

Put this into practice

The ethics are part of the method

Two constraints, both from the Catholic frame and both worth keeping. First: do not work on another person's mind without their participation. The Ephpheta is a request for protection, made about a place, addressed outward. Second: do not use this for harm, and notice that you will be able to construct a rationalisation for almost any target if you start looking.

Record before you interpret

Write down what you did and what you expected, and seal it or put it away before you check whether it worked. Interpretation done after the fact is where every method in this space convinces itself of things.

Do it again in thirty days

Not the same operation. A different one, same structure. What you are actually building with practice like this is not an effect on the world — it is the ability to hold an intention long enough to act on it, which is the thing Carroll said was the point, and it is the only part that reliably transfers.
