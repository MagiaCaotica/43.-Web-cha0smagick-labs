Entity Lore and Invocation

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 8 min read

The entity working literature is large, confident and internally contradictory, and almost all of its disagreement sits underneath three questions nobody in the genre ever settles. This is an attempt to name those three, say which parts of the practice survive once you have, and stop paying for the parts that do not.

What the Genre Claims

The standard pitch runs like this. Named spirits exist as coherent patterns. Each has an office, a form and a domain. You can call one, it will operate through you, and the results are better than what you would manage alone. Ritual apparatus improves the contact. A sigil focuses the intention. The whole thing is a technology with an interface.

Written well, this is persuasive. Written the way most of it is written, it is a set of assertions in the grammar of instructions, where a claim about a pattern is phrased as a step in a procedure and the reader has no way to tell which is which.

That confusion is the single most useful thing to notice about the genre. Once you can label a sentence as either an operational instruction or an unexamined claim, the material becomes much easier to use, because you stop treating opinions as method.

Three Questions Nobody in the Genre Settles

First: are the entities external, internal, or neither? The classical grimoires imply external. Chaos magic largely treats them as aspects of the practitioner. Ritual magic tends to hold both positions simultaneously and decline to choose, which produces a great deal of muddled advice about risk.

Second: does the entity act on the world, or does the operator act differently while believing the entity is present? Both are compatible with everything anyone has ever reported, and the genre has no instrument that could tell them apart.

Third: does apparatus change the outcome? Everybody says yes and nobody measures it, because a measurement would require a baseline and a control, and a control group in this material means deliberately doing less than you believe in. The honest answer is unknown, and the genre fills the gap with confident assertion.

Where the Sources Disagree With Each Other

The most structured catalogue in Western grimoire literature is the Ars Goetia, with seventy-two entries across nine Presidents, thirty-six Dukes, along with thirty Kings, each with a rank, a form, an office and named powers. It is structured, dated and citable, which makes it the most useful single document in the genre.

It is also a document about offices rather than about monsters. Underneath it sits a stratum of Jewish and Christian demonological writing, the Pseudepigrapha Book of Enoch among it, which reaches the grimoire lineage in the seventeenth century by way of the Conjuratio Ben-Maimon and the Lemuria. Read that way, the seventy-two are administrative and disciplinary figures, which is a very different set of relationships to the ones most modern retelling describes.

Where the genre is weakest is everything after that. The later accretions disagree with the source text, disagree with each other, and are frequently presented with the same confidence as the dated material, so a reader cannot tell which layer they are looking at.

What Carroll's Position Buys You

Carroll's position was that entities in chaos magic are best handled as fragments of the magician's own mind, not as external beings. The position is usually dismissed as a cowardly hedge, and that reading misses its practical value.

Treated as an aspect, the working becomes an exercise in directed attention: you are writing an instruction for a mode of your own cognition you do not normally have access to, and then performing it under conditions that make it stick. That account explains the most commonly reported experience in this material, which is that the results feel like a competence arriving from somewhere rather than an effort succeeding.

It also neutralises most of the risk without requiring anybody to be a sceptic about the whole practice. Nothing in the internal reading asks you to believe there is nothing out there. It only asks you not to need there to be.

Where the Genre Overstates

It overstates provenance. A great deal of what circulates as grimoire material is nineteenth-century invention, twentieth-century reconstruction, or forum consensus, and the genre treats all three as though they were the same kind of document. Provenance matters enormously in a system built on offices, because the office is the part that has to be right.

It overstates precision. Offices are stated in single sentences and then expanded by later writers into paragraphs of invented capability. The expansion reads as detail and is actually erosion.

It overstates the importance of apparatus. Circles, robes, names of power and multi-hour structures are described as necessary and are, on the evidence anyone has actually gathered, indistinguishable from doing the same short operation with fewer things to forget. The genre confuses ceremony with effect because ceremony is more publishable.

A Template That Survives the Critique

This survives every objection in the previous sections. It does not require you to hold any view about what entities are, it is identical whether the entity is external or internal, and it produces a falsifiable session every time. Line two is what defeats the genre's habit of inventing capability, because a citation can be checked. Line four is what makes the session capable of failing.

  1. First the name, transcribed the way you mean to voice it.
  2. The office in one sentence, from a source you have read, with the citation written underneath.
  3. The request, first person, present tense, phrased as a request.
  4. Fourth, the point at which you would count it settled, written as a date or a rule.

Testing Without Needing the Genre to Be True

You do not have to resolve any of the three questions to run a fair test. Write down one specific, checkable event before you start: a message that arrives, a decision that finally gets made, a detail you were missing that shows up. Leave it on the sheet. Come back and read the sheet rather than the memory.

Run three attempts with the office line corrected and the timeframe stated, shifting a single element per attempt so your notes have something to compare against. Three honest failures with a correct office line tell you the request is wrong, which is the most useful thing the exercise can produce and considerably more valuable than a single ambiguous success.

Feeling moved does not count. It is the most common outcome in the genre and the least informative, because it is available without any working at all, and it is the thing a confirmation-prone reader will most want to accept.

The Apparatus Question, Answered Honestly

The genre's answer is that more structure produces stronger contact. The measurable answer is that we do not know, because no one has run the comparison, and the reason is structural rather than accidental: a control condition requires a session where you deliberately withhold something you believe in, which most people cannot bring themselves to do on a question they care about.

What can be said is narrower and still useful. Structure reduces the number of decisions, and fewer decisions means more of your attention stays on the operation rather than on the setup. The effect is real, and it is far smaller than what the genre claims.

So the practical guidance is to build the smallest version that keeps the four lines and the written check, and to treat every addition as a choice to justify rather than a step to perform.

Record Keeping Is the Actual Practice

Strip away the parts of this material that cannot be evaluated and what remains is a log. Six fields, in this order: date, hour, the office you cited, the request in brief, the deadline, and the observation. The last column is the only one that carries information and the only one anybody avoids filling in.

This is unglamorous, and it is the entire durable discipline. A year of those lines will show you which methods actually produce results for you, which is a question no amount of reading can answer, because the answer is specific to your own attention and your own circumstances.

It also settles the argument in the third section without you having to settle it yourself. You do not need to know whether apparatus matters; you need a record that would show you if it mattered for you. That is the entire difference between a practice and a preference.

What to Keep and What to Drop

Keep the office line and the citation. Keep the written check and the stated timeframe. Keep the boundary, because it makes the hour distinguishable from other hours at the same table. Keep the log, and keep the failures in it.

Drop the borrowed reputation. Any name whose atmosphere comes from a video essay rather than a source is a name you have not researched, and that is the one genuinely avoidable hazard the genre has.

Drop the apparatus that exists for other people's benefit. And drop any framing that makes you more likely to need the result to be true next month, since a practice whose continuity depends on continued belief is not a practice you can evaluate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much set-up does one of these actually require?

No. The reduction in apparatus is the part of this material that survived contact with scrutiny. A defined intention, a sigil or a name, a stated frame with a citation, and a written end are enough to run something you can evaluate. Everything beyond that reduces the number of available decisions slightly and adds material to maintain, which is not the same thing as adding effect.

Should I work with demons at all?

No documented harm sits behind the page, and the hazard everybody skips is the only real one: opening a name whose source you never looked at. Read the stated office, confirm it covers the domain you need, and keep a written check. Under those three conditions this is bookkeeping with an unusual vocabulary, and the drama belongs to the retelling rather than to the procedure.

Does calling a spirit into view differ from asking it to work through you?

The older manuals treat those as two different jobs. One wants something brought into view, and it wants a cleared space, outward proof that it arrived, and a departure practised beforehand. The other wants the influence borrowed for a short stretch of work, and it wants an empty table. The two fail differently, so advice that swaps the words freely will give you contradictory instructions about both the effort and the risk.

Is it dishonest to treat an entity as an aspect of yourself?

Not if you keep the working honest in the part that can be measured. The internal reading asks you to stop requiring an external agent, which changes what you believe and not what you do. What would be dishonest is dropping the written check along with the belief, because then no outcome can ever count against the practice and you have built something unfalsifiable.

What is the most common mistake in this whole area?

Reading about a figure instead of citing one. An hour of biography leaves you with impressions, and impressions turn into a request that is really a wish. Name the source under the office line, even when the source is thin, because writing it down is what makes the thinness visible and keeps you from building on material you have not actually looked at.
