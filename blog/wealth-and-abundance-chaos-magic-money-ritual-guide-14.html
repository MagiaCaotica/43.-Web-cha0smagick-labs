A vocabulary sheet, and what it is for

Most money practices fail in the same place, which is that nobody can say what was supposed to happen. The aim is abundance, which cannot fail, the glyph is a shape, and the result is a feeling. Three months later nobody can say whether anything occurred.

A vocabulary sheet is five words with a definition, a commitment and an error attached. Five is enough. More produces a glossary nobody reads and a vocabulary nobody is accountable to. The words below are the ones that carry a practice, and each has one specific way of being misused.

Aim, and the imperative that makes it checkable

The aim is the sentence the glyph encodes, and it has to be an imperative. Not a hope, not a description of a desirable state, not a noun. A command with an agent, a verb, an object and a time.

The error this word invites is vagueness. I am abundant, more clients, financial stability, and things flow are all things people write, and none of them can fail, which means none of them can teach you anything. Test an aim by asking what you would count on a Tuesday. If nothing answers, it is a mood with a symbol attached.

Glyph, and what the letter reduction is doing

The glyph is the aim reduced to its letters, those letters reduced to a shape, and the shape reduced to a mark. It is a compression, and compression is what it is for: a short object that can carry a long sentence.

The error is treating the reduction as the work. Twenty minutes of careful letter-shaping is not more magical than a generator, and it is not less. What the twenty minutes is for is that they are the twenty minutes in which the sentence stays in your head, and a glyph built without that attention is a mark with nothing behind it.

Charge, and the hour that is not optional

The charge is the part where the glyph stops being a drawing and becomes a commitment. It is short, it is physical, and it is the step that the books call the important one. In practice it is ten minutes of looking at the mark while something is repeated in your head.

The error is skipping the hour afterwards, not the charge itself. The charge is ten minutes. What changes the practice is the fortnight in which you either act on the sentence or do not. A charge followed by a busy week is a charge that did not happen, regardless of how it felt.

Ledger, and the column that keeps you honest

The ledger is a record of money, opened on the day of the first charge, with three columns: what came in, what went out, and what you would have done regardless. The third column is the whole instrument.

The error is a two-column ledger, which is a mood in a spreadsheet. Without the third column you can attribute any month to the practice, because every month contains something you did. The third column forces you to subtract the things that would have happened anyway, and what is left is usually smaller than you expected.

Abundance, the word too wide to fail

Abundance is the most popular aim in the literature and one of the worst, because nothing in the world can contradict it. A good month is abundance. A bad month is abundance on its way. Any outcome is evidence for abundance, which means the practice cannot fail and cannot teach.

The fix is not to abandon the word but to subordinate it. Keep abundance as the reason and put a countable sentence underneath it one particular amount by one particular date out of one specific activity. The wide word is fine as a motivation and fatal as a measurement.

Twelve, and the borrowing marked as borrowing

The number that shows up constantly in abundance work is the one whose digits add to twelve, and twelve keeps appearing in temperament charts and number symbolism. What the practice does is borrow the figure and mark the borrowing as such.

The error is to let the borrowing become a mechanism. It is arithmetic and association, not a physical property of money. Marked honestly, it is a piece of numerological framing you happen to like. Unmarked, it becomes a causal claim about a number, and it will not survive a single conversation with anyone who has counted.

The three mistakes each term invites

Aim invites vagueness. Glyph invites mistaking the drawing for the work. Charge invites skipping the fortnight. Ledger invites two columns. Abundance invites unfalsifiability. Twelve invites a mechanism that is not there.

Six words, six errors, and every one of them is the obvious failure. That is not a criticism of the vocabulary. It is a property of any short list of practical terms: each one is a doorway and a doorway does not keep you out. Writing the error next to the word is the cheapest safety in the practice.

A first working built only from the sheet

One aim, countable. One glyph, twenty minutes. One charge, ten minutes. One ledger, three columns, opened that evening. One fortnight of follow-through, with three dated entries in the third column. No correspondence tables, no planetary hours, no extra apparatus.

This is deliberately bare. A first working built this way will be small and the numbers will be modest, and both of those are correct. What it produces is a record that a second person can read, which is the thing that makes a practice auditable rather than merely intense.

Reading the ledger against the terms

At the end of a month, read the ledger against the sheet. Did the aim pass the Tuesday test? Was there a fortnight, and did it contain dated entries? Is the third column full, or did you leave it empty because filling it was uncomfortable?

Those three questions decide whether the practice is running. They do not tell you whether the sigil did anything, and they are the only ones you can answer honestly. A month that passes them is a month that worked, whatever the balance did, because the practice is the one thing in the sequence that is fully under your control.

What the numbers will not decide

A good month does not confirm the practice and a bad month does not refute it. One month is a sample of one, and money has causes that have nothing to do with symbols. Anyone who tells you a balance is evidence is doing arithmetic in the wrong direction.

The numbers can do one thing reliably, which is keep you honest about the scale of the effect. If you are doing the third column properly, the honest scale is usually a small percentage of a small amount, and knowing that in advance is worth more than any framework that promises a mechanism.

Six words, and the one sentence underneath them

Six words, six errors, and one sentence I keep at the bottom: the aim has to be countable, the charge has to be followed by a fortnight, and the ledger has to have the uncomfortable column. Everything else in an abundance practice is decoration around those three requirements.

It is an unsatisfying sheet for a practice that promises abundance. It is also the version that produces a record, and a record is the only thing that will tell you whether you want to do this again with the same aim or a smaller one.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should an abundance aim actually say?

An imperative with an agent, a verb, an object and a time, and it has to survive the Tuesday test, which means you can name what you would count. Not I am abundant and not more clients: one particular amount by one particular date out of one particular activity. Abundance is fine as the reason and useless as the measurement.

Do I need to draw the sigil by hand or can a generator do it?

Either is defensible, and the twenty minutes of drawing is not more magical than the generator. What the drawing is for is keeping the sentence in your head while you work on it, because that is the part that makes the mark mean something to you. A generated mark with a written sentence beside it does the same job.

Why does the ledger need a third column?

Because a two-column ledger attributes everything to the practice. Every month contains something you did, so any month can be read as a result. The third column records what you would have done regardless, and subtracting it is the only way of finding out what the working actually contributed. It is uncomfortable to fill in, which is why it works.

What does the number twelve actually do in an abundance practice?

It is a borrowed figure whose digits add to twelve, a number that recurs in temperament charts and number symbolism. Marked honestly, that is numerological framing you happen to like. Unmarked, it becomes a claim that a number has a physical effect on money, and that claim does not survive contact with anyone who counts.

Does a good month prove the practice worked?

No, and neither does a bad month disprove it. One month is a sample of one, and money moves for reasons that owe nothing to symbols. What the month can tell you is whether the practice itself ran, which is the aim passing the Tuesday test, a real fortnight, and a third column that is actually filled in.
