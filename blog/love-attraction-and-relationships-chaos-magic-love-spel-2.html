Love, Attraction and Relationships: Where the Ethics Sit

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 7 min read

Every tradition that has ever written about love magic has eventually written about consent, and most of them did not enjoy the argument. The objection is not imported from outside. It comes from the position that the practitioner's will is the operative ingredient, and if your will is the ingredient then targeting a specific person means overriding a preference that person has already expressed. That is the core problem, and it survives every metaphysical dispute. This article works through the usual three categories, states which of them has a defensible mechanism, and is unsparing about the one that does not.

The Consent Objection in Its Own Terms

A sigil aimed at a specific person operates on the assumption that your will is the ingredient that does the work. If that assumption holds, the operation does not consult the target's preferences, because preferences are not part of the mechanism.

That is the argument in its strongest form, and it is internal to the tradition rather than an external imposition. It also has a consequence most practitioners avoid stating: the more effective the framing, the more clearly the operation is an override. A technique that reliably makes an uninterested person interested has not escaped the problem by being subtle.

The practical test is short. If the only way to score the operation is a change in another adult's behaviour that they have declined to commit to, the operation is the problem. Not the method, not the intent, the operation.

Attraction Magic Produces Rumination

Love sigils reliably produce obsessive thinking. That is the honest finding, and it is not the same as producing attraction. The mechanism of the ritual is attention committed to an object, and the object in the target-aimed version is a specific person, so the ritual trains your attention onto that person.

The result is usually unpleasant. Fixation makes every interaction legible as evidence, which damages relationships you already have and makes new ones almost impossible. Most people who do this once recognise the result quickly and most people do it more than once before recognising it at all.

The version with a mechanism you can defend is aimed at reciprocity, and reciprocity is a target you own. Your availability, your communication, your warmth, your sleep, your presentation, your willingness to be known. A sigil that encodes becoming someone easier to be around has a real behavioural pathway and no consent question, because you are the one being changed.

Reconciliation Is the Milder Case

Reconciliation magic restores contact with someone you previously had a relationship with, and it is generally treated as lower-risk than creating attraction where none existed. The reason is that a bond already existed, so the work is restoring a prior state rather than installing a new one.

That distinction holds but it should not be overread. A prior bond is not current consent, and a person who ended a relationship has usually done so for reasons. Restoring contact is an influence operation and it is still one, and the practical risk is that the contact produces a conversation you were avoiding rather than the resolution you were hoping for.

The framing that makes it more defensible is the one that accepts the other adult's right to say no again. If the operation's success condition is a reply rather than a reconciliation, you have built something considerably more modest, and it is a construction most people can live with.

Cascading

Occult sources warn that repeated love magic against the same target escalates. The metaphysics are arguable, and the behavioural description is not: the pattern of a person repeatedly trying to influence a specific ex or crush, against repeated refusal, is well documented outside this field entirely.

Take the description seriously and drop the metaphysics and you have something usable. Escalation shows up as more frequent operations, more elaborate framings, and a progressive narrowing of the person's other relationships and interests so that the target becomes the whole field of view.

The practical intervention is external. Set a written limit on the number of operations, tell one person who is not involved, and use a review date. A rule you set while calm is worth far more than a decision you will need to make at three in the morning.

What to Work On Instead

This is not a consolation prize. These are the actual mechanisms behind most relationships people are happy about, and they are the inputs you control without overriding anybody.

The last item is the one most people skip. Wanting a specific person is a way of postponing the question of whether you want the arrangement at all, and the sigil lets you postpone it indefinitely because each attempt feels like forward motion.

  1. Your availability. Whether you are contactable at all, on what terms, and for whom.
  2. Your communication. Whether you actually answer, and whether you say what you mean the first time.
  3. Your presentation. Sleep, grooming, clothes, the parts that other people read before you speak.
  4. Your willingness to be known. Reputation, history, and the things you have been avoiding being asked about.
  5. Your own decision about whether you want a relationship at all, rather than a particular person.

Where the Line Sits

Most ethical frameworks, including most occult ones, place the line at consent: a person has the right to choose, and an operation that removes the ability to choose is an override regardless of how you characterise your intentions.

Your own behaviour is the safe zone. Your availability, your boundaries, your schedule, your output, your honesty. Those have a mechanism you can describe and nobody else's autonomy is involved.

Working on a person who has not asked you to is outside it, and the fact that the operation is a file you made on your own laptop does not change that. Medium is not ethics, and the most common argument in this area, that the method being impersonal makes the target unaccountable, is an argument about the tool rather than about the act.

Reading the Room Before You Act

Every tradition that has written about relationship work includes a version of the same warning: look at what is actually in front of you before deciding what to do about it. The version that skips this is a version performing a ritual instead of solving a problem.

In practice this means checking whether the difficulty is yours or theirs, whether anything has been said out loud, and whether the person involved is an adult with their own account of the situation. The answer is frequently that the target of the working is the wrong target.

What the Framework Refuses to Do

It will not help you win someone who does not want you, and it will not disguise that refusal as a technical limitation. The available moves are all on your own side of the line, and the set is smaller than the marketing suggests.

That smaller set is the honest one, and it is also the one that produces durable results. Operations on your own availability, communication and willingness to be known compound slowly and keep working after the ritual ends, which is the only kind of result in this area that does not require somebody else to be uninformed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a reconciliation sigil?

It is a sigil aimed at restoring contact with somebody you previously had a relationship with. It is still an influence operation, but it is generally treated as lower-risk than creating attraction where none existed, because a bond already existed and the work is restoring a prior state. The framing that makes it more defensible is to accept that the other person may decline again, and to define success as a reply rather than a reconciliation.

Do love sigils actually produce attraction?

What they reliably produce is obsessive rumination, which is a different thing and a worse one. The mechanism is attention committed to an object, so a target-aimed sigil trains your attention onto that person, and fixation makes every interaction legible as evidence. The only version with a defensible mechanism is aimed at reciprocity, meaning your own availability, communication and willingness to be known, where you are the one being changed.

Is it manipulative to cast a love sigil on someone?

By most ethical frameworks, including most occult ones, yes. The problem is that a target-aimed operation removes the other person's ability to choose, and that remains the core issue no matter how the intent is framed. The generating method being impersonal does not help, since that is a claim about the tool rather than the act. Every tradition that has written seriously on this has eventually reached the same conclusion, which is a fair signal.

What is cascading and why should I care?

It is the escalation pattern occult sources describe when love magic is repeated against the same target: more frequent operations, more elaborate framings, and a narrowing of the person's other relationships until the target becomes the whole field of view. Drop the metaphysics and the behavioural description still holds, which is the useful part. Setting a written operation limit and telling someone uninvolved costs nothing and does more than any intention you could set.

Is there a paid app that helps with relationship work?

For this area of practice the only thing that reliably helps is a written record, because the failure mode is doing the same operation repeatedly while calling it persistence. A tool that keeps your operations, your limits, your review dates and your outcomes in one dated log is the useful category, and a written limit you set while calm is worth more than anything. I would rather own a place for the record than hold a subscription to something I might stop caring about next month, and I would want that place to hold my limits rather than my prompts.
