Escarlate

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 27, 2026 ⬢ 9 min read

A sequence is worth having precisely because it removes decisions. The temptation with any named figure is to spend the whole sitting deciding what to do next, and a sitting spent deciding is a sitting where the office never gets charged. This page is therefore a script rather than a set of suggestions: what to write first, what to declare, what to build, what to hold, and what to do when your attention breaks in the middle of it. The last of those is the step most people have never been told about, and it is the one that decides whether the rest of the sequence holds together.

The sequence, and why the order is not negotiable

The order exists because each step reduces the number of live questions in the next one. Office first means the form has something to be. Call second means the form has a job. Charge third means the two are in place before you need them. Notes last, while the material is still warm. Each step removes a decision, and a decision made inside a charge is a decision that displaces attention from the thing being charged.

Practitioners rearrange this constantly, usually in the direction of what they find most interesting. Beginning with the form, or with the call, or with an hour of visualising before writing anything, all feel more like real working and all produce a weaker record. The order is not a rule about power. It is a procedure about not wasting the one resource the charge actually uses, which is sustained attention.

Deciding the office before the candle

One line, present tense, four words or fewer, and something you would recognise as a job if it were described to you at an interview. The present tense matters because it removes the option of narrating a history, and the history is where most of the inflation lives. Not protecting my interests, but: interrupting one plan. Not bringing justice, but: closing one argument.

Write it and then read it aloud. If you flinch at any part of it, cut that part. The flinch is the most reliable indicator available of where the borrowing has outrun the text, and it appears as discomfort long before it appears as narration in the notes. The office you keep is the one you can say in a room without lowering your voice.

Writing the call in a single line

The call is a request in the imperative, and it should be one sentence that would be meaningless without the office in front of you. Interrupt this plan. Interfere with this negotiation. Watch this conversation. It is not a name, a title, or an invocation of lineage, because the lineage is not doing any work here and including it invites the question of whether the lineage is real.

There is a reason the call is short. A long call is a paragraph, and a paragraph is a stream of meaning that your attention has to hold open, and holding a stream open is the one thing a charge cannot do. The compression is functional. If your call is three sentences, you have not written a longer call, you have written a charge that will be interrupted by your own prose.

The opening: declaring, not bargaining

State the name, the office, and the length of the working. Nothing else is required and nothing else helps. Bargaining, promising, thanking in advance, offering a trade, or asking permission all introduce a second subject into the room, and the second subject is you.

This is not a claim about entities. It is a procedure about the only thing that is verifiably present in the sitting, which is your own attention. When you negotiate, part of the attention is on the terms. When you declare, the whole of it is available for the figure and the office. If a figure is going to be described as though it were a person in the room, the terms you are negotiating with are the terms you have written yourself, and that is the most likely source of any feeling of having been responded to.

Building the image from a description, not an invention

Take the form as the text gives it and nothing more: a shape, a posture, a number of limbs or eyes if that is stated, and two or three attributes. If the description is thin, the image will be thin, and a thin image is a fine instrument. The point is to have something steady to hold attention on, not to produce a convincing image.

Inventing detail is the common failure and it is usually done to solve a real problem, which is that a vague figure gives you nothing to look at. The solution for vagueness is duration and repetition rather than elaboration. Hold the same three-attribute image for fifteen minutes across four sessions and it will acquire detail on its own, and that detail is more useful to you than an image you composed in ninety seconds, because you will recognise what your attention actually supplied.

What the charge is: attention held flat

Fifteen minutes, eyes closed or open, the image in attention, the call repeated silently. Flat is the operative word. The charge is not a visualisation to be deepened, not a journey to be followed, and not an intensity to be built. It is a steady hold, and the steady hold is the entire mechanism.

The effect that follows is ordinary and documented: sustained attention on a compressed representation while an intention is held in words raises the salience of that intention in your own behaviour over the following weeks. That is a small effect with an unremarkable explanation, and it is the whole of what you are doing. A person who charges a working and then goes to sleep expecting an event will be disappointed, and correctly so, because nothing was promised by the charge itself.

The interruption, and restarting rather than continuing

Your attention will break. Somewhere in the middle of the charge you will notice that you have been thinking about something else for several minutes, or that the image has become a set of features you are describing, or that you are bored. This is normal, it happens to everyone, and what you do next is the step that most sequences omit.

Do not continue. Stop the charge, open your eyes, take sixty seconds, and begin again from the top with the call and the image. Restarting rather than continuing is not pedantry. A continued charge after the attention has gone somewhere else produces a record in which the first part is the only part that happened, and because the sitting feels long, the record overstates it. Restarting keeps your notes honest, and an honest record is the only thing the exercise produces.

Closing the working in the same session

One sentence, out loud, in the present tense, saying the sitting is closed. Then stand, and leave the room, or at least change what you are doing for five minutes. A closed working left open bleeds into the rest of the evening, and the bleeding is a real effect: your attention stays partly on the office, and the charge of the next session begins from a partly occupied state.

This is the least interesting step in the sequence and the one most often dropped, which is a reasonable place to economise if you are short on time. If you have fifteen minutes rather than forty, drop the call to a single word and keep the closing. A closed charge is worth more over a month than an open one is worth in a single sitting.

What to write in the ten minutes after

Four things, in this order: whether the charge was held or restarted and how many times, what the image actually became, what you felt, and what you are sure of versus what you are imagining. That last split is the whole discipline and it takes two seconds per line if you write it as a heading rather than a paragraph.

The count of restarts is the most valuable number in the whole exercise, because it is the one item in the record that is under your control and that you can improve. Most people begin with four or five restarts in fifteen minutes. Most people who keep the number are at two within a month. That change is not about the figure; it is about their own capacity for a flat hold, and it is a real, transferable skill.

Where the sequence fails at step three

Step three is the charge, and the failure is almost always the form rather than the duration. If the image you are holding has no detail, the attention has nothing to grip and will wander within a minute or two. If the form is rich and the office is vague, the charge is strong and the outcome is nil, because you have put a great deal of attention on nothing in particular.

The diagnosis is available from the notes. High restarts plus a thin form means build the form. High restarts plus a rich form and a clear office means reduce the length, because fifteen minutes is past the edge of your capacity and a charge you cannot hold is a charge that did not happen. The third possibility is the most common of all: the office names something you do not actually want, and the wandering attention is the only part of you that has noticed.

A sequence you can hand to someone else

The test of a written sequence is whether another person can follow it without asking you what to do. Write it out as a numbered list on one page, with the office blank, and hand it to someone who has never done this. The blanks you leave are the parts of the sequence you have not actually decided yet, and they are usually hidden inside phrasing that sounds decided.

Keeping that page is worth more than any notebook. It is a reusable template for any name, it makes the office the only variable you have to supply, and it removes the possibility that a session is quietly improvised on the basis of what you felt like doing. The template is the part of this practice that scales, and the figure is not.

What to change after the first month

One variable, chosen from the record rather than from a hunch. The candidates are narrow: the office, the length of the charge, the hour, the frequency, the form. If restarts are still high, change the form or the length. If restarts are low and the record is empty, change the office, because the charge is working and the aim is not.

Do not change the sequence itself in the first month. A sequence that is altered while you are still learning it cannot be evaluated, and the most common way people lose a working is by improving it. Month one is for finding out which step you skipped. Month two is for changing exactly that one thing and leaving the other nine alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the right order to build a working in?

Office, then call, then form, then charge, then notes. Each step narrows what the next one has to decide, and a decision made during a charge displaces the attention the charge needs. The order is not a claim about power. It is a procedure about not spending the only resource the practice actually consumes, which is sustained attention.

What should I do when my attention breaks during a charge?

Stop, open your eyes, take about a minute, and restart from the beginning. Do not continue. A charge that has been broken and then continued produces a record in which only the first part happened, and because the sitting felt long you will overestimate it. Restarting costs two or three minutes and keeps your notes true, which is the only output the exercise has.

How long should a charge be for a first working?

Fifteen minutes, with restarts counted. For many beginners that is already past their capacity for a flat hold, and the honest response is to shorten it rather than to push through. What matters is that you can hold it without attention leaving, and the number of restarts in your notes is the measurement of that. When restarts fall to one or two, lengthen the sitting.

Should the call or invocation name the entity?

A single short line naming the figure, the office, and the length of the working is enough. Longer invocations that add lineage, titles, or a history of the name introduce a second subject and split your attention, and the lineage is not doing any work in the practice. What matters is that the sentence would be meaningless without the office in front of you, because that is what binds the charge to a specific aim.

How do I tell whether the working is producing anything?

Read the record, not the feeling. The record has three fields: the count of restarts, whether the office was still narrow enough to state in one line, and any decision you took in the following week that you would not have taken otherwise. Only the third is an outcome, and it is the only one worth claiming. A warm feeling after a charge is not a result and should not be written as one.
