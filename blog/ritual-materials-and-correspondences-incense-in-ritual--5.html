The law, and the word that gives it away

Correspondence claims that there is a resemblance between everything above and everything below, and that the resemblance runs through a chain: a planet, a metal, a colour, a day, a number, a set of qualities, a god. The word doing the work is as above, so below. It is a claim about resemblance, and resemblance is not contact.

That distinction is the whole argument. If a table entry is a description of a shared quality, then acting on one entry while holding the others in mind is a coherence exercise. If it is a description of a force, then the table is a physics and the candles are an instrument. The literature supports the first reading considerably better than the second, and almost all the incoherence people notice comes from switching between them.

Where the tables come from

The organising principle is codified in tables that reach back to the three books published in 1533, the first major printed Western synthesis of planetary, angelic and demonic hierarchies. They remain a primary source, which means you can check them, and they are also a sixteenth-century document, which means their groupings are a sixteenth-century classification.

The chain got there by accumulation rather than derivation. Arabic, Jewish and Latin material went into the early compilations; a corpus associated with Hermes was translated into Latin in the fifteenth century and then combined with the other strands, and the resulting tables are the product of that combination rather than of any observation about the world.

A filing convention, and nothing more than that

Anything can be described along the same set of axes, and if the axes are chosen well enough then two things described on the same axes can be treated as one thing in a working. That is a description of a filing convention and it is a genuinely useful one.

It also explains why the tables are so satisfying and so unfalsifiable at the same time. Any pair of things can be joined if you are willing to redefine one of them, and the redefinition is invisible because it happens inside the join. The cure for that is to write down what the join asserts, in ordinary words, before using it.

Four choices that have to agree with each other

A correspondence practice that is internally inconsistent does not work as a correspondence practice, whatever else happens. Pick a planet, pick a metal, pick a colour, pick a day, and let each one say the same thing. That is harder than it sounds, because the tables disagree with each other in places, and because the newest material will happily add a correspondence that no older source supports.

The discipline is arithmetic rather than mystical. Four choices, one meaning, no contradictions. Once that holds, the material becomes a statement in a language you built, and the statement is what the ritual is actually made of.

Three claims the tables never make

They do not claim that Mars causes anything. They do not claim that a ruby carries an influence. They do not claim that burning a specific substance changes an outcome in the world. Read as a table of qualities, they are a way of making a set of materials say one thing instead of five.

This is a narrower claim than people want and it is enough for a practice to be coherent. A working built on four consistent correspondences is a working with a uniform symbolic field, and the uniformity is the active ingredient. Everything else in the arrangement is a way of making that field easier to stand in.

Smell, and the only mechanism in the whole apparatus

Smell is unusually durable as a memory channel and largely independent of verbal memory, which is not a occult claim. It is a well-supported finding, and it explains why a distinctive scent becomes an effective anchor for a sequence.

This is the honest mechanism in the whole apparatus, and it is worth more than any table. A scent that is yours, used for one working type, will return you to that state faster than any symbol, because the association is old and automatic rather than symbolic and argued. If you want a component that does measurable work, it is that one.

One page, five rows, and the column that keeps you honest

One page. Five rows: the material, the quality, the correspondence chosen, the source, and the date you last checked it. The source column is the one that keeps you honest, and the date column is the one that tells you when a book you copied from was published.

Then the test. Read the page as a sentence. If the five rows do not say the same thing, you have not got a correspondence, you have got five objects. Fixing it takes ten minutes and it is the difference between a set and a pile.

The power column, and why reading it ruins the practice

The power column. In most tables of this kind there is a column listing what a correspondence is associated with in the world, and that column is where the correspondence stops being a description and becomes a claim about causation.

Almost all the incoherence in correspondence practice comes from reading that column as a prediction. It is a list of associations, accumulated by people who were not running experiments, and the associations are not wrong so much as unfalsifiable. Treat the column as a source of imagery and leave the causal reading alone.

Check the law before you pick the substance

One substance with documented religious and magical use across Hindu, Rastafarian and folk-Christian contexts has a legal status that changes sharply between jurisdictions and sometimes between municipalities. That is not a footnote. It is the constraint that decides whether a particular material practice is available to you at all.

The sensible approach is boring: check what is legal where you live before building a practice around a substance, keep substitutes available, and treat the legal question as part of the correspondence rather than as an obstacle to it. A practice that depends on something you may be arrested for is a practice with a single point of failure, and the failure is not metaphysical.

Where correspondence misleads

It misleads by inviting you to believe that a symbol has a property. You light a candle for a planet and the candle is now a channel, and the language of channel is the problem. It is a piece of wax with a colour and a shape, chosen because the choosing is meaningful.

It also misleads in the other direction, which is more common and less discussed. People read correspondence as arbitrary and dismiss a working on that basis, when the arbitrariness is the feature. The symbols are chosen, the choice is yours, and the value is in the choosing and the repetition rather than in any property of the material.

The ethics of the arrangement

Two of them. First, an inanimate arrangement cannot consent, and treating it as though it can is the beginning of a habit that will be applied to people later. The materials do not agree to anything. The practitioner agreed, in advance, by building the set.

Second, the legal question. Not because a law is a moral authority, but because the material constraint is where a practice becomes a risk to people who are not in the room. Handle it as a design constraint rather than a moral judgement and you will get a more durable practice than the one that ignores it.

The sentence worth carrying

The tables are a classification, not a physics. The symbols are chosen, and the choosing is what carries the meaning. The one component with a documented mechanism is the smell. And the substance column has to survive a legal check before it goes anywhere near a working.

Four sentences, and each one removes a mistake. A correspondence practice built on them is narrower than the popular version and considerably easier to run for thirty years.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does correspondence mean planets actually influence things?

It does not, on the evidence. Read as a table of qualities and resemblances, correspondence is a way of making several materials say one thing, which is a useful coherence exercise. Read as a physics it becomes a claim about causation that no table supports, and the switch between those two readings is the source of most incoherence in practice.

What is the practical function of a correspondence table?

Coordination. Choosing a planet, a metal, a colour and a day that all carry the same quality turns a collection of objects into a single symbolic statement, and the uniformity is the active ingredient. The test is whether the five rows say the same thing when you read them as a sentence. If they do not, you have a pile rather than a set.

Which part of a material practice has real evidence behind it?

Smell. Memory for scent is durable in a way that does not depend much on verbal recall, the evidence for that is good, and that is why a distinctive scent in one specific kind of working becomes a reliable anchor. It is a stronger candidate for the active ingredient than any symbolic attribution in the tables.

How should I handle the power column in a correspondence table?

Read it as a list of associations rather than a set of predictions, which is what it is. That column is where correspondence stops being a description and starts implying causation, and it accumulated without anyone running experiments. Use it for imagery and leave the causal reading alone.

What about substances that are restricted by law?

Check the local position before building a practice around one, keep a substitute available, and treat legality as a design constraint. Some substances with genuine documented religious and magical use have a status that varies sharply between jurisdictions, and a practice that depends on something you may be arrested for has a single point of failure that has nothing to do with symbolism.
