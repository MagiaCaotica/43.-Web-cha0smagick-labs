The Clauneck Wealth Ritual, Reviewed Honestly

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 16 min read

Take the framing off the Clauneck wealth ritual and what is left is short enough to write on one line: a sigil, a charge, a timing choice, and a defined action repeated on a schedule you set in advance. That is the whole instrument. Everything else in the presentation, the secret, the legion, the thirty thousand soldiers, the inherited fire and the inherited darkness, is packaging around four practical components, and the four components are where all the actual effect lives. This article takes the ritual apart, keeps the parts that work, and names the four ways it fails that have nothing to do with any demon.

The ritual, with the framing removed

Four components. The sigil is a glyph built for one specific intention, constructed rather than drawn, and its job is to hold a meaning you would otherwise have to keep re-arguing in your head. The charge is the act of putting attention into that glyph, and it is the only component that requires a person. The timing choice is when you do the charge, and in a well-built working it is a small number of specific windows rather than a general lucky period. The defined action is what you do afterwards, on a schedule, and it is the component that does the work in the world.

That is it. Four items, and three of them are ordinary craft that any chaos magician already does with sigils for other purposes. If your practice is sound, you can perform this ritual tomorrow without learning anything about goetic tradition beyond the name attached to it, and the results will be within the ordinary range of your technique. This is not a deflation. It is the reason the ritual works when it works.

The one component that carries real risk is the defined action, and it carries it because it is the one that changes your life whether or not the rest of the apparatus functions. A working with no action attached produces nothing in the world and produces a great deal in the practitioner's interior. Most failed wealth workings are not failed because the demon declined. They are failed because the fourth component was left blank, and the blank was filled by reassurance.

The word secret is the product, not a description

A secret is not a description of a thing. It is a description of the relationship between the seller and the buyer. When a ritual is called secret, the property being sold is the seller's withholding, and the ritual is the alibi. This is worth stating plainly because the framing does a real amount of work on the reader before any technique is applied, and it changes what a person believes is possible.

The four components cannot be secret. Sigil construction is described in full in Spare, Carroll and Chaos. Charging methods are described in full in hundreds of places. Timing tables are astronomical arithmetic. A defined action is a defined action. Anyone who wanted to reconstruct this working from scratch could do it in an afternoon, and the number of people who have in fact arrived at something workable this way is very large, which is itself the argument.

What keeps the framing alive is that the secret is renewable and the technique is not. A technique that has been published can be applied by anyone and therefore stops being scarce. A withholding has to be re-established on every audience, which means the presentation is never finished. So the framing is not an accident of marketing. It is the only part of the offer that cannot be commoditised, and it gets the most attention for exactly that reason.

The practical consequence is small and useful. Ask which of the four components you are actually being given and which you are being told about. A ritual that supplies a sigil, a charge schedule and a defined action is a complete offer with a lot of decoration. A ritual that supplies a story about why it is secret, and no defined action, is a decoration being sold as an offer. You can tell them apart in about two minutes of reading.

Three mechanisms, and the honest question of which one is running

Expectancy restructuring is the first, and it is the most reliably documented of the three. Deciding in advance, in writing, on a specific date, that a specific amount is in your account by a specific date changes how you behave over the interval. You stop spending at the margin, you take the call you were avoiding, you notice the opportunity you had been reading past. None of this is supernatural and all of it is real, and it is the mechanism that produces most of the durable results in a well-run wealth working.

Implementation intent is the second. A written statement of what you will do, with a day attached, converts a resolution into a scheduled action, and the conversion rate between the two is where most money is quietly lost. The resolution exists in a warm state with no date on it and dies there. The dated statement survives contact with Tuesday, and surviving contact with Tuesday is most of the achievement.

Decision compression is the third, and it is the least discussed. Once you have decided, the number of decisions that follow is much smaller, because you are no longer deliberating. You are executing. Deliberation is expensive in time and energy and it is where the actual money leaks, and closing it down is a genuine mechanical advantage rather than a psychological one.

Here is the honest question underneath all three. If these mechanisms are sufficient to produce most of the durable results, what exactly is the demon doing? There are three available answers and practitioners pick one by temperament rather than by evidence. The deflationist answer is that nothing is added. The enchantment answer is that the added presence makes the mechanisms more reliably operative, through attention and commitment. The agnostic answer is that the question is malformed, because the system is the four components together and decomposing it is a category error. The agnostic answer is the one that survives contact with outcomes, and it is also the one that requires the least defending in conversation.

The failure that looks exactly like success

This is the one that costs the most and it is never mentioned. A wealth working with a sigil, a charge, and a defined action that is not careful about money can produce an increase in spending that the practitioner experiences as an increase in flow. The new behaviour includes spending, and the spending is on things that feel like abundance, and the practitioner logs the whole thing as evidence.

It is entirely explicable without any magic. You have written down a money intention, you have begun paying attention to money, and attention to money increases both the noticing of it and the volume of decisions about it. If the defined action is discretionary spending, or if the working has produced a sense of being allowed to spend, the spending rises. The balance sheet is worse and the mood is better, and the mood is the metric most people are actually tracking.

The two are easy to tell apart and almost nobody checks. A genuine increase shows up as more in the account over a full cycle, including the months when nothing happened. A false positive shows up as a change in velocity, and velocity is the thing that changes first in both directions. Write down the balance at the start, the balance at the review date, and the total spent in between. Three numbers, and they are the only ones that settle the question.

There is a second, related trap in the same family. A working can produce a large amount of attention and negotiation about money without producing any money, and the negotiation feels like progress because it is effortful and it is about the topic you care about. If the review date arrives and the balance has not moved while the amount of thinking about money has gone up sharply, the working produced rumination, and rumination is a common and very convincing substitute for the real thing.

Why timing systems amplify, and where the claim breaks

Lunar and planetary timing systems are the most technically sound component in a wealth working and the most over-claimed. The astronomy is real. Moon phase and phase angle are computable to the arcminute, the planetary hour charts are deterministic given a date, a location and a time, and the tables involved have not meaningfully changed in centuries. That is a genuine foundation, and it is worth more than the interpretive overlay most practitioners add on top of it.

The mechanism by which timing helps is mundane and solid. A narrow window forces you to be ready at a specific moment, which means you have to have done the preparation in advance, which means you did the preparation. Scheduling a hard constraint on a favourable day is a deadline. Deadlines are the most reliable behaviour-change device that exists, and a planetary hour chart is a deadline that you did not have to invent, which makes it psychologically easier to accept.

The claim that breaks is the causal one. That the moon is doing something to your circumstances is not supported by the mechanism, and the honest description is that the window is useful because it is specific. A practitioner who uses the astronomical window and credits the planet is doing the same thing as a practitioner who works on new moons because new moons feel right, except that the astronomically grounded version is more consistent, which is the part that actually earns the effort.

The practical version is worth the whole discussion. Use the astronomical system because it is exact, because it gives you a hard date, and because consistency is easier to maintain than discernment. Drop the causal claim, or at least hold it loosely, because the day where the planet is not cooperating is a day you will otherwise spend explaining to yourself why you did not charge the sigil.

The review date is the only part that matters

Every one of the four components is optional except one. You can improvise the sigil, you can improvise the charge, and you can improvise the timing within a very wide range and still produce a working. You cannot improvise the review, because the review is the thing that converts a ritual experience into information.

The review has a specific form. A date, chosen in advance, before the working. A number, written down before the working. And a decision rule, written down before the working, that says what you do depending on what the number does. That last part is the one that is always skipped, and it is the one that determines whether the review produces a decision or produces a mood. A review without a pre-committed decision rule is a ritual for generating feelings about money, and feelings are the thing you already had.

Write the decision rule in the most boring possible form. If the number has not moved by the date, run the working again with the defined action made more specific. If the number has moved, stop and do not increase the dose. If the number has moved and you are spending more, stop immediately and address the spending. Three branches, written down, in advance, and the working becomes an instrument rather than a mood.

The reason this matters so much is that the review is what makes the practice falsifiable. Everything else in a wealth working is self-confirming, because the same person who did the working evaluates whether it worked, and that person is invested, and the material is symbolic, and the outcome is slow. A dated number with a pre-committed branch is the single thing in the whole structure that can contradict you. Without it you have built a practice that cannot be wrong, and a practice that cannot be wrong is not a practice.

The version with no demon in it

It is worth writing out the same working with the goetic frame removed entirely, because the comparison is instructive and because some readers will want to do this. There is a sigil, because a constructed glyph concentrates a complex intention into something you can act on without re-arguing. There is a charge, because the charge is the moment of commitment and it is the only part of the structure that requires you to be present. There is a timing window, because a hard date is a deadline. And there is a defined action, repeated on a schedule.

That is the whole ritual, and it is the version that most consistently produces results, because every component is doing a job you can point at. The sigil holds the intention. The charge commits. The date constrains. The action changes the world. Remove the demon and nothing mechanical is lost, and what remains is a symbolic-technique core that works in a dozen domains.

This is not a claim that the goetic frame is worthless. It is a claim about where the value sits. The frame supplies a specific kind of permission, a sense of addressing something with standing rather than manipulating a mechanism, and that permission is not nothing when the technique is fine and the resolve is absent. What the frame cannot do is supply the fourth component, and the fourth component is the one that determines the outcome.

The most productive practitioners I would point to as a model work both ways. They use a demon when the framing helps them commit, and they can drop the demon when the technique is doing the work and the permission is no longer needed. The people who only ever work one way tend to be either unable to commit without external authority, or unable to sustain a technique once the symbolism is stripped.

Signals that the four components are working

The signal set is entirely behavioural and entirely checkable, which is the reason it is short. You start doing the thing you had been meaning to do. The margin on some category of spending goes up, which shows up as a balance rather than as a feeling. You take an action you had been avoiding, and you take it earlier than before. You find that you have more conversations about the specific thing you were working on, which usually means the situation has become more real and therefore more negotiable.

One positive signal is specific to timing-based work and worth naming because it is the strongest one available. You begin planning ahead of the window instead of on the day. A practitioner who is charging a sigil three days early, having sorted the materials and written the intent, has demonstrated the thing the ritual was supposed to produce, and they have demonstrated it before the ritual happened. That is the mechanism, visible.

The negative signals are the same four that show up in every working. Vocabulary without behaviour, which means richer language and identical conduct. Escalating intensity for the same effect. Loss of privacy that you did not choose. And the one that ends it, the practice starting to cost sleep, health, money or relationships that have nothing to do with the practice. None of the four are ambiguous, and all four are the practitioner's, not the demon's.

When to stop, and what stopping looks like

The stopping rule is simple and it should be written at the beginning rather than discovered at the end. Two reviews, on the two pre-committed dates, with the pre-committed decision rules applied at each. If the balance has not moved across both, the working is not working, and the reason is far more likely to be the defined action than anything else. Stop and rewrite the fourth component with something more concrete than it currently is, or accept that the outcome is not available to you in the time frame you set.

There is a second stopping condition, which is behavioural and which overrides the schedule. If the practice has started to require the sacrifice of ordinary capacity, stop immediately and treat the loss as the result rather than as the price. Sleep, health, income and relationships are the costs that matter. A working that takes them is not a strong working, and the escalation pattern is the same one that shows up in every other compulsive structure.

The third is the one that requires the most honesty. If the only thing the working reliably produces is the ability to talk about money, and the balance has never moved across two reviews, then the practice is functioning as a way of managing the feeling of not having enough, and that is a real thing to need, and it is not what you set out to do. In that case the useful move is to keep the sigil craft and drop the wealth frame, and to address the actual problem directly.

Stopping is not a failure of nerve and it is not evidence about the demon. It is a measurement. Two reviews, a written number, a written rule, and you have an answer. Very few people in this field ever generate that much information about their own workings, and having it is worth more than the working was.

What to build if you want this to actually work

Start with the fourth component and write it before anything else, because it is the one that does the work and the one that is always written last or not at all. Not a category, not a feeling. A number, an action and a frequency, on a named day. A transfer on the first, three calls made on the fourth, one proposal sent by the seventh. If you cannot name the action, you are not ready to build the sigil, and the sigil will turn out to be the part you enjoy most, which is a trap.

Then build the sigil, and build it properly, because it is a real technique with real craft and it is cheap to learn. Construct rather than draw, one glyph for one intention, and check it against your own method for a while before you rely on it in something that matters. The discipline of writing a sigil well is the discipline of stating your intention precisely enough that you cannot hand-wave it, which is worth having on its own.

Then set the timing from the astronomical tables, take the earliest usable window, and put the date in the calendar at the moment you build the sigil rather than when you get around to charging. A window that is selected in advance is a deadline. A window selected on the day is a preference, and preferences do not produce the scheduling behaviour that the rest of the structure depends on.

Then write the review. One number, one date, three branches. And keep the record, because the accumulated record across several workings is the only real data you will ever accumulate, and it is the difference between a practice and a series of private experiences you have had.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Clauneck wealth ritual require a demon or a pact to work?

No. Strip the goetic frame and four components remain, a sigil, a charge, a timing window and a defined action, and all four are ordinary chaos magic technique that works in domains with no demon involved at all. The frame contributes a sense of addressing something with standing, which can help with commitment, but it supplies none of the mechanics. The component that determines the outcome is the defined action, and that is not the demonic part.

How long should a money ritual run before I judge it?

Two reviews on two pre-committed dates, with the decision rules written before the first one. Shorter than that and slow-moving outcomes have not had time to register. Much longer and you have lost the falsifiability, because a working evaluated only after a year can be made to look successful by any amount of reinterpretation. Two dated reviews is the point where you have real information and you still have the option to change the method.

Is spending more during a money ritual a sign it is working?

Usually the opposite. Increased spending alongside a money working most often means the attention to money has increased without the income moving, or that the working has produced a sense of permission rather than a change in the balance. The three numbers that settle it are the starting balance, the balance at the review date, and the total spent in between. Rising spending with a flat or falling balance is a false positive, and it feels exactly like success while it is happening.

Do I need the lunar or planetary timing system to get results?

The timing system is useful and the causal claim behind it is not established. What the astronomical windows reliably provide is a hard, externally derived date, which forces preparation in advance and creates a genuine deadline, and deadlines are the most reliable behaviour-change device available. Use the tables because they are exact and consistent. Drop the belief that the planet is participating, because that belief costs you the days you skip the charge when the sky does not look right.

Why is a review with a pre-written decision rule so important?

Because it is the only part of the structure that can contradict you. Everything else in a wealth working is self-confirming, because the practitioner who did the working is the one judging it, the material is symbolic, and the outcome is slow. A dated number with a branch committed to in advance, repeat the working or stop or address the spending, converts the practice from something that feels like it is working into something that can be wrong. Without it, there is no instrument, only an experience.
