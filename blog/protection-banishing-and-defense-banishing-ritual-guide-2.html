Protection, Banishing and Defense

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 27, 2026 ⬢ 11 min read

A shield working and a banishing working are different operations, and the difference is easy to state and routinely ignored. A banishing declares a limit and asks for removal. A shield declares a limit and asks for it to hold, and it therefore requires something that was optional in the banishing: a witness, a maintenance schedule, and a record. The default rite in most of the ceremonial tradition is a banishing, which is why people who reach for it when they want a shield report that nothing happened and conclude the wrong thing. What follows is the operational sequence for a shield, in order, with the parts marked that are load-bearing and the parts that are decoration. It assumes no particular tradition and no particular text, and it can be done in one evening the first time and fifteen minutes on the occasions after that.

The shield, and what it is not

A shield is a maintained boundary. It has three components that the ceremonial tradition treats separately and that a working has to bind together in a single sitting: a declaration of what the boundary is, a witness to it, and a maintenance action that keeps it in place. Remove any one of the three and what remains is a different operation. Without the witness a declaration is a sentence. Without maintenance a shield is a ceremony performed once.

What a shield is not is a talisman carried on the person. A talisman is a portable object and its operation is proximity. A shield is a maintained boundary and its operation is a relationship between a declared limit and the conditions it is supposed to hold under. The two are frequently confused, and the confusion is expensive, because a talisman gives the feeling of maintenance without the maintenance and therefore produces a boundary that quietly stops holding without any moment at which you would notice.

Deciding the boundary before the words

The boundary has to be named before anything is said, and the naming is the step that does the work. Not what you are protecting yourself from, which is usually a category too broad to be maintained, but which specific situation, in which specific place, for how long. A boundary that reads as no interruptions to my work in this room between nine and six is maintainable, and it can be checked, because at six you know whether it held.

The reason to be this specific is that a broad boundary has no failure condition. If the declaration is that you are protected from all harm, then a bad afternoon is consistent with it, and there is no event that could be counted as a breach. A narrow boundary has a test. This is the same principle that makes a written aim countable, and it is the difference between an operation and a gesture.

Writing the declaration in one line

The declaration goes in one line, in the imperative, naming the situation, the limit, and the duration. The imperative matters for a structural reason rather than a rhetorical one: an indicative sentence describes a state and a command issues one, and a working that issues a command is a working that requires a response. Most of the ceremonial versions are in the indicative and it costs nothing to put yours in the imperative.

It also matters that the line is short. The default rite is a paragraph, and a paragraph can be recited without deciding anything because it is already written. A line you composed has to be decided, and the deciding is where the specificity comes from. If you cannot get the boundary down to a line, you have not decided the boundary yet, which is a useful diagnostic even though it is uncomfortable.

The witness, and why something must observe it

A witness is whatever observes the declaration, and in ceremonial practice it is usually an element that is given a name and a function. It is not optional, and the reason is not that spirits require witnesses. It is that a declaration made only to yourself is a note, and a declaration made in the presence of something you have specified is a public act within a system you have partly built.

The witness can be an object in the room, a part of your own attention held in a particular way, or a written record that the declaration is entered into. What all three have in common is that they are not you doing the same thing again. The simplest version that works in practice is a written entry with a time on it, and it costs nothing, and it is the component most often left out of informal workings on the grounds that the intention is enough. The intention is not enough because nothing observes an intention.

The rising, timed to the end of the sentence

Many traditions specify a physical movement as part of the banishing, and the best-documented version is the rising: standing, moving toward the door, and completing the declaration as you arrive at it. The movement is not symbolic in the sense of standing for something else. It is the exit, and it is timed to the end of the sentence so that the boundary and the departure are a single action rather than a declaration followed by a movement that could have happened in the other order.

In a shield working the rising does something slightly different and the difference is worth naming. In a banishing the rising is the physical component of removing something. In a shield the rising marks the inside, because you end up standing at the threshold having just declared what the threshold is. That is the whole content of the gesture, and it is why a shield sequence that omits the movement tends to produce a boundary with no location attached to it.

Where the shield differs from the banishing

The differences are four and they are all consequences of maintenance. A banishing is complete when it is said; a shield is complete when it is first declared and then never complete again. A banishing asks for removal and accepts a return; a shield asks for a condition to hold and therefore needs a condition under which it is reasserted. A banishing has no record because nothing needs checking; a shield has a record because a shield that fails without anyone noticing is the definition of not having had one.

The practical consequence is that the shield sequence has a longer tail. Most of the material in a shield working is not in the first evening, it is in the fortnight afterwards when you find out whether the declaration was specific enough to be testable. Practitioners who find the first evening satisfying and then stop are running a banishing and experiencing it as a disappointment, which is the most common account I have of the rite not working.

Twenty-five minutes, and the five that matter most

The order is: choose the boundary, write the line, put the room or the situation in the state you are declaring, speak the line, move to the threshold, witness it in writing with a time, and then sit down for a few minutes before doing anything else. That last step is not decoration. Four minutes is what separates a working from a moment. The sitting is where the maintenance schedule gets decided, and if it is skipped the schedule is never made and the shield has no second operation.

Total time on the first evening is about twenty-five minutes, and roughly half of that is the twenty minutes of writing and revising the line. If you cannot spend that, the honest thing is to run a banishing instead, which is a perfectly good operation and takes five minutes, rather than to run a shield with a boundary nobody chose.

Maintaining it after the first week

The maintenance is a reassertion, and its frequency should be set in the first sitting rather than decided later. The options are daily, which is more attention than most people want to give a boundary, or tied to an event, which is the one that works: you reassert when the condition you declared recurs, which is the first time you notice the boundary being approached. Event-triggered maintenance is also the only version that scales, since it does not require you to remember.

A reassertion is short and it is identical in form to the original. The line does not change, the witness does, the movement does not. What changes is that you are now doing it for the second time and the second time is the one that tells you whether the first was a declaration or a sentence. If the second one requires a new composition, you did not have a boundary in the first place, you had an occasion.

The fortnight when you forget

You will forget. The shield sequence has a long tail and nobody holds a boundary in mind continuously, and the fortnight when you forget is not a failure of discipline, it is the ordinary condition of a maintained thing. The response is a reminder tied to the event rather than to your memory, and the cheapest version is a note on the object you touch first in the morning.

The more useful response is to ask what the forgetting tells you about the boundary. A boundary you never test is a boundary you have no evidence about, and a boundary you test only when the fear returns is one that tracks fear rather than conditions. If you notice that you reassert only when something has already gone wrong, the boundary is being maintained by the wrong trigger and it will fail the next time something goes wrong that you did not notice happening.

A record that would show a breach

The record for a shield is four columns and it is designed so that a breach would be visible rather than reinterpretable. What was declared, in the line as written. When it was reasserted. What happened in the declared situation. And a fourth column recording what you would have done in any case, which is the column that stops a bad day from being counted as a breach after the fact.

The fourth column is the hardest and the most valuable. Without it, any intrusion in the protected situation is a failure of the shield, and the number of failures is then determined by your attention rather than by the boundary. With it, you can distinguish a week where something happened and you would have done the same thing anyway from a week where something happened because the boundary was not holding, and the second is a finding while the first is a Tuesday.

When the shield should be rebuilt

Rebuild rather than maintain when the boundary has been breached in a way the record shows, when the situation you declared has changed so that the line is now describing something that no longer exists, or when the maintenance has become automatic enough that you could not say when you last did it deliberately. Those three conditions cover most rebuilds, and the third is the one people miss because automatic maintenance feels identical to good maintenance from the inside.

The reason to rebuild rather than reassert is that a reassertion preserves the declaration, including any vagueness in it. If the boundary failed, the most likely reason is that the line was broader than a situation, and reasserting a broad line produces a broader failure. Rebuilding means going back to the writing, and the writing is where the specificity comes from, so a rebuild is a return to the first evening rather than an addition to it.

What the sequence cannot protect you from

It cannot protect you from a situation you failed to declare, and the failure mode is always the same: the boundary gets drawn around the easy one. The interruption that ruins the morning is declared and the thing that ruins the year is not, because the second is harder to name in a line. Every shield sequence in every tradition has this property and no amount of ceremony changes it.

It also cannot protect you from inside the boundary, and this is worth being blunt about because the phrase implies otherwise. A declared limit on intrusions says nothing about your own conduct within the protected space, and a working that produces a feeling of safety is more likely to be followed by less caution rather than more. If the operation works as intended, you are more confident, and confidence is a local reduction in the caution you were carrying, and that reduction is the real cost of the whole practice.

The sheet beside the door

The final component is a sheet kept where the working happens, and it is not a talisman. It is a record: the line, the date, the maintenance trigger, and the four columns. The reason it belongs beside the door rather than in a notebook is that the trigger is physical and the trigger fires at a door, and a record you have to find is a record that will not be there when the condition recurs.

Keep it boring. No symbols, no protection of the paper, no renewal rites. A sheet that has acquired a ritual of its own is a talisman, and a talisman gives the feeling of maintenance without the maintenance, which is the exact failure this sequence was designed to avoid. The whole apparatus is a declared boundary, a witness, a trigger, and four columns, and the four columns are the part that does the work.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a shield working and a banishing?

A banishing declares a limit and asks for removal, and it is complete when it has been said. A shield declares a limit and asks for it to hold, which requires a witness, a maintenance trigger, and a record. A banishing has no tail and a shield never finishes, which is why the same rite feels disappointing when someone reaches for it expecting a shield.

Why does a boundary have to be specific enough to fail?

Because a broad declaration has no failure condition. If you declare protection from all harm, then a bad afternoon is consistent with the declaration and no event can count as a breach. Naming a specific situation in a specific place for a stated duration gives you a test at the end of the duration, and the specificity is what makes the maintenance schedule possible.

What does the witness in a shield working do?

It provides something other than the practitioner performing the same act twice, which is what makes a declaration public within a system rather than a note. It can be an object in the room, a specified form of held attention, or a written entry with a time on it. The written version is the cheapest and the one most often skipped, on the mistaken grounds that intention is sufficient.

Why is the physical movement timed to the end of the sentence?

So that the boundary and the departure are a single action rather than a declaration followed by a movement that could have happened in the opposite order. In a banishing the movement is the physical component of removal. In a shield it marks the inside, since you end standing at the threshold having just declared what the threshold is, which is why a sequence that omits it produces a boundary with no location.

What can a shield working not protect you from?

From a situation you never declared, which is usually the one that was harder to name, and from conduct inside the boundary, since a limit on intrusions says nothing about what you do once inside. There is also a cost worth naming: if the working works, you are more confident, and confidence is a local reduction in the caution you were carrying, and that reduction is the real price of the practice.
