ASTRAN

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 8 min read

ASTRAN appears in a great deal of modern invocation material and in almost none of the historical sources. That single fact decides how the name should be handled, and handling it badly is the most common way this kind of working turns into something the practitioner cannot defend when somebody checks. So these notes begin with the provenance problem, then set the name aside entirely, then rebuild the practice from the structure that remains once it is gone. What survives is short, public, physical, and worth keeping. The name is a passenger, and the notes are written so that the vehicle still runs if it gets off at the next stop.

A Name That Is Not in Any Catalogue

ASTRAN does not appear in the Ars Goetia, in the lesser keys, in the Grimorium Verum, or in any of the demonological compilations that can be dated with confidence. It circulates through the same channels where a great many undocumented names circulate: video essays, comment threads, app catalogues, and lists assembled by people who found the name and assumed the catalogue would catch up with it.

That does not make the name useless. It makes it undocumented, and those two words carry different obligations. An invented name can be worked with as a psychological construct, a placeholder, or a piece of fresh vocabulary, and all three are legitimate provided you know which one you are doing. The failure mode is claiming a historical office for a term that has never had one, and then being surprised when the claim does not survive inspection.

What Was Actually Done, Stripped of the Name

The sessions described below used a name with no traceable provenance. Before anything was recorded, the practice was written out twice: once with the name in place and once with the name removed. The second version is the one worth keeping, because it strips away the only element that cannot be justified and leaves the structure that can.

What remained was a short sequence with four moving parts. A declaration of a boundary, stated in the plainest language available. A physical movement toward and through a door. A counted silence at a defined threshold. And a closing action that marks the end rather than the beginning. That is a workable frame. It asks for no name, no entity, and no lineage.

The Lesser Banishing Is Doing the Work

The Lesser Banishing of Ritual appears in the Golden Dawn's initiatory material and has been the default banishing in Western ceremonial practice since the nineteenth century. It is short, it is public, and it is worded so that a listener who had never heard of it would still understand the instruction. That last property is the important one, and it is also the reason no name is required.

Compare two sentences. I am protected, which is a statement about you. And this boundary is closed to anything that has not been invited, and I am standing on the far side of it, which is a declaration about the room. The second can be understood by anything present, hostile or otherwise, because it does not require an addressee. Petitions need a recipient. Declarations do not, and a declaration is what a banishing is.

Rising Toward the Door Is Not Symbolic

Many traditions specify a rising, meaning you physically move toward the door while banishing, and it is tempting to treat that as imagery. It is not imagery. The movement does three jobs at once: it puts measurable distance between you and the space, it gives the sequence a completion that cannot be faked while sitting down, and it hands the body an unambiguous signal that the session is over.

Across the ten sessions recorded here, the rising was the component people reported first and named least. Asked what had helped, the usual answer was something about standing up and walking out. Asked what had changed the room, the answer was a name. Those two responses are not measuring the same thing, and only one of them is carrying the procedure.

clearing, purifying and settling are separate operations

Contemporary writers in this field separate three operations that used to be run as a single lump. The first of those clears the space, the second purifies what occupies it, and the third puts your own footing back under you in a place you mean to stay in. People frequently need all three, and they are not interchangeable. A practice that is only a banishing leaves somebody feeling cleared but not settled, and a practice that is only a cleansing leaves them feeling purified and still unsteady.

The working recorded here ran all three in sequence on days when practice had been disturbing. Banish at the threshold, cleanse inside the room, ground at a fixed point. On days when nothing had happened, the banishing alone was used and the omission was deliberate, because running a full three-part sequence every evening turns a tool into a compulsion, and the compulsion is what starts manufacturing the anxiety the practice was meant to address.

The Ten Sessions

  1. Sessions one to three: threshold banishing only, no name spoken, no expectation recorded. The purpose was to establish that the frame works before anything was added to it.
  2. Sessions four to seven: the same sequence with the undocumented name spoken once at the opening. Nothing else changed, which is what made the comparison possible.
  3. Session eight: the name omitted again by choice, in order to check whether the earlier result had quietly depended on it.
  4. Sessions nine and ten: the full three-part sequence used after late practice, on two occasions only.
  5. Between sessions: one line in a diary with three columns, recording what happened rather than what it felt like.

Two Results Worth Reporting

The first result was behavioural and easy to verify. Requests to leave a room at the end of a session stopped occurring in the household where the practice was being run. That is a countable change, it involved other people who were not part of the practice, and it did not depend on anybody's interpretation of a name.

The second result was subjective and should be labelled as such. Practitioners reported that the room felt less watched. Nobody else confirmed this, no measurement was attempted, and it stays in the diary as an impression with no claim attached to it. Recording it in that form is not doubt about the practice. It is the reason the first result can still be believed a year later.

Why the Name Never Got Credit

The name went in last and it is the first thing that gets blamed. This is a predictable pattern. A named element is the most quotable part of any practice, so it is the part that gets cited, screenshotted, and carried into the next version of the working. The structure travels badly because it is invisible, and by the time the name has been passed along, the sequence it was embedded in has usually been dropped.

If the name is worth keeping, keep it inside a frame that would survive its removal. A practice whose only distinctive element is a single word will not survive a conversation with somebody who has never heard of it, and a practice that cannot survive that conversation is not a practice. It is a membership signal.

The Failure That Was Not a Failure

One session produced nothing whatsoever and was logged as a null. Checking the diary afterwards showed the cause was plain. The practitioner had run the sequence from bed, in the dark, without standing, and had said the declaration while looking at a ceiling. Every component that made the frame work was absent, and what survived was the version of the practice that functions as a comfort object.

That is the useful failure, because it shows the procedure is not indifferent to form, and form is cheap. A boundary declared while sitting down in the dark is a thought. The same boundary declared while walking out through a door is an act, and acts can be repeated on the days when conviction happens to be missing.

A Card for Daily Use

Write five lines and keep them where you work. The boundary, in plain language, addressed to anyone present. The movement, from where you are to the door and through it. The silence, counted rather than estimated. The closing action. And a note of what you intend to be doing in the room you are standing in, which is the part that makes the sequence an arrival rather than an exit.

The name, if you have one, goes on the card too, and it goes in a position where deleting it is easy. If the practice stops working the moment the name comes out, the name was carrying more than the frame was, and the frame needs rebuilding before any further sessions are logged.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ASTRAN a real spirit?

There is no evidence for it in any source that can be dated, which is a different claim from saying the name is not real in any sense. What can be said is that no historical office has ever been documented for it, so every statement about what it governs is a modern statement about a modern invention. If the name does useful work in your practice, keep it, and be accurate in your notes about where it came from.

How often should something like this be run?

Traditionally daily at a fixed threshold time, which remains defensible if you are barely doing any other practice. The version that survives contact with a busy life is to run it after practice that was disturbing, after a difficult conversation, or when a room genuinely needs clearing. Frequency chosen from the real calendar beats frequency chosen from an ideal one, because an unsustainable daily rule gets abandoned inside a month and a dropped rule teaches you nothing at all.

Does a declaration like this work against people too?

A boundary declaration does not care what it is for, which is worth remembering before setting one. The working recorded here was aimed at a felt atmosphere in a room, and it did change how the people in that house behaved at the end of the session. Point the same structure at a colleague and you have built something with a legitimate application and no ethical framework attached to it, and the second fact does not undo the first.

What should I do if nothing feels different afterwards?

Log it and change nothing else. The most common cause is that the frame was run from a chair rather than a doorway, and the correction for that is to stand and walk, not to intensify the wording. The second commonest cause is expecting a sensation rather than running a procedure, since a procedure executed properly can leave you feeling exactly as you did beforehand. That is a mismatch in what was being measured rather than a failure of the working.

Do I need something to burn, or can I simply speak?

Speaking is sufficient for a boundary declaration, and a great many people do better without fire, smoke, or a room arranged for effect. The components that repeatedly proved necessary were physical: standing, moving through a door, and a counted silence. If you want the classical form, the Lesser Banishing of Ritual uses incense, salt, water, and a short circuit of the four quarters, and that structure remains in general circulation whatever the exact wording.
