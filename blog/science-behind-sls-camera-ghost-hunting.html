The Science of SLS Cameras: How Machine Learning Detects Anomalies in Ghost Hunting

Skeptics and believers alike agree on one thing: if paranormal investigation is to be taken seriously, it needs rigorous methodology and verifiable technology. Enter the SLS camera â€” a tool that applies real computer vision science to the search for the unknown.

What Is SLS Technology?

SLS (Stick Lens System) technology originated in the gaming industry with Microsoft's Kinect sensor. The principle is straightforward: project an infrared grid into the environment, measure how the grid distorts against surfaces, and use that depth data to identify human-shaped forms.

What made SLS revolutionary for paranormal investigation was its ability to detect human forms independently of visible light conditions. In complete darkness, the infrared projection still produces a depth map, and the skeleton tracking algorithms still work.

Google ML Kit Pose Detection: The Engine Behind NOCTEM

Modern SLS camera apps like NOCTEM use Google Mobile Vision's ML Kit for pose detection. Here's how it works:

  1. Input: The camera feed is processed frame by frame.
  2. Detection: ML Kit's pose detection model analyzes each frame for human anatomical structures.
  3. Key Points: When detected, 33 skeletal key points are identified â€” from head and shoulders to fingertips and ankles.
  4. Overlay: These points are connected to form a stick figure skeleton overlay on the camera feed.

Technical Detail: ML Kit's pose detection model was trained on millions of images of humans in various poses, lighting conditions, and environments. It doesn't just detect full bodies â€” it can identify partial skeletons when only parts of a form are visible, which is particularly relevant in paranormal contexts where entities may be partially manifested.

Why This Matters for Paranormal Research

The key advantage of ML-based SLS detection over human observation is objectivity. A human investigator might miss a subtle form in the darkness, or might see something that isn't there (pareidolia). An ML model applies the same detection criteria consistently to every frame, eliminating subjective interpretation at the capture stage.

Limitations and How Responsible Investigators Address Them

No technology is perfect, and honest paranormal research requires acknowledging limitations:

  • False positives: Objects with human-like shapes can trigger detection. Mitigation: environmental control â€” clear the area of furniture and objects before SLS scanning.
  • Training bias: The ML model was trained on living humans, not paranormal entities. Mitigation: cross-reference SLS captures with EVP recordings and other sensor data.
  • Interpretation: The skeleton overlay is an interpretation, not direct evidence of an entity. Mitigation: always present raw camera footage alongside SLS overlay for context.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there any science behind SLS cameras for ghost hunting? â–¼

Yes. SLS cameras use machine learning models trained on thousands of human poses to detect skeletal structures. When the model identifies a human-like form in an environment where no visible person exists, it flags an anomaly. This is applied computer vision science.

How accurate is ML Kit skeleton tracking? â–¼

Google ML Kit pose detection identifies 33 skeletal key points with high accuracy in normal conditions. In paranormal contexts, it can detect partial skeletal patterns that the human eye would miss entirely.

Can SLS cameras be fooled by objects? â–¼

Like any ML model, SLS cameras can produce false positives. Chairs with certain shapes, mannequins, or even coat racks can trigger skeleton detection. Experienced investigators account for this through environmental control and cross-referencing.

Why use machine learning for paranormal investigation? â–¼

Machine learning excels at pattern recognition. An ML model trained to detect human skeletal structures can identify partial or subtle patterns that humans cannot see, especially in low-light conditions common in paranormal investigation.

What an SLS camera can contribute to a ghost-hunting session

Scanning laser sources, or SLS, are sometimes presented as a way to reveal a hidden presence by making dust, mist, or a beam appear. The technique can be visually striking, but an image is not evidence of a ghost by itself. A beam is affected by air movement, dust, temperature, vibration, and the camera settings. Before interpreting a shape, document the room, the equipment, the movement of people, and every change made to the image.

SLS is best treated as a structured observation tool. Use it to compare a quiet baseline with a controlled change, not to hunt for a sensational moment. A stable tripod, a locked exposure, a known focus distance, and a written protocol make the footage easier to review. If you move the camera or change the light, label that segment. The Eerie Roads app page can help organize a location log, but it cannot validate the cause of a visual effect.

A repeatable recording checklist

  1. Record a ten-second empty-room baseline before entering or touching anything.
  2. Write the camera model, lens, frame rate, shutter, ISO, focus distance, and light position.
  3. Make one controlled movement at a time: breathe nearby, open a door, or change the beam angle.
  4. Keep the original file and make a separate processed copy. List the changes exactly.
  5. Review the footage with the sound and notes before deciding what the image means.

Air movement is one of the easiest explanations to test. A slammed door, a heating vent, an automated curtain, and a walking observer can all change dust patterns. A moving shadow may come from an object outside the frame. Compare the image with a second camera or a conventional light when possible. A second view is a check on the scene, not a guarantee that a supernatural cause exists.

Reviewing the footage without self-deception

Write down what you can see before discussing what it might mean. A pale outline is an observation; “the outline of a person” is already an interpretation. Keep alternative explanations in the notes and ask another participant to review the unlabelled clip. If people consistently select different shapes, that is useful information about the limits of the image. If one person is frightened by the footage, stop or switch to a lower-intensity investigation. The goal is careful observation, not pressure to produce a result.

Frequently asked questions

Does SLS prove that a ghost is present?

No. SLS can make air movement and particles visible, but it cannot by itself identify the cause of a shape.

What should I record before the investigation?

Record a baseline, the room conditions, the exact equipment settings, and the order of every deliberate movement.

Should I edit the footage before sharing it?

You can create a clearly labeled processed copy, but preserve the original and disclose the changes.

When is a session too intense?

Stop when people become frightened, sleep is disrupted, privacy is compromised, or the investigation creates conflict or compulsive behavior.

A lab-style review sheet for SLS footage

Before labeling a clip interesting, create a small record that another person could follow. Write the camera model, lens, focus method, recording format, ambient light, room noise, temperature if relevant, and the exact time of the recording. Then describe what changed in the frame without naming a spirit: a light shifted, a reflection moved, a shadow entered, or nothing distinguishable from ordinary exposure variation occurred. This separation prevents the expectation from becoming evidence.

Use three passes

  1. First pass: watch once with sound off and note only observable changes.
  2. Second pass: add timestamps, compare the same view with a control recording, and record the settings that differed.
  3. Third pass: invite a second viewer to mark clips independently, then compare notes without prompting either person.

Keep the original files, make a working copy, and document edits. If a clip is shared publicly, remove names, addresses, device identifiers, and unrelated household details. A camera cannot establish that a paranormal event occurred; it can preserve a traceable record for further testing. If the activity involves a private home, ask everyone present for permission and stop if anyone feels unsafe.
