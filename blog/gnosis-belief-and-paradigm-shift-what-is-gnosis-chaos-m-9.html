Gnosis, Belief and Paradigm Shift

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 6 min read

Almost every argument about magic and belief is conducted as though the two were separate topics that occasionally meet. They are not separate, and the confusion is not academic: it is why people end up either abandoning a practice that was working for them, or defending claims that no evidence could support. This piece is a reference rather than a position. It takes the four claims that come up constantly, states what the evidence actually covers for each, and marks where the honest answer stops. It also separates gnosis from visualisation, because the two get collapsed constantly and the collapse produces a great deal of nonsense. If you want a table rather than an argument, this is the table. If you want a protocol for telling one of these apart from three other things that look like it, that is a different guide.

A Table Rather Than a Position

The useful form for this material is a table with four columns: the claim, what has been measured, what has not, and what a practitioner can reasonably do with the remainder. Positions on belief tend to collapse under contact with the first row. Tables survive.

The reason a position is a bad instrument here is that the honest answer to most claims is partial, and partial answers feel like refusals when you wanted a yes. Four columns let a partial answer sit next to the part that is solid, so the solid part can be used while the rest is held loosely.

That is also the difference between a practitioner and a dupe. The dupe needs the whole claim. The practitioner needs the part that survives contact with Tuesday.

The Parabola, Properly Attributed

One idea did most of the early work and is usually misquoted. The parabola is the proposal that a piece of magic has no independent existence and serves whatever end you point it at. It came out of the 1970s chaos current, and it is the most cited idea in the field and the least accurately stated.

Misstatements usually drop the second half. The claim is not that outcomes are unlimited, because they plainly are not. It is that the outcome depends on the frame you bring rather than on properties of the symbol. Taken literally it is unfalsifiable, and taken that way it is useless.

Taken as a practical instruction it is merely a description of self-deception, which everyone who does any of this already suspects. The instruction is to point it deliberately and check.

What Expectancy Effects Cover

Expectancy effects show real measured influence over reported pain and over a range of physiological outcomes. That is not a demystification of the placebo response. It is a description of it. The size of the effect varies by condition and outcome, and the honest summary is that perceived state changes genuine state, sometimes substantially and sometimes not at all.

What that does not extend to is curing disease, moving objects, or reaching outside the practitioner's own body and circumstances. The record is clear on the boundary. People who say the evidence supports mind-over-matter are usually reading a genuine effect on pain and inflating it into a claim about physics.

The distinction is worth holding carefully because both halves are true at once. Attention is doing real work on your experience. Attention is not doing work on the external world.

Confirmation Bias Is Not a Personal Failing

It is heavily documented and not a character defect. It is a property of how attention works, which is why it survives contact with people who know about it. Knowing the name of the bias does not immunise you against it. That is a common and costly error in self-directed practice.

It also has a specific shape in occult work, because the vocabulary is unusually accommodating. A method that asks you to pay attention will produce a coincidence, a dream, a strong feeling, or a synchrony, and the vocabulary of the practice supplies a ready name for all four. Names are cheap. The moment a name exists, the observation feels explained, and explained observations stop being evidence.

The defence is structural rather than moral. Decide in advance what would count as the method not working, because you will not be able to decide afterwards.

The Bayesian Version

You do not have to choose between sincere commitment and a record that is not flattering to you. Update as you go. Write down what you expect, write down what happened, and when they diverge, say which one moved and why.

This is compatible with taking the work seriously. Refusing to update is not strength, it is attachment to having been right, which is the thing practitioners are usually trying to escape. The people whose practice gets stranger tend to be the ones who never once recorded a failure.

The log is the mechanism. Belief without a log is a mood with vocabulary, and moods are not a technology.

Gnosis Against Visualisation

These get collapsed so often that the collapse is worth naming directly. Gnosis is receptive. You are watching what is already happening in yourself and in the room, and you are trying not to add anything. Visualisation is generative. You are producing content, and the production is the work.

Gnosis has a failure mode of self-absorption, because receptive attention given to your own state produces interesting state and nothing else. It needs a target that is not you, or it becomes meditation with an occult label.

Visualisation has a different failure mode, which is that it feels vivid and vividness is mistaken for contact. Vividness is a property of imagery, not evidence of reception. Keeping the two apart is most of what discipline means in this domain.

A Log That Would Survive Scrutiny

Six fields, one line per session: date, intention as stated, the action taken, the outcome as observed, what you predicted beforehand, and a rating from zero to one of how honest you were writing.

The last field is the unusual one, and the unusual one is what makes the rest of the record trustworthy. Nobody records how honest they were, so the log silently claims a reliability it does not have. Adding the field costs four seconds and turns a self-report into something closer to a measurement.

Review the log monthly, not daily. Daily review turns the record into a source of reassurance, which defeats the entire purpose.

The Line I Draw

Attention and expectation reliably change what I notice, what I do, and how I feel. They do not move money, cure illness, or alter the behaviour of other people. A method can change my behaviour in a way that indirectly affects a colleague, and that is the entire mechanism most honest workings rest on.

That position is narrower than the one I started with and I have stopped apologising for it. The narrower version is the one you can run for a decade. The wider one collapses the first time a prediction fails, and collapsed practices do not get resumed.

Holding a limited claim seriously is a skill. Holding a large one loosely is not possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I avoid fooling myself?

Write the prediction down before you start, and write down what would count as failure before you start too. The second one is the one that matters, because afterwards you can always find a reason. A monthly review of a six-field log with an honesty rating is more protection than any amount of care taken in the moment.

Gnosis and visualisation are not interchangeable. What separates them?

Gnosis is receptive attention on what is already occurring, and its risk is self-absorption. Visualisation is deliberate image construction, and its risk is mistaking vividness for reception. One watches and one produces, and the results get confused often enough that keeping them labelled separately is a real discipline.

Do you have to believe something can happen first?

You have to be able to state an intention and follow it through, which is a behavioural requirement rather than a metaphysical one. Many people run a serious practice while being entirely agnostic about mechanism. Others run one while holding much larger claims, and the log gets worse rather than better.

If the mechanism is just my own attention, is anything real happening?

Something is real, and it is not small. Attention changes what you notice, how you act, what you persist with, and how you interpret the result. That is enough to explain a great deal of what gets attributed to a symbol. If you want external confirmation, a structured tracker is the cheapest way to look for it and will at least tell you the shape of your own pattern.

Does paradigm shift mean I have changed reality?

Usually it means the frame you read the situation through has changed, which is a real and useful thing that leaves the situation alone. Where a frame change produces a difference in outcomes, the difference runs through what you did afterwards, not through the frame itself. The phrase is used loosely enough to support claims that do not survive a day of scrutiny.
