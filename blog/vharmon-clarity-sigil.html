VHARMON

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 27, 2026 ⬢ 7 min read

Clarity is the one target in this material that most practitioners have direct evidence about, because attentional control is among the most thoroughly studied cognitive faculties there is. That makes a clarity sigil a strange and useful object: the underlying capacity has excellent replication behind it, and the sigil is a commitment device aimed at methods that already work. This guide covers criterion conflict as the real cause of most decision paralysis, why writing an aim in the imperative forces a decision that more information will not, the logic behind using cards as a thinking surface rather than a predictive one, and the older contemplative discipline that the modern term gnosis borrows its name from. It also covers the difference between becoming clear and becoming convinced, which is where clarity practice most often goes wrong.

Attention as a trainable faculty

Attentional control improves with practice, and the improvement is measurable, replicable, and not fast. The methods that work are unglamorous: sustained attention on a single target, resisting the urge to switch, tolerating the discomfort of staying with something boring, and returning after interruption without drama.

This matters for clarity work because it inverts the usual expectation. A clarity sigil is not aimed at producing a state nobody can reach. It is aimed at a set of methods that reliably produce the state, and its function is to make you commit to the methods on the days when the methods are unpleasant. The gain comes from the practice, and the sigil is what gets you to it.

Criterion conflict, and why more data does not help

Decision paralysis is frequently misdiagnosed as an information deficit. It is usually criterion conflict, meaning you are holding two or more goals that cannot both be satisfied and have not made the trade-off explicit. In that state, more information makes things worse, because each new fact gets weighed against criteria that already contradict each other.

The cure is not more data. It is deciding which criterion wins. Write the two criteria side by side, in the plainest possible terms, and then write the sentence that resolves the conflict. That sentence, once written, is a much better input to a sigil than a description of the situation, because it has already done the hard thinking.

The imperative sentence that forces the choice

Drafting a clarity aim as an imperative is a diagnostic instrument as much as a spell. A request phrasing, something like I should decide about the job, leaves the question open. An imperative, I will send the decline today and start looking on Thursday, has already resolved what kind of decision this is.

If you cannot write the imperative, you have located the actual obstacle. Most unfound clarity problems turn out to be a missing criterion, an unstated constraint, or a decision you are not authorised to make. The drafting fails and tells you why, which is a better outcome than a charged sigil aimed at a problem you have not described.

Cards as a thinking surface, not an oracle

Projective techniques, from the Rorschach to the Thematic Apperception Test, were built to reach material that conscious self-report skips. A card with an ambiguous image provides a surface to project onto, and what comes back is often a theme you were already carrying without having articulated it.

That is the whole honest case for cards in this kind of practice. They are a generative surface, not a source of information. The distinction matters because treating the card as a source of information produces confident readings of nothing, while treating it as a surface produces a question you then answer yourself, which is occasionally useful and never supernatural.

Gnosis, and the discipline inside the word

Gnosis in this tradition means sustained, non-abstract contact with an experience, and it carries contemplative lineage considerably older than the modern movement. The word travelled from Gnostic theology through ceremonial practice into the chaos magic vocabulary, losing most of its religious content and keeping its technique.

The technique is worth keeping because it is a corrective to the abstractness that most self-improvement encourages. Gnosis is not a state you achieve by thinking about clarity. It is the moment of direct contact with a specific thing, unmediated by your description of it. Practised alongside a clarity working, it is the check on whether the clarity is real or merely verbal.

A clarity working, built in one sitting

Draft the resolved sentence in the imperative, with a date. I will finish the draft by Thursday and send it without rereading it three times. Reduce it to a glyph, charge it in a short fixed sequence, and then write the operational version. The operational version is the if-then structure that tells you what to do when the moment arrives.

One sitting, roughly forty minutes, is enough. Clarity work that requires several days of preparation is usually resistance wearing the costume of diligence. If the drafting is genuinely hard, that difficulty is information about the decision and it should be examined now, not deferred into a ritual that will let you avoid it for a week.

Desk, clock, and the fortnight you commit to

Two instruments, and no others. A clock, because clarity work involves sitting with an unresolved question rather than resolving it, and an open-ended sitting will sprawl. A notebook, for the written record that distinguishes gnosis from rumination, since the difference between the two is almost entirely whether anything got written down.

Fifteen minutes daily for a fortnight. The focus is one specific question, held for the entire sit, with the return counted rather than the depth judged. Depth is not a useful measure and it is the one most practitioners reach for, because it feels like progress and it is mostly a reflection of how tired you are.

Where clarity workings break

They break in one predictable place: the operational version gets written and then not followed. The charge produces a commitment, the fortnight requires an unpleasant action, and the action does not happen. Nothing about the method is at fault, and no additional ritual will fix a missing Tuesday.

The second break is subtler. A clarity working aimed at a decision you do not have the authority to make produces a fortnight of clarity about a situation you cannot change, which feels like progress and is not. If the operational version contains no action that is genuinely yours, the working is misaimed and should be redrafted around what you can actually decide.

A record you could hand to somebody else

The log has four columns: date, the question held, what came up, and whether the operational action happened. That is enough for somebody else to read and form a view, which is the standard worth holding yourself to. Records kept only for yourself drift toward whatever is convenient to remember.

The most informative column is the fourth one, and it is the one people leave blank. A fortnight of fifteen-minute sits with a complete fourth column is a real practice with real evidence behind it. A fortnight with blank fourth columns is a series of sessions, and the difference between those two things is the difference between a method and a mood.

What would change your mind about the method

Decide the stopping condition at the drafting stage, as you would for any other target. A workable one: a full fortnight with the operational action completed on most days, and no reduction in the number of times the question returns unbidden during ordinary work.

Clarity practice is unusually vulnerable to self-deception because the felt experience of a good sit is genuinely pleasant and easy to confuse with progress. A pre-committed stopping condition is the only defence. Without one, the practice continues indefinitely and reports success indefinitely, which is not a practice but a mood with a vocabulary.

Consent, when the target is your own attention

Self-directed workings avoid the consent problem that makes other targeting ethically fraught, and the avoidance is real rather than technical. You are the only party whose judgement is being affected, you consented to it by drawing the sigil, and nobody outside the working is exposed.

Two limits are worth setting anyway. Not every question is a good candidate, and a working aimed at suppressing a feeling you should be having tends to produce a rebound rather than a resolution. If the clarity you want is clarity about something uncomfortable, keep the discomfort in the question. Clarity that removes the uncomfortable part is not clarity, and it tends not to hold.

The distance between clear and convinced

Clarity is a property of your thinking. Conviction is a property of your relation to a conclusion. They come apart regularly, and the disjunction is where clarity practice gets turned into a device for confirming what you had already decided.

Notice it when it happens: the moment the working starts generating reasons rather than observations. A clarity practice that produces three good arguments for the position you already held is not working. One that produces a genuine reordering of your criteria, including against your preference, is. Keep the second one and be suspicious of the first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a sigil make me clear-headed?

It can make you commit to the methods that improve clarity, which is a different and more modest claim. Attentional control is trainable and the training is boring: sustained attention, tolerating dullness, returning after interruption. A sigil is a commitment device for that training, and the benefit accrues from the practice rather than from the glyph.

What is criterion conflict and how do I get past it?

It is having two goals that cannot both be satisfied, without having decided which one wins. You cannot think your way out of it, because more information gets weighed against criteria that already contradict each other. You decide your way out of it, by writing both criteria down and then writing the sentence that resolves the trade-off.

Are cards useful for clarity if they predict nothing?

As a generative surface, yes. A card with an ambiguous image gives you something to project onto, which surfaces material that deliberate thought tends to skip. The output is a question you answer, not information you receive. Treating the card as predictive produces confident readings of nothing, which is worse than not drawing it.

How do I know a clarity working is actually working?

By the fourth column of your log, not by how the sittings felt. Over a fortnight, ask whether the specific question returns unbidden during ordinary work less often than it did at the start, and whether you completed the operational action on most days. Felt clarity during a sit is pleasant and is not evidence of anything lasting.

Should a clarity sigil target a decision I cannot make?

No. If the operational version contains no action that is genuinely yours, the working will produce clarity about a situation you have no authority over, which feels like progress and changes nothing. Redraft around the part you can decide. Sometimes the honest version of a clarity working is about accepting that a decision belongs to somebody else, which is a clearer statement than working on the decision itself.
