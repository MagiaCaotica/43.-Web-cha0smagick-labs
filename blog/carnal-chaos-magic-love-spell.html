CARNAL

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 27, 2026 ⬢ 10 min read

A reference sheet is not a spellbook. It is a set of definitions that stop you from using words in the wrong order, and love practice needs one more than any other area of the tradition because the vocabulary here is unusually slippery. Attraction and reconciliation sound like two intensities of the same thing and are not; a bound person and an unbound one are different grammatical problems; and the single most consequential distinction in the whole field is one most people never learn to state plainly. This page builds that reference from the ground up. It starts with the words themselves, moves to the four questions any love aim has to survive before it is charged, and finishes with the honest reason a reference is more useful than a promise: it constrains what you can aim at, and most of what people want to aim at should not be aimed at.

The vocabulary, and why the words are not interchangeable

Tradition, love, and the modern chaos magic vocabulary are all in circulation and they do not describe the same operations. An older formulation treats a working as an appeal to a power with a certain jurisdiction over a certain kind of person in a certain kind of situation. A modern formulation treats it as a compressed statement of intent, charged and released, with the practitioner as the operative ingredient. Both are usable, but mixing them produces aiming that is vaguer than either would produce alone, because the older form supplies an imagined listener who can be blamed and the modern form supplies none.

The practical consequence is that you should decide which grammar you are writing in and stay there for the length of a working. Write a modern imperative statement and charge it as though your own attention were the whole mechanism. Write an older petition and understand that you are asking, not commanding, and that a request can be declined. Most disappointing results in this field are grammar failures rather than failures of technique.

Attraction, reconciliation, and the two grammars

Reconciliation starts from an existing bond. That single fact changes everything downstream: there is already a history, already a mutual investment, already a channel that carried something in both directions. An attraction working has to create a channel that does not exist, which is a harder thing to describe and a much harder thing to be honest about, because there is nothing in the other person's history that points toward you.

This is why reconciliation is generally considered the lower-risk operation and why that reasoning holds up. You are not introducing something foreign into a closed system, you are addressing a relationship that already has a shape. The ethical weight shifts too: a reconciliation working is a statement about two people, while an attraction working is a statement about one person's desire that happens to be aimed at another. Keeping the two grammars distinct in your notes is the first practical protection against the category error that most of the literature on this subject is actually about.

The four questions a love aim has to survive

Before you reduce anything to a glyph, put the aim through four questions. Can you state it as a single imperative sentence in under fifteen words? Is there a named person in it, or have you smuggled one in through a pronoun? If there is a named person, have they said yes, in a form you could show somebody? And finally, what observable difference in the world would count as the aim having worked, described without reference to the other person's feelings? If you cannot answer the last one, you do not have an aim, you have a mood.

These four questions are not mystical and they are not a substitute for judgement. Their function is to move the problem from the level where intention feels pure to the level where it can be examined. Almost everyone fails the second question, because the pronoun is how desire stays unnamed, and an unnamed target is precisely what lets the ritual be aimed at several people at once while feeling specific.

Consent as the first entry in the reference

The objection that has survived longest in this field is about asymmetry. A sigil aimed at a specific individual overrides that person's stated preferences, and there is no version of the practice in which the override is symmetric: the person being worked on did not choose to be worked on. The chaos magic position is usually stated as the practitioner's will being the operative ingredient, and that position is precisely what makes targeting an individual ethically fraught, because it removes any external authority that could say no on the target's behalf.

The part worth dwelling on is that the objection does not require you to reject the metaphysics. It is a procedural objection and it can be satisfied by design rather than by belief. Write the consent condition into the charge. State in the aim that it is conditional on the other person participating freely, and record what freely would have to look like in practice. Workings that include such a clause are rare, which is itself information about the state of the tradition.

What the classical position actually claims

Older sources in this area were not writing about manipulation, whatever the popular framing says. A petition aimed at love in a grimoire of the period is a request directed upward, usually to a named power operating under a name associated with love, asking for a change in a particular person's disposition. The requester is in a subordinate position in that structure, the power is not obliged to comply, and the person who receives the benefit is usually not the person who made the request.

Read that way, the form is not a violation of consent at all; it is a supplication on someone's behalf, which is a very old and generally accepted thing to do and which people do without any ritual apparatus. The reason this matters is that a genuine break happened here. When the form was retained and the position of the addressee was dropped, a request to a power became an instruction to a person, and the ethics of the two are not comparable.

Reconciling a bond that already existed

If you are building a reconciliation reference, the useful columns are not about technique. They are about history and about asymmetry of fault. A bond that ended for a reason will end for that reason unless the reason is addressed, and a ritual applied over an unexamined cause produces a temporary resumption followed by a worse ending. This is the most common failure in the reconciliation field and it is not a failure of the working; it is a working applied to the wrong problem.

The second column is whether the reconciliation would require the other person to be told something they do not currently know. If it would, the working is not a reconciliation but an intrusion, and no amount of narrowing the aim will change that. An honest reconciliation reference has a line in it that rules this out, written before the charge rather than discovered afterwards.

The escalation pattern, described rather than endorsed

Occult sources from several traditions describe repeated love working against the same target as cascading: the first working asks for a small change, the second asks for a larger one, the third assumes the previous one succeeded, and by the fourth the aim no longer resembles the first. The description is worth taking seriously as a description of a pattern that occurs in ordinary human behaviour and has nothing to do with whether the workings did anything.

The mechanism is easy to state. Each round produces some signal, the signal is ambiguous, and ambiguity gets resolved in the direction that permits more effort. What breaks the pattern is not discipline of will but a written stop condition: a specific number of charges, a specific date, and a written statement of what you will do if nothing has changed. Cascading is very difficult to detect from inside, because each individual step looks reasonable.

Building a reference sheet you will keep

One page, four headings, and a rule that the page stays blank until you have something to put in it. Under definitions, write the two grammars and one sentence each. Under constraints, the four questions from earlier in one line apiece. Under the consent clause, the actual wording you will use and the reason it is there. Under failure modes, the specific ways you have seen this go wrong, written in your own language, because a generic list will not stop you and a specific one might.

The page should be boring. Its value is that it is available at the moment of decision, which is the only moment it can do any work, and a beautifully formatted reference that lives in a notebook you read once has no availability at all. If you keep it in one place and keep it to a single sheet, the odds are that you will actually consult it before the third charge, which is where the discipline is usually lost.

A working drawn from the reference, in outline

The outline fits on a card. Write the aim in one imperative sentence with no pronoun standing in for a person. Add the consent condition as a second sentence rather than a footnote, because its position in the construction matters more than its wording. Reduce to a glyph, charge it while doing something physically uninteresting that you will do again tomorrow, and record the date, the number of charges, and the stop condition you set before you started.

Then do nothing for a fortnight. The most common error in this field is a second charge scheduled before the first has had time to produce either an effect or nothing, and a fortnight is the shortest interval at which the absence of an effect becomes informative rather than premature. Record the outcome in a column headed with what you would have done anyway, which is the only control you have against reading a normal week as a result.

Where the reference is most likely to mislead you

A reference on this subject tends to overstate specificity. Writing a person's name, their birthday, and a description of their situation gives the working a feeling of precision without supplying any additional mechanism, and the additional detail mostly serves to make the aim harder to fail, which is not the same as making it more likely to succeed. A well-built aim is short, and the shortness is what allows you to check it.

The second way it misleads is by implying that the working is the variable. In the cases where something changes, the change is almost always in the practitioner's behaviour, availability, or willingness to be in a particular room at a particular time, and the sigil is the thing that made the change obligatory rather than optional. Keeping that in the foreground is not deflation; it is the only reading of the evidence that has survived contact with a record.

Reading a month against the sheet

Lay the sheet next to the log and read them together rather than separately, which is the only way the sheet does any work. Three things become visible. Whether the consent clause was actually written into the charge, or whether it exists only on the page, which is the single most common failure in a well-intentioned practice. Whether the stop condition was honoured. And whether the aim drifted, which almost always shows up as the noun getting vaguer over successive charges until the final one could be satisfied by almost anything.

The drift is the finding worth having, because it is a description of your own wanting rather than of the ritual's power. If the fourth charge asks for something the first charge would not have asked for, the working has functioned as a device for moving your own position, which is a real effect and one you can use deliberately in other areas. It is simply not the effect the aim described.

The aim you should hand back

There is a version of this practice that survives its own scrutiny, and it does not involve a named person you have not asked. It involves a condition rather than an individual: clarity about your own preference, availability for a relationship that already exists, the capacity to leave a situation that is not working, a specific and achievable change in your own conduct. These can all be written as imperatives, all can be charged, and all can be checked against a column headed with what you would have done anyway.

The reason to prefer them is not squeamishness. It is that the aims which survive scrutiny are the ones you can write down in a form you would not mind being read aloud, and the aims which do not survive are the ones whose only advantage was that nobody else would see them. That is a practical test, and it can be applied in under a minute, before the charge rather than after.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main ethical objection to love magic?

It is the asymmetry of consent. A sigil aimed at a specific individual overrides that person's stated preferences, and the chaos magic position that the practitioner's will is the operative ingredient removes any external authority able to refuse on the target's behalf. The objection is procedural rather than metaphysical, so it can be satisfied by building consent conditions into the construction.

Why is reconciliation magic considered lower risk than attraction magic?

Because a bond already existed in the reconciliation case. There is already a history, a mutual investment and a channel, so the working addresses a relationship that has a shape rather than attempting to create a channel that does not exist. This also means the ethical weight differs, since a reconciliation statement concerns two people while an attraction statement concerns one person's desire aimed at another.

What does cascading mean in love magic?

It is the pattern in which repeated working against the same target escalates: the first aim asks for a small change, later ones assume success and ask for more, until the final aim no longer resembles the first. The description is worth respecting as an observation about real behaviour, since each individual step looks reasonable from inside. Written stop conditions are what interrupt it.

How specific should a love sigil be?

One imperative sentence of under fifteen words, with any named person stated directly rather than smuggled in through a pronoun, and an observable difference in the world described without reference to the other person's feelings. Adding biographical detail produces a feeling of precision rather than any additional mechanism, and it makes the aim harder to check for failure.

How long should you wait between charges?

About a fortnight before the second charge. The commonest error in this field is scheduling another charge before the first has had time to produce either an effect or nothing, and a fortnight is the shortest interval at which an absence becomes informative rather than premature. Record what you would have done anyway, as the only available control.
