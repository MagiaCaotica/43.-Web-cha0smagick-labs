Occult Culture and Modern Practice

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 11 min read

Modern occult practice no longer arrives through initiatory doors. The route most people take runs through search results, forum threads, short videos and playlists, and that change has consequences that are easy to feel and hard to name. This guide looks at the current scene as a structure rather than a scene: how discovery works now, what the older initiatory model was carrying that the search model cannot, which traditions survive intact outside the Northern European reconstruction, and how the folk and industrial lines have always run alongside the formal orders. It is written for someone who has been in this material long enough to have noticed that almost nobody is doing the same thing, and who wants to understand why. You will not come out of it with a lineage. You will come out of it with a better map of the ground you are already standing on.

The audience is online, and that changes the entry route

The overwhelming majority of people arriving at this material now do so through a screen, and the discovery model that follows is search and forum rather than introduction. Someone wants to know about sigils, types a phrase, and is handed a thousand videos ranked by whatever the ranking rewards. Within two clicks they have more material than a traditional novice would have accumulated in two years.

That is a real gain and it comes with a specific cost. Traditional introduction was slow because it was filtered: a teacher decided what a student was ready for and withheld the rest. Online discovery is fast because it is unfiltered, which means the order in which you meet things is decided by ranking algorithms rather than by anything about your readiness. Most people therefore meet the dramatic material first, because drama is what ranks.

The practical consequence is that almost every beginner's first impression of this field is a caricature of it. The esoteric content exists, and it is good, but it is competing for the same attention as the content that makes a thirty-second clip. Rebuilding the order deliberately is therefore not a refinement. It is the actual work of the first year.

What the loss of initiatory routes removed

Something real was lost when initiatory structures stopped being the normal way in, and it is worth being precise about what. A teacher in a lineage carries context that no book carries. They know which parts of a practice are load-bearing and which are decorative, because they have watched people get hurt by the wrong ones. They know what a student can hold and what they will drop. That knowledge is transmitted by contact, and it is not recoverable from a transcript.

The second thing lost is correction. In a lineage, somebody is present who will tell you that what you are doing has gone wrong, and that they are not being rude. Online, the only correction available is a comment section, which reliably produces defensiveness instead. Most practitioners in this field have therefore had no one to tell them when the practice had become fixation, which is a gap that the more serious failure modes fall straight into.

The third loss is harder to name and easier to feel. An initiatory structure supplies an answer to the question of who decides whether you are progressing. Its absence leaves a vacuum, and the vacuum is filled by intensity. When nobody can tell you whether you have learned anything, the only available signal is how much you are doing, and that is the measure that leads people into the escalation failure we will come to later.

What the loss also released

The same shift freed things that the older structure suppressed. Access to primary texts is now effectively universal, and a person in a city with no local order can read Crowley, Carroll, Spare and the grimoires in an afternoon for the price of a coffee. A person with no religious background can build a serious practice without converting to anything, which was a real barrier and is now gone.

It also released people from performing belief they do not hold. For a long time, participating seriously in any of this required a degree of credulity that functioned as a social filter. The current scene is largely built by people who take the methods seriously and decline the metaphysics, and that combination turns out to be a productive one rather than a compromised version of the real thing.

The cost is the mirror image of the benefit. Anything the initiatory model filtered out is now unfiltered too, and a great deal of it never had a filter behind it. Both descriptions are true at once. The field is more accessible and less sorted than it was, and sorting it is now the practitioner's job rather than someone else's.

Reconstruction, and the traditions it never touched

A large part of what circulates as witchcraft is a twentieth-century Northern European reconstruction, assembled from surviving fragments of older practice and from the folklore sources of one region. The scholarship on how that reconstruction happened is still genuinely contested, and the honest position is that we do not know the proportions.

What matters for a practitioner is that this leaves a great deal untouched. Traditions in Peru, Mexico, Brazil and the Caribbean differ substantially from the Northern European version, and many of them preserve plant knowledge and family practice that never passed through Wicca or through Gardner at all. These are living practices carried by living families and communities, not reconstructions of anything.

The practical lesson is about method rather than borrowing. A living tradition with continuous transmission has information that a reconstructed one structurally cannot have, and that information tends to be practical rather than symbolic. If you want to understand working with plants, or with the material of a household, the sources that have the most to say are the ones that were never interrupted.

The punk and industrial line, running since the late seventies

Alongside the initiatory structures and the reconstructions there has been a continuously running line of do-it-yourself practice rooted in punk, industrial music and the margins of the subculture, running essentially unbroken since the late 1970s. This line has always been where people work with material nobody has approved, and it has produced a substantial body of practice that formal orders have never touched.

What distinguishes the line is a low tolerance for authority and a high tolerance for experimentation. A ritual gets changed because it stopped working, not because a committee approved a revision. That is chaos magic as a temperament rather than as a doctrine, and it has been a reliable engine for finding out what actually does something.

It is also a line that has produced the field's ethics debates, since its tolerance for experimentation occasionally outruns its attention to other people. A practice that treats consent as a topic to be worked through rather than a boundary to be respected is a practice that will eventually cause harm, and the harm tends to land on somebody who was not consulted. Anyone drawn to this line should build the consent habit early.

Shared symbols, and how they actually spread

Meme-based practices function as genuinely shared symbolic objects, and their spread is a live and rather clean case study in how a symbol becomes collectively real. A figure like the KEK archetype has no ancient provenance and no fixed meaning, and it is nonetheless recognised, joked about and deployed by people who have never met one another.

The mechanism is not mysterious and it is not magical in the narrow sense. A symbol repeated across enough contexts acquires meaning through social reinforcement, in exactly the way a word acquires meaning, and the resulting object is genuinely shared without anybody having intended to create it. Several researchers have looked at this seriously and the pattern holds up as far as they have taken it.

There is a practical lesson for anyone building a practice. Shared symbols carry more charge than invented ones because they have a population behind them, and you can use that deliberately. A sigil built from a motif that a large number of people already hold in mind will be doing more work than one built from a private neologism, and you will be able to say honestly what you are borrowing and from whom.

Being public, and what obscurity used to buy

The older advice was to keep your practice private, and the reasons were not all mystical. Some were legal, since a public claim can attract attention from people with an interest in your money or your housing. Some were social, since a public commitment in this field invites a specific kind of unsolicited intervention. And some were about the work itself, on the argument that secrecy focuses attention inward.

Obscurity no longer buys what it used to. Publishing a grimoire in the seventeenth century guaranteed a hostile reception from the institutions with power over you. Posting a ritual online in the present guarantees a comment section. The risk profile is different in kind rather than merely lower, and the old precaution does not transfer.

The reasonable position now is to be ordinary in public and specific in private. Nobody needs a costume, and nobody gains anything from declaring a tradition in a bio. What is worth keeping private is the material that names other people, which is a boundary question rather than a secrecy question, and it deserves the same attention whatever you post.

The teacher question, asked properly

Should you follow a single teacher is the wrong shape of the question, and it produces the wrong answer. A teacher is useful for a defined skill. Somebody who can show you how to structure a working, or how to read a source critically, or how to close a cycle properly, is saving you months. That is a short relationship with a clear end, and it is the kind that works.

The structural risk is different. A single figure as a destination, for years, with your practice organised around their approval, is the most common way people in this field end up stuck. The approval is what you are working toward, the work is the reason you are there, and when the figure disappoints or disappears there is no structure left holding the practice up. It is a much more common failure than a bad teacher, which tells you something about where to put the attention.

A workable arrangement is to take technique from anyone competent and take direction from nobody indefinitely. Read across several traditions rather than deeply within one. Keep the practice your own, and hold the specific claims you make with enough scepticism that you could be wrong about them without your life collapsing.

Two texts, and one history book

If you want the shortest route through this material, it is short. Liber Null and Liber Kaos give you the method and the attitude, and between them they contain most of what the modern movement actually contributed. They are short, they are inexpensive, and they can be read in an evening.

Add one book of history. Almost any serious history of Western esotericism will do, and reading one properly will do more for your judgement than another twenty practical manuals, because most of the errors in the practical material come from a distorted view of where the material came from. A practitioner who knows that the goetic text is a seventeenth-century compilation handles it very differently from one who has absorbed the viral version.

Then read the sources themselves in the original where you can. The commentary is where the confident additions accumulate, and a few hours with a primary text will make you notice how much of the surrounding material is unsupported. This is not elitism. It is the difference between doing the work and merely describing it in detail.

A year of deliberate exposure to more than one tradition

The most reliable thing you can do in the first year is arrange your reading so that you are not learning from one voice. Pick a formal initiatory source, a reconstruction, a folk tradition from outside Northern Europe, and one practitioner of the punk and industrial line, and read all four. They will disagree about almost everything, which is the point.

The disagreements are diagnostic. Where the sources concur, you have probably found a human universal, something about ritual, attention or community that holds regardless of framing. Where they diverge, you have found a local decision, a choice a particular group made, which tells you something about what that group needed. Neither is more authentic. They are answering different questions.

By the end of a year of that, you stop needing anyone to tell you which one is correct, because you have stopped asking the question. You will have a practice assembled from parts you can name, and you will be able to say what each part is for. That is a much stronger position than certainty, and it is one the initiatory model used to supply automatically and now has to be constructed deliberately.

The failure mode specific to this scene

There is one failure that belongs to the online scene rather than to magic as such, and it is worth naming because it is invisible from inside. A person with no training meets a great deal of confident material, absorbs a set of claims they cannot evaluate, and then finds that the claims have organised their understanding of the world. The structure is not the practice. The practice is a small set of actions. What has been built on top of it is an interpretive framework, and it has no contact with evidence.

The tell is not believing in magic. It is inability to say what would count as a failure. A person with a real practice can describe the request, the deadline, the record and the result, including the result being nothing. A person with a framework cannot, because the framework has no way to lose, and a position that cannot lose has stopped being a claim.

The defence is the record, and it is a very cheap one. Keep it, and the framework stays honest. Let it go, and within a few years you will be defending a set of statements about reality that you assembled out of search results, and you will not remember which parts you checked. That is the failure this scene is actually good at producing, and it has almost nothing to do with magic.

Frequently Asked Questions

What books are worth reading first?

Liber Null and Liber Kaos between them cover the method and the attitude, and a serious history of Western esotericism will give you the context that most practical books get wrong. Three books is enough to start. Resist the urge to assemble a reading list of forty before doing anything, because the list will expand to fill the space and the practice will not begin.

Is it ok to be public about it?

Yes, and the older advice to stay hidden no longer buys what it used to. The realistic risks now are targeted harassment and financial approaches rather than institutional attention. What is worth keeping private is any material that names other people, which is a boundary question rather than a secrecy question and deserves the same care regardless of what you post.

Should I follow a single teacher?

For a defined skill, yes, and the arrangement should have an end. The structural risk is not a bad teacher but a single figure as a long-term destination, because then your progress depends on their approval and there is nothing underneath when that stops. Take technique widely, take direction from nobody indefinitely, and keep the practice your own.

Is there a paid course or membership that would get me further than free material?

Free material is genuinely extensive and the method costs nothing to learn. What money buys is structure: someone else's sequencing, feedback on your specific log, and a deadline you did not set. That is worth paying for if you have tried self-directed work and stalled, and worth skipping if your problem is that you have not yet started.

How do I avoid drifting into something I cannot stop doing?

Fix the schedule before you fix the intensity, and keep it small enough that a bad week does not break it. Write the record daily including the empty days, because that is what keeps a practice in proportion. And decide in advance what the practice is for, since a routine that has quietly become the centre of your life is the failure everyone recognises afterwards and nobody prevents in advance.
