Start With Where the Name Came From

METATRÓN appears in material that borrows the visual language of the seventy-two without inheriting their structure. That combination produces a name with a strong impression and a thin record, and working with it without noticing the difference is how people end up convinced the name itself is the problem.

So the first task is provenance, and it is unglamorous. Find the earliest text you can that uses the name for the purpose you have in mind. If that text is a nineteenth-century grimoire, note the page. If it is a forum post from 2011, note that too, because knowing which one you are working from changes what you can reasonably expect.

A name with clean provenance gives you an office. A name without one gives you an atmosphere. Both can be worked with, but they need different requests, and mixing them up produces a session that is coherent in feeling and empty in structure.

What the Seventy-Two Offer That Freeform Names Do Not

Across three ranks the Ars Goetia presents seventy-two spirits: nine Presidents, thirty-six Dukes, thirty Kings, and beneath those, families of two, three or four. Each entry carries a rank, a visible form, an office and named powers. That is a taxonomy, and a taxonomy is worth more than a mood.

The reason is lookup. When you are stuck in a situation you cannot name, a list of seventy-two specific offices turns a vague feeling into a question you can actually put to paper. Nineteen spirits of libraries is a different intervention from one spirit of lost property, and the catalogue is what lets you tell the difference.

A name invented on the spot has no such advantage. It carries whatever you brought to it, which means it will confirm whatever you already believed. The seventy-two are useful partly because they were not designed by you and partly because they constrain you.

The Reputation Problem in One Paragraph

The modern Goetia as a brand, the video essays, the apps, the commentary layered on top, is a twenty-first century phenomenon sitting on older material. That older material runs back through Jewish and Christian demonological writing, the Book of Enoch among them, and arrives by way of the seventeenth-century Conjuratio Ben-Maimon and the Lemuria.

What the modern layer did was strip the context. In the classical scheme these were administrative or disciplinary officials, figures that enforce a rule, and the shift to a cast of menacing supernaturals changed what people think they are being asked to deal with. METATRÓN, sitting in that stream, inherits the inflated reputation without a matching catalogue entry, which is the worst of both positions.

The practical consequence is that you should assume a stranger's description of this material is wrong about the office and right about the tone. Work from the tone, ignore the office.

Building a Working From Four Lines

Line two is the one that changes everything. Writing the source under the office is a small discipline with a large effect: it converts an atmosphere into a citation, and it makes you aware the moment you are working from something you made up. Line four is what gives you something to be wrong about.

  1. The name, spelled as you will say it.
  2. The office, in one sentence, from the best source you have, with the source named underneath.
  3. The request, in the first person and the present tense, phrased as a request.
  4. The timeframe, a date or a number of days, and the condition under which you would call it answered.

The Sequence on Paper

Step five resists people the most. Four seconds, one name, and it feels insufficient for a name with that much atmosphere behind it. The sufficiency is not a matter of volume. What makes the naming distinct from ordinary speech is the setting, the single utterance, and the fact that the rest of the session is built around that one instant.

  1. Clear the surface. Wipe it. Put down a dark cloth or a clean sheet.
  2. Write the date and the hour at the top before anything else.
  3. Mark a boundary with chalk or salt so the working has an edge.
  4. Copy the four lines onto the sheet in your own handwriting.
  5. Say the name once, out loud. No titles, no repetitions, no second attempt.
  6. State the request in exactly the words you wrote, then do something with your hands for a few minutes.
  7. Repeat the request in the same words, unchanged.
  8. Close by saying the working is closed, then snuff the light if you lit one.
  9. Read the sheet back before putting it away.

Landing Versus Feeling Good

These are different and only one of them is information. A working that lands produces the specific thing you wrote in line four, inside the timeframe you wrote there. A working that merely felt good produces a warm hour, a sense of having done something, and a stronger version of the belief you started with.

The warm hour is not worthless, but it is not evidence, and it is the more seductive of the two because it is available on demand. Keep the check written on the sheet, and read the sheet rather than the memory.

The test that catches most self-deception is timing. If a result shows up three weeks later with no relation to the timeframe you stated, you are reading backwards, and the honest description is that nothing happened inside the experiment you set up.

Three Trials and an Honest Stop

Before drawing any conclusion, give it three honest tries with the office line repaired and a deadline attached. Change one variable each time, so the log gives you a comparison rather than three identical evenings.

If all three come back with nothing you can point to, the framework is not the problem. The request, the hour, or the source line is. Change the question and run it again, which is cheaper and more useful than adding apparatus.

The stop condition matters as much as the trial count. A named point at which you will end an approach and write it up as ended is what keeps a practice from becoming a slow argument you are losing.

The Kit, the Hour and the Log

Pen, paper, chalk or salt, one light you can snuff, and an hour nobody interrupts. The fourth item is the one people try to substitute with equipment, and it cannot be substituted.

Material contributes almost nothing here, which is worth stating plainly for anyone who assumed otherwise. What marks the surface matters as a boundary, and the boundary matters because it makes the hour distinguishable from every other hour you spent at the same table.

The log is the third element and the one most often skipped. Date, hour, office line, request in five words, timeframe, and what you observed. That last column is the one that will be uncomfortable to fill in some weeks, and it is the only one that makes the other five worth having.

Keeping It Alive Without Going Stale

A first and second and third working on one office forms a sequence; a fourth is normally just noise. Past that point the repetition stops producing information and starts producing a feeling of effort, which is easy to mistake for progress.

The way to keep it productive is to change one thing each time and keep the frame fixed. Change the hour, or the phrasing, or the timeframe. Hold the rest steady so that a run of sessions produces something you can actually compare against itself.

And rotate out. A name you have worked forty times is a habit, not a tool. Put it down for a season and pick up something with a different office, and the material stays alive because the approach is still being tested rather than merely repeated.

Ethics and the Limits of the Frame

Nothing in the documented history of this catalogue points to any hazard beyond the ordinary. The real hazards are ordinary ones: a name used without ever reading its source, and a request aimed at another person's decisions rather than your own situation.

The second deserves stating without euphemism. A working aimed at somebody else overriding their choices is a different act from a working aimed at your own circumstances, it is not made acceptable by using a religious vocabulary, and it does not belong in the same category as the rest of this material.

The third is honesty about what you have. An approach that requires continued belief in order to produce results is not a technique, and the log is the only thing that tells you which kind you have. Write the failures down, let a poor result end the working, and the practice stays something you can rely on rather than something you have to be convinced of.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which spirit gives money?

Within the Ars Goetia, Mammon is the spirit of wealth, Clauneck a duke credited with riches, and Paimon the giver of dignities and confirmations of favour. Those are three different requests and none substitutes for another. Whatever name you settle on, read its stated office in the source text before you work with it, and state the timeframe out loud so you have something to be wrong about.

Who are the nine Presidents?

Nine Presidents head the list, Bael first and then Agares, Vassago, Samigina and Marbas, followed by Valefor, Aamon, Belphegor and Saleos, every one carrying a documented rank, appearance and area of power. Presidents are the broadest of the three ranks, so they fit questions about a whole area of life rather than a single task. The office line decides the outcome far more often than the rank does, so read that before you light anything.

Do the Goetia entries count as demons in any real sense?

Historically they are rows in a demonological catalogue, with nothing behind them that could act. Practically they are a rich and usable symbolic vocabulary, seventy-two named offices covering ordinary situations, which is more than most frameworks of personal symbolism offer. Read the office, keep the check honest, and the material in this catalogue is bookkeeping with an unusual set of names attached.

Is it a problem that METATRÓN has unclear provenance?

It is a problem you can work with, provided you treat it as a fact rather than a surprise. A name with thin documentation gives you an atmosphere and no office, so the request has to be built from the atmosphere rather than from a citation, and the check has to be stricter because there is less to hold you to. Write the source under the office line even when the source is weak, because that is what makes the weakness visible.

How often should I repeat a working on the same name?

Three times with a stated check, then reassess. A fourth attempt on the same office usually produces a feeling of having worked rather than a result, and repeating further only reinforces whatever you already believed. If the three were honest and nothing arrived, change the request rather than the method, and let a name rest for a season before you return to it.
