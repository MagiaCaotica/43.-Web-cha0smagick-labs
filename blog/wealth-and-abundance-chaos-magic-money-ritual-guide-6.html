Wealth and Abundance

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 12 min read

Money is the most common subject in modern occult practice, and the material written about it ranges from plainly sensible to openly fraudulent with very little in between. That is not a criticism of any one author, it is a property of a genre where a real technique and a nonsense claim can be sold in the same paragraph. This piece takes the genre apart. It names the four claims that do almost all the work, checks each one against what is actually established, and separates the part of the method that operates whether or not anything supernatural exists from the part that requires you to stop asking. It is not a dismissal. Plenty of the practice survives inspection. But the parts that survive are less dramatic than the marketing, and knowing which is which is the difference between a method and a bill.

What is actually being sold under this heading

A great deal of it is cheap and legitimate: a reduction method, a charge procedure, a log template, a reminder to write the target as a command. A great deal of it is expensive and largely inert: courses that sell certainty, coaching that sells accountability at a price that assumes you cannot hold yourself to a standard on your own, and a set of recommended objects at retail. Both categories are sold under the same name and the difference between them is not visible from the outside.

The distinguishing question is simple and worth asking of anything in this genre. Does the claim describe an action the seller will help you perform, or does it describe a state you will be granted? The first can be useful. The second can only be delivered as a story. Much of the expensive end of the market answers the second question, and the answer is usually delivered with a testimonial rather than a mechanism.

The four claims that do the work, and each one's status

Strip away the ornament and four claims carry almost the entire genre. First, that a written and charged statement of intent produces a change in your behaviour that a mere intention would not. Second, that a specific number of days is required for the result to arrive. Third, that the quality or timing of the charge affects whether it lands. Fourth, that money is an unusually suitable target because it is quantifiable and socially legible.

The first is the only one that survives contact with evidence, and it survives for a boring reason: writing a specific imperative and acting on it changes what you do, which is a documented property of commitment devices rather than of magic. The second is arbitrary. The third is unestablished and inconsistent across practitioners. The fourth is an observation about symbolism that has no consequence for outcomes, and it is nonetheless treated as a discovery in almost every text.

The number in the abundance material, examined

A number recurs through abundance practice, usually with the explanation that its digits sum to a figure that appears across temperament and numerological charts. The observation is real in the sense that people have been doing arithmetic on numbers for a long time and some of those sums do recur. What is not established is that the recurrence means anything, and the practice borrows the appearance of a mechanism without one.

The honest description is that this is a piece of traditional framing that has been retrofitted with a numerical explanation. It is not fraudulent, because nobody is claiming a physical pathway, but it is not doing work either, and a practitioner who insists the number is load-bearing has taken on a claim they cannot support. It is entirely reasonable to keep using the number as a mnemonic and to stop describing it as a cause.

Where the sigil method earns its keep

The sigil's value is narrow and genuine. Reducing a sentence to a glyph takes an abstract aspiration and turns it into a specific artefact, and that conversion is genuinely hard to do well by wishing. The result is a finished object you can point at, which forces a certain kind of clarity about what you are actually asking for. Many people who cannot write a good imperative sentence can produce a good sigil, because the reduction refuses to accept vagueness.

That is the whole case for the method, and it is a case about communication rather than about influence. Nothing in it requires the glyph to be doing anything to the world. What it does is make a vague commitment concrete enough to act on, and act on it or not is entirely up to you. Practitioners who describe the sigil as a device for transmitting influence have misunderstood their own method, and practitioners who describe it as a focus for attention have understood it.

The mechanism nobody has to believe in

The strongest feature of the tradition is that it never required belief. That is not a courtesy formulation. It is a design decision that turns out to be enormously useful, because it means the method can be applied and evaluated by someone who holds no supernatural commitment at all. A skeptic can reduce a sentence, charge the glyph, keep the log and check the window, and everything that is actually going to happen will still happen.

This is a much rarer position than it sounds, and it is the reason the modern tradition has outlived most of its contemporaries. When a practice makes no demand on your private convictions, it can recruit people who were never going to join anything, and it can be tested by people with no stake in the answer. Any teacher in this genre who tells you that belief is a prerequisite has added a condition that the method does not need and that you will not be able to check.

The observation window, and why the number is arbitrary

Almost every text specifies an observation period, and the figures cluster between about a month and three months. There is no evidence for a particular value. The window is a compromise between long enough that slow results can appear and short enough that you do not abandon it before the effort has had a chance. Both considerations are legitimate and neither is metaphysical.

Where the figure becomes a problem is when it is presented as a deadline imposed by the method, or when a working is declared failed the day the window closes. A deadline you chose in advance is a useful discipline. A deadline the tradition imposes is an arbitrary rule you can fail without anything having been tested. If you adopt a window, write it down before you start and treat it as a date for review rather than a date for judgement.

Timing, ranked where it does not belong

Timing is the most over-weighted variable in the genre. A precisely timed working aimed at a vague goal will underperform a plain working aimed at one specific measurable action, and this is reported often enough across enough practitioners that it should be treated as the working assumption. A bad target on an auspicious day is still a bad target. A good target worked on an ordinary Tuesday is usually better.

There is a small real effect available here and it is not the one the texts describe. Choosing a specific date in advance raises the likelihood that you will still be thinking about the goal on that date, which is a salience effect rather than a planetary one. If you want to use timing, use it for that, and skip the calculations. The lunar phase in particular is very unlikely to be doing anything except making you feel you have done something more elaborate than you did.

The compulsive version and how it starts

The most reliable way this practice goes wrong is not supernatural and not financial in the way people imagine. It is a slow tightening of the link between your sense of self-worth and the outcome of a working. Early on the working feels like a tool. Later it begins to feel like a verdict, and the checking becomes compulsive. The first sign is checking more often than you pre-registered for.

The mechanics are ordinary. You made a prediction, you care about it, and uncertainty is uncomfortable, so you reduce the uncertainty by looking. The looking produces no new information and the discomfort returns, so you look again. This is the same loop that drives compulsive checking in every other domain, and it responds to the same intervention, which is a fixed check schedule and a decision rule applied on that schedule. If the compulsion has started, the correct action is to stop the working rather than to escalate it.

What a null result would look like if you looked

Most people never find out, because a null result is not emotionally available. You made a prediction, you told nobody the prediction, and at the end of the window there is nothing in front of you. The honest move is to write that down as a null. The dishonest move, which almost everyone makes at least once, is to widen the observation until something counts, and then to call it a partial result.

The reason this matters is cumulative. A record of twenty workings in which four produced a clearly observed result is a much stronger object than twenty workings in which nineteen were recorded as successes. The first record tells you something about the method. The second tells you only what you hoped. The discipline of writing the null is unglamorous and it is the single change most likely to make this practice worth doing.

The part that is genuinely about your behaviour

Once the claims are stripped, what remains is a set of changes in behaviour, and they are straightforward to state. You will have written one specific sentence instead of holding a general aspiration. You will have a date by which you will review, which means the goal stays live in your head on days when nothing happens. You will have removed a decision by committing to one. And you will have a written record, which means the next time you face the same question you start from what you already know instead of from scratch.

Each of those is a real behavioural change with documented effects on follow-through, and none of them requires anything about the world to be different. This is not a consolation prize. It is the actual mechanism, and it was available to the people who wrote the earliest instructions in this tradition. The metaphysical framing was added later, by writers who were describing a mechanism they could not see clearly rather than one that was missing.

How the genre handles disagreement

Poorly, and the pattern is worth recognising because it is a general one. A practitioner's account of a working is treated as evidence for the method rather than as a data point about one person on one occasion. Disconfirming experiences are usually reclassified, either by adjusting the target afterwards or by attributing the miss to insufficient belief, poor timing or an unrelated outside factor. The reclassification is always available, which is what makes the position unfalsifiable.

The correction is cheap and it is entirely internal. Pre-register the observation, decide in advance what would count as a miss, and allow the working to be logged as a miss. A tradition that can record a miss is a tradition that can accumulate knowledge, and one that cannot is a collection of anecdotes with a shared vocabulary. The difference is visible from the outside and it is a fair test of any teacher you are considering.

A diagnostic for any wealth claim you meet

Four questions, applied to any claim in this genre, will separate the useful material from the rest. What action of mine does this change? What would I have to believe for it to be true? What would count as evidence that it is not true? And is the seller asking me to pay for the method, for the accountability, or for the reassurance. A claim that fails the first question is not worth your time regardless of how it answers the others.

Run those four against a paragraph of any course description and you will usually get a clear verdict quickly. The claims that survive all four are rarely dramatic. They tend to be about writing things down, picking one action, and reviewing on a date, and they are unglamorous enough that nobody sells them. That is not a reason to distrust the material. It is a reason to prefer the parts that nobody is trying to sell you.

What to keep when you strip the claims away

Keep the statement of intent, the reduction, the charge, the dismissal, the written log and a fixed review date. Keep the discipline of naming one target rather than four. Keep the habit of writing the null. Discard the mandatory observation period, the numerical correspondences presented as mechanism, the claims about planetary influence on financial outcomes, and the entire apparatus of escalating sacrifice.

What is left is a compact and rather good method for forcing a vague intention into a specific action. It works, it does not require belief, and it can be improved measurably by keeping the record. That is a better position than the one this genre usually offers, because nothing has been given up that was doing work, and the parts that were not doing work are now simply not occupying your attention.

The question to ask before you pay anyone for this

Ask what happens if the method fails. If the answer is that you will need a more expensive version, or a longer course, or a private consultation, then the purchase is not for a method. It is for a process in which your continued payment is the intended outcome. A method that fails should make you review your log, not your invoice. Anything that requires ongoing payment in order to interpret your own results is selling a dependency, whatever the technique being taught.

A fair alternative exists and it is not much more expensive than most of what is on offer. A notebook, a timer, a reliable calendar, and a rule that you review on a date you set in advance. Everything else is convenience. Spend on convenience if you enjoy it, but know which part of your purchase is the part you would miss, and that part is smaller than the marketing suggests.

Frequently Asked Questions

What would a paid course on this actually teach me?

Probably a better version of what is described here: write one imperative sentence, reduce it, charge it, log it, review on a date. That is a genuinely useful body of method and it does not take long to explain. The expensive part is the accountability, which is genuinely valuable to some people and is the thing you can also get from a friend, a group, or a fixed appointment with yourself. Buy the structure if you cannot build it alone, but understand which part you are buying.

Is any of this actually dangerous?

The documented risk is psychological rather than occult, and it is a real one. Tying your sense of self-worth to an outcome produces anxiety, compulsive checking and occasionally a large financial decision made in a panic, usually under the impression that the working has failed and needs more investment. If you find yourself checking daily, escalating, or borrowing to fund an offering, stop. Any practitioner who tells you that intensity is a sign of progress is describing the failure mode.

Does the sigil need to be charged on a particular day?

No, and the evidence anyone offers for it is entirely internal. The one timing effect that survives is mundane: fixing a date in advance keeps the goal salient on that date, which is a salience effect rather than a planetary one. A working with a vague target performed perfectly on an auspicious day will underperform a working with one measurable target performed on an ordinary one, and the second variable is doing nearly all of the work in both cases.

How long should I observe a working before deciding?

Choose a window in advance, write it in the log, and treat the date as a review rather than a verdict. Between one and three months covers most of what a behavioural change can produce, and the specific figure is a judgement call rather than a fact. What is not a judgement call is deciding afterwards that the window was too short, because that is the mechanism by which a method can never be found to have failed.

Are the older grimoires any better on this subject?

Not on the substance, and reading them is instructive precisely because of that. Older wealth material describes access, discovery, luck and the removal of obstacles, which is a considerably more modest claim than money arriving. It also attaches conditions and obligations to the arrangement, which is the opposite of the friction-free version sold today. The older material is harder to read and easier to trust, because it was not trying to close a sale.
