The deal, with no small print

What the bundle does not include: anything else. There is no course attached, no coaching, no membership that renews quietly in the background. It is nine PDFs you read. If someone is selling you that this comes with more, they are selling you something else.

The discount is time-boxed because the bundle has a purchase window, not because the price is going up. When the window closes the bundle goes back to its normal price, and the individual books stay where they are.

The nine, one by one

This is the honest breakdown: what each one covers and who it is for. Not every book suits every practitioner, and a book that is wrong for you does not get smarter because it is on sale.

Codex Chaoticus ($4.99)

The complete chaos magick grimoire: sigils, gnosis, servitors, paradigms and the exercises for each. If you are buying one book, this is the one, because everything else in the bundle assumes you have read it. It is also the right starting point for someone who has never done this before, and the reference the practicing reader keeps coming back to.

Magical Servitors Manual ($3.99)

Creating and working with servitors: design, activation phrase, feeding, maintenance. The shortest of the nine and the one that produces a visible result fastest, because a well-designed servitor shows up inside a single session. For anyone who has done sigils and wants something that keeps working between sessions.

Treatise of Chaos Hunter Runes ($3.99)

The chaos rune system in full: the reduced alphabet, the planetary and day attributions, and how to cast deliberately. It is the natural companion to Tarot Chaos rather than a substitute for it. One works with character, the other with timing and situation.

Tarot Chaos ($9.99)

The Rider-Waite deck read from a chaos magick position: the symbols as a map of the person rather than as prediction. For readers who already use tarot and want the chaos reading of the same cards, or who want tarot without the fortune-telling framing.

Mind The Gap ($9.99)

Altered states, practically: how to prepare, what to do at each level, and how to avoid turning the experience into a ladder of Huxley and friends. No dogma, just protocol. For the practitioner who no longer needs the concept explained to them.

CATHOLICONOMICON ($9.99)

Chaos magick for Catholic practitioners: how symbolic work sits inside a faith, what can and cannot be done, and where the line is. It is the book that prevents the most questions. For the Catholic who takes their faith seriously and wants to practise without breaking it.

Liber Lvpinux ($3.99)

The book of the wolf: lycanthropy as philosophy and practice, in a chaos magick key. Not a novel. A piece of work on shadow, hunger and identity, for readers drawn to the mythology of the wolf and the psychology of the limit.

Ouija Cazadora ($3.99)

Spirit communication through the ouija board, in Spanish, with the full protocol. It is the only one of the nine originally written in Spanish and the most argued-over in the collection. If the ouija is your thing, this is the one. If it is not, it is not worth buying because it sounds interesting.

The Complete Practical Treatise on Magical Servitors ($9.99)

The long servitor grimoire, with everything the short manual leaves out: variations, transference, long-term maintenance, and the problems that show up once a servitor has been running for months. It completes the $3.99 manual rather than duplicating it.

Where to start if you are starting from zero

Order matters more than quantity. If you have never practised, this is the route that works:

  1. Codex Chaoticus for the framework and your first sigils. It is the book the other eight lean on.
  2. Magical Servitors Manual once sigils are familiar: it is the first step from a one-off working to something with continuity.
  3. Treatise of Chaos Hunter Runes or Tarot Chaos, whichever calls you. They are the same level and you do not need both at once.
  4. The rest when you actually need them.

If you already have a practice and want the whole shelf, the bundle makes sense on price. It does not make sense because you need nine books.

What you do not need yet

One thing the discount hides: buying a single book is more expensive per book. The bundle is better value per unit, but only if you are going to read them. If you are torn between two, buying those two on their own leaves enough budget for the third in two months, when you know whether you want it.

And the useful warning: none of this starts with reading. It starts with doing. One chapter, one practice, one written record. Reading nine books without practising is collecting opinions, and no discount fixes that.

Try before you buy

Before you pay, check that this is the tradition for you. There are 70 free tools on this site, and several of them hand you the thing you were looking for:

  • The sigil generator: your first sigil, in the browser, no account, no payment.
  • The I Ching oracle: a full three-coin cast, to see whether the system suits you.
  • The rune drawer and the lunar phase calculator.

If you want the whole method in writing after using those, the bundle is for you. If you already know it is, buy it now. Both are fine answers.
