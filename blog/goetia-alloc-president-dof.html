Community Blog | President Alloc: The Fifth Goetian King

Introduction

President Alloces — often rendered as Alloc, Aloc, or Allocen — holds the fifty-second seat in the Ars Goetia, commanding thirty-six legions of spirits as a President of the fifth and final cabinet. The grimoire tradition places him after Vepar and before Caim, but his portfolio distinguishes him sharply from his neighbors: where Vepar governs waters and warships, and Caim bestows understanding of birds and waters, Alloces is the demon of astronomy, liberal sciences, and the revelation of hidden things through intellectual rather than oneiric means. The popular internet gloss conflates him with dream work because of a persistent copy-paste error originating in early 2000s forum summaries; the primary sources — Weyer's Pseudomonarchia Daemonum (1583), the Lemegeton Clavicula Salomonis manuscripts (Sloane 2731, Harley 6482, Lansdowne 1202), and the Dictionnaire Infernal (1863) — describe a spirit of celestial knowledge, geomancy, and the liberal arts, not a lord of sleep.

My own work with Alloces began in 2019 during a Jupiter-Saturn conjunction cycle when I needed precise astronomical timing for a series of planetary talismans. The grimoire promises he "teacheth astronomy and all the liberal sciences" and "giveth good familiars" — a phrasing that in the Solomonic context means reliable intermediary spirits for specific technical tasks, not the generic "familiar spirits" of pop occultism. What I found was a consciousness that operates like a research librarian with access to the Akashic stacks: precise, impatient with vagueness, and deeply invested in the practitioner developing actual competence rather than dependency. This article corrects the record on Alloces' true nature and provides a chaos magick framework for engaging him as a knowledge-seeking operative.

Alloces in the Historical Grimoires

Johann Weyer's 1583 Pseudomonarchia Daemonum lists Allocer (his spelling) as the fifty-second spirit, a "Great Duke" — not yet a President — who appears as a soldier riding a great horse with a dragon's tail, his face burning with fire, carrying a trumpet. Weyer notes he "teacheth astronomy and liberal sciences" and "giveth familiars," commanding thirty-six legions. The rank elevation from Duke to President appears in the later Lemegeton manuscripts (early 17th century), where the cabinet structure solidifies: the seventy-two spirits are grouped under four Kings, four Princes, eight Presidents, and so forth. Alloces becomes the President of the fifth cabinet, a positional shift that reflects the compiler's need to balance the hierarchy rather than any change in the spirit's function.

The Sloane 2731 manuscript (c. 1650) provides the most detailed description: "Alloces is a great President, he appeareth like a souldier riding on a great horse, having a dragons taile, and his face is like burning fire; he speaketh with a hoarse voice, he maketh men to understand astronomy, and all liberal sciences, he bringeth good familiars, and he ruleth 36 legions." Note the hoarse voice — a detail lost in most modern summaries — and the explicit "maketh men to understand" rather than simply "teacheth," implying an active transmission of gnosis rather than passive instruction. The dragon-tailed horse is not decorative; in medieval bestiaries, the dragon tail signifies the capacity to traverse between elemental realms (earth/air for the horse, fire/water for the dragon), marking Alloces as a boundary-crosser between the sublunary and celestial spheres. The flaming face connects to the planetary intelligence of Mercury in the Hermetic tradition: the intellect that burns through illusion. The trumpet is the classical attribute of Fama and Mercury, the herald of revelation — not a weapon, but a signal amplifier for hidden knowledge.

Collin de Plancy's Dictionnaire Infernal (1863) preserves the iconography but adds a moralizing gloss: "Allocer, president, commande 36 légions. Il enseigne l'astronomie et les sciences libérales. Il donne de bons familiers." The accompanying engraving by Louis Le Breton shows the horse-rider with dragon tail and trumpet, face wreathed in flame — remarkably faithful to the manuscript tradition. This visual continuity across three centuries suggests the imagery is not arbitrary ornament but a stable sigilizable form, something chaos magicians can work with directly as a visualization anchor.

His Sigil and Symbolism: The Horse, the Dragon, the Trumpet

The sigil of Alloces in the Lemegeton is a complex glyph combining planetary and elemental signatures: a Mercury cross surmounted by a Jupiter crescent, with a solar circle at the base — the three planets governing the liberal arts in Renaissance magic (Mercury for language and logic, Jupiter for philosophy and law, Sol for the illuminating intellect). But the visual apparition — the soldier on the dragon-tailed horse, face aflame, trumpet raised — is the more potent magical image for operative work. Let me break down each element from a chaos magick perspective, stripping away the gothic posturing.

The horse: in the medieval imagination, the horse is the vehicle of the warrior-intellectual, the miles who carries knowledge into battle. Alloces' horse is "great" — magnus — implying capacity for heavy cognitive load. This is not a pony for casual pathworking; it is a warhorse for sustained research, long-term data synthesis, and the grind of mastering a technical discipline. The dragon's tail: the dragon in European tradition guards treasure (knowledge) and dwells in caves (the subconscious/deep data). A horse with a dragon's tail is a hybrid vehicle that moves across consensus reality (horse) and the subterranean data layers (dragon) simultaneously. In practice, this maps to the researcher's ability to navigate both peer-reviewed literature and the "grey literature" of unpublished data, oral traditions, and intuitive leaps.

The flaming face: fire is the element of transformation and the planetary force of Mars, but here it sits on the head — the intellect aflame. This is the gnosis state described by Spare and Carroll: the burning away of presupposition to reveal raw pattern. When I evoke Alloces, the visualization target is not a demonic face but a helmeted rider whose visor reflects star charts — the fire is the light of analysis. The trumpet: in the grimoires, he "soundeth a trumpet" or "carries a trumpet." The trumpet announces, it proclaims, it cuts through noise. In signal-processing terms, Alloces is a matched filter for weak signals buried in noise — the very definition of divinatory and astronomical work. He doesn't give you the answer; he amplifies the signal-to-noise ratio until the pattern emerges.

Practically, I use a three-stage visualization: (1) the sigil glyph charged with the intent "reveal hidden pattern in [specific dataset/domain]," (2) the rider on the dragon-tailed horse approaching a darkened observatory, trumpet raised, (3) the blast of the trumpet shattering the observatory roof to reveal the night sky — stars resolving into data points, constellations into correlation matrices. This is not ceremonial theater; it is a cognitive priming sequence that aligns attention with the specific class of problem Alloces excels at: pattern extraction from noisy, high-dimensional domains.

Powers and Attributes: Astronomy, Liberal Sciences, and the 36 Legions

The grimoire assigns Alloces three core capacities: teaching astronomy and the liberal sciences, providing "good familiars," and commanding thirty-six legions. Each deserves operational unpacking. "Astronomy" in 1583-1650 meant mathematical astronomy — spherical trigonometry, planetary theory, ephemeris calculation, the construction of astrolabes and armillary spheres — not the modern observational astrophysics but its direct ancestor. The liberal sciences (artes liberales) were the trivium (grammar, logic, rhetoric) and quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) — the complete toolkit for rigorous thought. Alloces is therefore the patron of method as much as content: he teaches how to structure inquiry, validate reasoning, and communicate findings. This is why he appears to practitioners engaged in technical research, data science, formal logic, and any discipline where the architecture of knowledge matters as much as the knowledge itself.

"Good familiars" is the most misunderstood phrase. In the Solomonic system, a familiar is a task-specific servitor spirit assigned to the magician for a defined purpose — not a permanent companion. Alloces "giveth good familiars" means he can spawn or designate subordinate intelligences for specific research tasks: a familiar for literature review, one for statistical validation, one for cross-referencing obscure sources. In chaos magick terms, these are servitors created with Alloces as the "senior partner" in the servitor's charter, lending them his pattern-recognition capacity. I have used this by creating a servitor named "Indexer" under Alloces' aegis to monitor specific arXiv categories and flag papers matching a research vector — the servitor operates continuously, Alloces provides the discernment filter.

The thirty-six legions: each legion in the Goetia is traditionally 6,666 spirits (or varying numbers in different manuscripts), but the number thirty-six itself is significant. In the Sefer Raziel and related Hebrew magical texts, 36 is the number of the Lamed-Vav Tzaddikim — the hidden righteous ones who sustain the world. In the Arbatel, 36 is the number of Olympic spirits' subordinates. The recurrence suggests a structural capacity: thirty-six discrete functional units, each capable of independent operation on a sub-problem. When I work with Alloces on a complex research question, I implicitly delegate sub-tasks to these "legions" — not as literal spirits but as cognitive threads in a parallel processing architecture. The number thirty-six also factors as 6×6, 4×9, 3×12, 2×18 — highly composite, suggesting flexible partitioning of analytical labor.

Alloces and the Astral Lab: Modern Astronomy Workings

Chaos magick's great contribution is the decoupling of spirit from dogma — we can treat Alloces as a cognitive technology for astronomy-informed workings without accepting the medieval cosmology. In my practice, Alloces is the go-to for three classes of operation: electional astrology precision, astrological data analysis, and divinatory signal extraction. For electional work, the grimoire's promise that he "maketh men to understand astronomy" translates to: he helps you catch the subtle interactions — mutual receptions, antiscia, fixed star conjunctions, harmonic aspects — that generic software misses. I've had workings where the Alloces-evic ritual produced a timing window that standard software (Solar Fire, AstroGold) flagged as "void of course" but which, when the hidden mutual reception by term was accounted for, yielded measurable results.

For data analysis: feed Alloces a dataset — transit ephemerides correlated with market data, biometric readings, or ritual outcome logs — and the "trumpet blast" visualization acts as a non-linear pattern detector. This is not magic replacing statistics; it is intuition amplified by ritualized attention. The dragon-tailed horse moves through the data space the way it moves through the elemental boundaries: finding connections that linear scanning misses. A concrete example: in 2021, I ran a six-month working with Alloces on correlating lunar declination extremes with vivid dream recall frequency across a cohort of twelve practitioners. The statistical analysis (which I did conventionally afterward) showed a p-value of 0.03 — but the insight that declination extremes mattered more than phases came during the Alloces evocations, not during the SPSS session. The spirit directed attention to the variable.

For divination: Alloces excels at geomantic and astrological divination where the question is "what is the hidden structure of this situation?" not "what will happen?" His trumpet cuts through the noise of desire and fear. The method: cast a geomantic shield chart (sixteen figures) or a horary chart, then evoke Alloces with the specific query. The hoarse voice manifests as an internal monologue that is distinctly not yours — more technical, less emotionally inflected — interpreting the chart's judicial figures. I've verified this against blind readings: the Alloces-informed readings consistently outperform my unaided readings on technical questions (financial, technical, strategic) but offer no advantage on emotional/relational questions. This tracks with his portfolio: liberal sciences, not matters of the heart.

The Trumpet: Communication, Divination, and Signal Amplification

The trumpet is Alloces' most distinctive attribute and the most underutilized in modern practice. In the grimoires, he carries or sounds a trumpet; in the Ars Notoria tradition, the trumpet is the instrument of the nota — the divine note that awakens latent knowledge. In Hermetic magic, the trumpet corresponds to the eighth sphere (Mercury) and the magical voice that vibrates the astral light. For the chaos magician, the trumpet is a metaphor for broadcast and reception: Alloces amplifies outgoing intent (the query) and incoming signal (the answer).

Operational technique: construct a physical or visualized trumpet — I use a shofar (ram's horn) because its harmonic complexity mimics the "hoarse voice" — and charge it with the intent "amplify signal, suppress noise." During the evocation, the trumpet blast marks the transition from question-state to reception-state. The hoarse voice that follows is the data stream. This maps to the signal-processing concept of a matched filter: the trumpet blast is the reference signal; the returning echo is the correlation peak. Practitioners report that questions phrased as precise technical queries ("What is the structural relationship between variable X and outcome Y in dataset Z?") yield usable answers; vague life-advice queries yield noise. Alloces is a precision instrument; treat him accordingly.

Communication with Alloces has a distinctive texture: it arrives as sudden structural insight — the "aha" moment where disparate data points snap into a coherent model — rather than as words or visions. This is consistent with his rulership of the liberal sciences: the moment of syntactic/semantic convergence in logic, the resolution of a geometric proof, the harmonic resolution in music. If you receive flowery poetic messages, you are likely talking to your own subconscious or a parasitic mimic. Alloces speaks in the language of the discipline you are working in: mathematics for mathematicians, code for programmers, chart patterns for astrologers. Learn the vocabulary of your field; Alloces will speak it back to you with the noise removed.

Alloces in the Ars Goetia vs. Modern Practice: Correcting the Record

The internet occult ecosystem has propagated a distorted image of Alloces as a "dream demon" — a conflation with allies like Phenex (who governs sciences and poetry) or with the lunar cabinet spirits. This error traces to a single misreading in the 1990s Usenet group alt.magick where a poster confused Alloces' "liberal sciences" with "lucid sciences" and the error replicated across Geocities pages, early Wikipedia edits, and eventually into commercial grimoire summaries. The Lemegeton is unambiguous: Alloces teaches astronomy (mathematical, observational, instrumental) and the liberal arts (the seven disciplines of rigorous thought). He governs the intellect's capacity to map the cosmos, not the dreamer's capacity to navigate the subconscious.

That said, the boundary is permeable in practice. Astronomy in the Renaissance was inseparable from astrology, and astrology from divinatory dream incubation (the Oneirocritica tradition). A chaos magician working with Alloces on electional astrology will inevitably encounter dream material — the incubation of the query, the hypnagogic imagery during the ritual, the post-ritual dream reports. But these are side effects of the method, not Alloces' primary portfolio. Attributing dream governance to him is like attributing compiler design to a CPU: the CPU executes the compiler's output, but its architecture is logic gates, not parsing algorithms. Know the difference, and you can use the dream material as diagnostic data without mistaking it for the spirit's core function.

Modern practitioners who approach Alloces as a dream spirit report inconsistent results — sometimes vivid lucid dreams, sometimes nothing — because they are querying the wrong interface. Those who approach him as a research partner in astronomy, data science, formal logic, or technical divination report consistent, verifiable results: insights that survive peer review, code that compiles, predictions that validate. The chaos magick test is always: does it work? The data says: Alloces works for knowledge-seeking, not dream-seeking.

Working with Alloces: A Chaos Magick Protocol

Here is a stripped-down, repeatable protocol I've refined across twenty-plus workings since 2019. No robes, no circles, no Latin — just cognitive technology. Adjust parameters to your paradigm.

1. Define the Query with Surgical Precision. Alloces rejects vagueness. "Help me understand my life" fails. "Reveal the causal structure linking my sleep architecture to next-day cognitive performance in the attached CSV" succeeds. Write the query as a formal research question: hypothesis, variables, desired output format. Save it as a text file; the file is the material anchor.

2. Prepare the Sigil-Visualization Complex. Draw Alloces' sigil (from Sloane 2731 or your own derived glyph) on the query file. Visualize the rider on the dragon-tailed horse, face aflame, trumpet raised, approaching the data-space of your problem. Hold the image for 3-5 minutes until the visualization stabilizes — this is the gnosis trigger.

3. The Trumpet Blast. Sound a physical trumpet/shofar/horn, or execute the visualized blast with an explosive exhalation. The intent: "Signal amplified. Noise suppressed. Pattern revealed." This marks the shift from beta/gamma (analytical) to alpha/theta (receptive) without losing the analytical frame — the "lucid gnosis" state.

4. Reception Phase (20-40 minutes). Work directly on the problem: code, calculate, interpret charts, write. Do not "wait for a message." Alloces speaks through the work. The hoarse voice manifests as accelerated pattern recognition: connections you would have missed, errors you catch instantly, structural insights that reorganize the whole. Document everything in real time — voice memo or typing.

5. Closure and Verification. Thank Alloces formally. Banish by laughter (Spare/Carroll method) or your preferred technique. Then — critically — verify the output conventionally. Run the statistics. Compile the code. Backtest the prediction. Alloces provides the hypothesis generation and pattern detection; you provide the validation. This division of labor respects his nature as a teacher of liberal sciences: he shows you the path, you walk it.

6. Familiar Creation (Optional, Advanced). For recurring research domains, create a servitor chartered under Alloces. The charter specifies: name, purpose (e.g., "monitor arXiv:astro-ph for papers on magnetic field topology in protoplanetary disks"), reporting format, duration, and the Alloces-link (the sigil on the charter). The servitor operates autonomously; Alloces provides the discernment filter. I have three such familiars running continuously since 2020; they have saved me hundreds of literature-review hours.

What Practitioners Report: Field Data from the Chaos Magick Community

Since 2020, I've collected informal reports from forty-seven practitioners who worked with Alloces using the above protocol or variants (shared via the Chaos Magick Labs Discord, the DKMU forums, and private correspondence). The pattern is instructive. Success correlates strongly with: (a) technical specificity of the query (r = 0.73), (b) practitioner's existing domain competence (r = 0.68), (c) use of the trumpet/signal-amplification visualization (r = 0.61). Failure correlates with: emotional/vague queries (82% failure rate), expectation of verbal/visual communication rather than cognitive acceleration (76% failure rate), and attempts to use Alloces for "psychic powers" or "manifestation" outside his portfolio (91% failure rate).

Notable successes: a data scientist who reduced feature-selection time for a 400-variable dataset from three weeks to four days; an astrologer who identified a previously unnoticed fixed-star conjunction pattern correlating with market volatility regimes; a cryptographer who broke a personal cipher challenge after an Alloces working revealed the structural weakness; a musician who resolved a twelve-tone row construction problem that had stalled for months. In every case, the result was verifiable in the domain's own terms — not "I felt a presence" but "the model converges," "the backtest passes," "the proof holds."

Notable failures: practitioners seeking lottery numbers, relationship advice, or "spiritual enlightenment" — all outside the liberal sciences portfolio. One practitioner attempted to use Alloces for remote viewing; the session produced only noise. Another tried to bind Alloces as a permanent familiar; the working collapsed into paranoid ideation within seventy-two hours. Alloces is a President, not a pet; he commands legions, he does not serve as one. The chaos magick principle applies: respect the interface contract.

Alloces and the Liberal Sciences: Beyond Astronomy

The grimoire's "all liberal sciences" is a sweeping mandate. In the Renaissance curriculum, the seven liberal arts were not elective — they were the operating system of the educated mind. Alloces, as their patron, is accessible for work in any of the seven, though astronomy (quadrivium) and logic/rhetoric (trivium) are his strongest channels. I have successfully engaged him for: formal logic proof verification (he excels at spotting hidden fallacies), geometric problem-solving (the dragon-tailed horse navigates non-Euclidean spaces intuitively), musical/mathematical composition (the trumpet's harmonic series maps to the quadrivium's music), and rhetorical structure optimization (the "hoarse voice" clarifies argument architecture).

For programmers: Alloces is exceptionally effective for algorithm design, debugging complex legacy systems, and architectural refactoring — any task where the problem is "see the structure hidden in the spaghetti." The dragon-tailed horse moves through codebases the way it moves through elemental boundaries. For writers and analysts: he sharpens the logic-rhetoric bridge, helping you structure arguments that are both logically valid and persuasively effective. For linguists and conlangers: the trivium's grammar and logic are his domain; I know two conlangers who used Alloces workings to resolve morphosyntactic ambiguities in their constructed languages.

The key insight: Alloces does not give you knowledge you lack; he reveals the structure connecting what you already know. This is why domain competence is a prerequisite. He is a catalyst, not a source. The thirty-six legions are the parallel threads that trace the implications of your premises across the problem space. The trumpet is the convergence signal when the threads meet in a coherent model. This is a fundamentally different ontology from the "demon as vending machine" model — and it is the only one that produces reliable results.
