STARLIGHT

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 27, 2026 ⬢ 8 min read

Money is the target occult writers return to more than any other, and the reason is not greed. It is that money is unusually legible as a symbol. It is universal, it is quantifiable, other people can read it, and a change in it is visible from across a room. That makes it an excellent target for a practice that works by making an intention concrete and then acting on it. This guide works through a money sigil from the first sentence to the charge, treats the traditional numerological material honestly as borrowed rather than as mechanism, and spends most of its length on the part that decides whether any of it works: the fortnight afterwards, when the sigil is no longer interesting and you have to actually go and do the thing the sigil was pointing at.

Why writers keep aiming practice at money

Occult literature has treated wealth as a favourable target for centuries, and the reasoning has less to do with cosmology than with measurement. Money is a quantity you can count before and after. It converts an intention into something with a before and an after, which is exactly what a practice needs in order to be reviewed honestly.

It is also socially legible, which cuts both ways. Other people can see the result, so the practice is exposed to feedback that most occult work avoids. A sigil aimed at peace or clarity has no external referee. A sigil aimed at income does, and that exposure is uncomfortable and useful in roughly equal measure.

Seven, four, one, and the number twelve

The number 741 appears often in abundance material because its digits sum to twelve, a figure that recurs across numerological and astrological schemes. The practice borrows the association. It does not claim a physical link between the digits and a bank balance, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.

The honest way to use it is as a focusing device. Reducing an intention to a short numeric string gives the mind a compact handle to return to, and there is nothing magical about the arithmetic. If the number works for you it is because a short string is easier to hold in mind than a full sentence, which is also why sigilisation exists.

An imperative, not a request

The single most consequential formatting decision in chaos magic is grammatical mood. A sigil encodes a statement of intent, and statements of intent in this tradition are written as commands. I will receive work. I will have three clients. I will sleep without that loop.

This is not a claim that the universe takes instructions. It is a practical observation about how people behave. A request leaves room for negotiation with oneself, and the negotiation is where the effort goes. An imperative does not. Whatever else is true about a money working, the phrasing changes what you do in the following fortnight, and that is the part you control.

From sentence to glyph in one sitting

Write the sentence in the imperative. Strip out every letter that repeats, working from the first occurrence and keeping it, then reduce the remaining letters to a single glyph by whatever method you use. The glyph is the point where the statement stops being readable to you.

That loss of legibility is the mechanism, whatever you believe about the mechanism. A sentence you can still read is a sentence you can talk yourself out of, because the argument against it is also available in words. A glyph cannot be argued with by the part of you that produces arguments. The reduction is a commitment device and the commitment is what the following fortnight is made of.

The charge, and what it is for

The charge takes a few minutes and its purpose is to attach the glyph to a specific state. A fixed sequence works better than an improvised one because consistency is what makes the association learnable. Dim the light, hold the glyph, and bring to mind the state you want to be in when the money arrives rather than the money itself.

Emotional targets outperform numerical ones. A sigil charged with the feeling of having options, of being the person whose bills are boring, is more use than one charged with a figure, because what actually changes your behaviour is the felt state. The number is the output. The state is the aim.

The first fortnight, where the real work happens

Nothing visible happens in the first fortnight, and this is the point at which most money workings die. The charge is emotionally satisfying and then the work arrives, which is unsatisfying, and the two feelings are not obviously related. Practitioners who treat the charge as the event and the fortnight as an inconvenience have misunderstood where the work is.

The sigil's real function is a commitment device that survives low motivation. You committed while in a good state, and now you have to execute while in an ordinary one. Treat the fortnight as the working and the charge as the paperwork that authorised it. That reframing sounds harsh and it is what makes the thing function.

What the ledger shows and what the bank shows

Two different records are needed and they will not agree. The bank statement is the outcome measure. The ledger is the process measure: applications sent, conversations had, quotes issued, follow-ups made, work published.

When the bank does not move, the ledger tells you which step failed. Six applications and no replies is a targeting problem. Thirty conversations and no quotes is a pricing or positioning problem. No applications at all is a commitment problem. Without the second record you are left with the first one and no way to diagnose anything.

Where a money working quietly fails

The most common quiet failure is a target with no verb attached. Wealth is a noun and nouns do not generate activity. Writing a sigil for abundance, with no statement of what changes on Monday morning, produces a token rather than a commitment.

The second is a target outside your control. A sigil aimed at a particular employer, a particular person, or a particular market segment is a request for somebody else's decision, and the results are not yours to interpret. The third is running several money workings at once, which makes the ledger useless because you cannot attribute any outcome to any of them.

Tools that help and tools that distract

Two tools earn their place. A sigil generator, for the reduction step, because doing letter-stripping by hand for every working is a way to avoid doing the work. And a plain spreadsheet for the ledger, with one row per action and a date column, because the diagnosis above requires the data to actually exist.

Astrological timing for money work has a reasonable rationale, which is that it marks a decision point and puts a date on a commitment. It has a poor record as a cause of financial change, and it becomes an excuse when the ledger is empty. A transit calculator is a way of choosing a Tuesday. It is not a way of earning anything.

Keeping the working from going stale

A money working has a natural life of about a month before it stops being a live commitment and becomes a piece of paper in a drawer. The refresh is a rewrite, not a re-charge. Take the original sentence, update the specific number or the specific action, and build a new glyph. Same aim, current form.

Some practitioners run a monthly cycle and others run one per job or per client. The monthly cycle suits a general income aim and the per-opportunity version suits freelance work. What does not work is letting a single sigil run for a year, because by then it is a talisman rather than a decision and the fortnight it authorised has long since passed.

A working that changed nothing you can point at

If the ledger is empty after a month, the working did not fail through a weak charge. It failed because there was nothing for the charge to act on. This happens, and the correct response is to run the ordinary job of finding work with the sigil set aside, then to charge a new one for the next specific action.

That is not an admission that the method is nonsense. It is the same structure as any commitment device that was pointed at the wrong thing. The sigil did its job, which was to make an intention specific, and then the intention turned out to be insufficiently operational. Write a better one.

The ethics of an abundance practice

The line worth drawing is between influencing your own decisions and influencing other people without their knowledge. A sigil for finding work is aimed at your own application rate. A sigil for a named client is aimed at that person's decision, and the results are not yours to interpret.

There is also a question about what happens when it works. Sudden income often arrives alongside obligations, and a practitioner who has spent a month in a scarcity state can make commitments that a more settled person would decline. If the working produces what you asked for, the next step is a second working about the obligations, which is less satisfying to write and more useful to have.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a money sigil actually attract money?

There is no evidence that a glyph causes a change in the world, and the honest account is narrower. Writing an aim as a command, reducing it to a glyph, and charging it is a commitment procedure. Its effect is on what you do afterwards, which is real and measurable in the ledger. Whether you find that satisfying depends on what you were hoping the sigil was.

What money targets work best in sigil work?

Targets with a verb in them. Sending five applications, publishing three pieces of work, having two pricing conversations, following up on the outstanding quote. Abundance, wealth, and financial flow are nouns and they generate no activity. The better the target describes a countable action on a specific date, the more useful the working is.

How long should a money working run before I refresh it?

About a month, and the refresh should be a rewrite rather than a repeat. Update the specific figure or the specific action and build a new glyph. A sigil that has been charged for a year is a talisman and no longer functions as a decision device, because the decision it authorised has already been made or abandoned.

Does timing matter for a money sigil?

Timing works as a marker. Choosing a date puts a boundary on the commitment, which is useful, and the ritual quality of a set date makes the start easier to begin. Timing does not appear to function as a cause of financial change. Use it to make the decision concrete and do not let a transit calculation substitute for the ledger.

Is it manipulative to work on money when other people are involved?

It is, if the target is another person's decision. A sigil aimed at a named employer, client, or partner is an attempt to influence someone who has not agreed to it, and the results are not yours to interpret or count. Money workings aimed at your own application rate, your own pricing, or your own published output raise no such problem, and that is the boundary worth holding.
