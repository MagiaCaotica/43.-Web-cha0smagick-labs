Demonic Wealth Pacts

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 8 min read

This is the operational version of the demonic wealth pact. It walks the whole sequence from the first hour to the review date, including the parts that are usually skipped: choosing the spirit by its stated domain, writing the fallback before the request, and deciding in advance what counts as a failure. The order matters more than the symbolism. Most broken versions of this practice fail at the review, not the ritual, because nobody decided what a miss looks like while they were still able to think clearly. What follows assumes you have already read the source material and know that no pact in the historical record creates money where none existed. It assumes you want access, discovery or the lifting of one obstacle. If you want a specific sum, the correct answer is that no spirit in the text offers it, and no sequence of steps will change that.

Step one, before any ritual happens

Decide what you are actually asking for, in one sentence, using the vocabulary the sources support. Access to a specific opportunity. Discovery of something already present. Removal of a named obstacle that you can name. A sentence containing the word amount is a request for a guarantee, and the text does not contain one.

Then name the obstacle or the field of search. Vague intentions produce vague logs, and a vague log cannot answer the only question that matters at review, which is whether anything specific changed. One noun phrase, written down, is worth a page of atmosphere.

  1. Write the request as one sentence using access, discovery or removal.
  2. Name the specific field, employer, contract, person or obstacle involved.
  3. Name the review date before you name the offering.

Step two, choosing between the two candidates

Only two spirits in the older material carry a wealth domain in any consistent way, and they are not interchangeable. Clauneck, a duke, is described in terms of riches, arts, sciences and the discovery of hidden things. Mammon, a king, is described in terms of coin, favour and the removal of an obstacle.

Match the description to the sentence you wrote. Discovery of hidden opportunities matches Clauneck. A blockage sitting between you and something already in circulation matches Mammon. If neither description fits what you wrote, that is information, and the information is that your request needs rewriting rather than a different spirit.

Step three, the terms, stated in four lines

The terms are the whole practice. Write four lines and date them. The first line is what you give, expressed as a figure or a fixed habit that ordinary income covers. The second is the request from step one. The third is the review date. The fourth is the correction you will apply if the review finds nothing, agreed with yourself while you are not under pressure.

The fourth line is the one that gets left out, and leaving it out is the mechanism by which these arrangements escalate. Without a pre-agreed response to failure, every cycle concludes with a negotiation, and the person doing the negotiating is the one who wants the outcome most.

  1. Line one: a specific amount, or a named habit, sustainable from ordinary income.
  2. Line two: the single request, one sentence, copied from step one.
  3. Line three: a calendar date, not a season or a lunar phase.
  4. Line four: the action you take if the date arrives with nothing to report.

Step four, the working itself

The traditional forms were brief. A fixed offering, a stated request, a period of attention and a dismissal. Length is not what makes it work, and a long ceremony for a small request tends to inflate the significance of the small request, which is the opposite of what you want.

Mark the offering as given rather than merely intended. The distinction sounds pedantic until you notice how many of these practices fail through a drift in which things were planned and which were done. Intention is the cheap part. The record is the part that costs anything, and it is the part that keeps the arrangement honest.

Step five, the cycle, kept boring on purpose

Run the same cycle on the same interval until the review date. Consistency is the only parameter here that has any evidence behind it, because repeated identical behaviour is how you isolate the one thing you changed. Change the spirit, the timing, the music and the offering at once and you will learn nothing, which is the most common way people conclude that the practice does not work.

Repetition also does quiet work on the intensity of the wanting. An arrangement that is running quietly in the background for eight weeks is much less likely to be driven by a mood on any given evening than one you keep re-deciding nightly.

Step six, the review, and what a miss looks like

At the review date, read the log before you read the terms. Write what actually changed, not what you hoped for. A miss is not a failed ritual. A miss is the condition your fourth line anticipated, and you wrote it in advance precisely so that this moment would not require a decision.

If the log shows the request was vague, the miss is in the sentence rather than in the arrangement, and you rewrite the sentence. If the log shows you gave more than the terms stated, the miss is in the escalation, and you stop. If the log shows you gave exactly what you said and the world did not move, that is a genuine result, and it is worth recording plainly because it is the only kind of entry that tells you anything true.

  • Vague request, nothing happened: the sentence needs rewriting, not the spirit.
  • Giving exceeded the stated terms: the arrangement has escalated, close it.
  • Terms were met exactly and nothing moved: a real result, and record it as one.
  • You cannot produce the log: you have a wish, not a pact, and the difference is the log.

Step seven, closing properly

Every cycle ends with a dismissal, and the dismissal is not decoration. It is what converts an open arrangement into a completed one, and it is the step most often omitted by people who are relieved the cycle is ending and do not want another obligation on the calendar.

Then write the closing line in the log: what was given, what was asked, what was received, and the date. Four fields. After that you are free, and being free is the state the practice was designed to reach, whatever the promotional material implies.

A worked example with the numbers left in

Someone wants access to contract work. The request becomes one sentence: to be considered for three contracts in a named field before a date four weeks out. The offering is a fixed sum taken from ordinary income, the same every cycle. The review date is set at the start. The fourth line says that if nothing arrives by the date, the arrangement closes and the log gets written up as a miss.

Four weeks later the log shows one enquiry from a company that had not been on the list, no paid contract, and an outgoing application sent on the second cycle. That is a partial, and the fourth line does not distinguish partials from misses, so the correct action is to close. The useful part is that the record now says something specific, which is more than most informal versions of this produce in a year.

What to do when a cycle produces something odd

A cycle can produce an outcome that does not fit the request, and the temptation is to fold it in as a partial success. Resist it, or at least record it separately. An unexpected event attached to a pact is the point where the accounting goes wrong, because the frame has already started paying out in ambiguous currency.

Write the event into the facts column with no interpretation attached, then write your reading in the interpretation column where it can be argued with later. If the two columns agree in a month, you have learned something. If they only agree when the interpretation column is allowed to be generous, you have learned that the frame is doing the work.

  • Record the event in the facts column with no meaning attached.
  • Put your reading in the interpretation column, where it can be revised.
  • Do not raise the offering to test whether the odd result was the arrangement's doing.
  • Do not extend the review date; close the file and start a new cycle if you want another.

The one-week and four-week versions of the same cycle

Both are legitimate and they answer different questions. A one-week cycle tests whether you can complete a bounded commitment under time pressure, which is a real and useful thing to find out. A four-week cycle tests whether something outside the practice responds, which is a much harder question with a much lower hit rate.

People who are new to this tend to start with the one-week version and conclude from a clean four-week miss that the whole framework is empty. The inference does not follow. Running the short version well tells you nothing about the long version, in either direction.

  • One week: measures adherence, scheduling and whether you can close the file cleanly.
  • Four weeks: enough signal for a countable external outcome, still a small sample.
  • Twelve weeks: the shortest horizon where a pattern in the log can be argued about.

What changes when the same spirit is used twice

Repetition with the same spirit is closer to the traditional pattern than switching spirits is, and the tradition is a series of small recognitions rather than a single dramatic encounter. In practice this means familiarity is doing most of the work in what people report, and familiarity is exactly the thing that cannot be attributed to the spirit.

If you want to test that rather than assume it, alternate. Run the same terms with two different spirits over comparable periods and compare the logs, keeping the offering and the schedule identical. It is not a controlled experiment in any real sense, but it does convert a belief about which spirit to use into a comparison of two records, which is more than the belief was worth.

Either way, write the second cycle as a fresh cycle with its own terms and its own review date. Reopening a closed arrangement retroactively is how the record stops being able to tell you anything, and it is the single most common bookkeeping error in this material.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the correct order of steps for a money pact?

Write the request first, using access, discovery or removal rather than a sum. Name the review date before the offering. Then pick the spirit whose stated domain matches the sentence, run a short repeatable cycle, and close it with a dismissal and a written closing line. Most failures happen at the review, because the response to failure was never decided in advance.

Should I use Clauneck or Mammon?

Match the text to your sentence. Clauneck is described as granting riches, arts and sciences and as discovering hidden things, which suits a request about finding opportunity. Mammon is tied to coin, favour and the removal of an obstacle, which suits a blockage you can name. Choosing by modern reputation rather than by stated domain is the most common selection error.

How much should be given?

A figure that ordinary income covers without strain, given the same way each cycle. The traditional forms were a tithe, a percentage or a fixed offering. The test is not whether it feels meaningful, because meaningful amounts escalate easily. The test is whether you can keep the same figure for a full cycle without borrowing or cutting something else.

What if nothing happens by the review date?

Apply the fourth line you wrote before the cycle started. Most often the request was vague and needs rewriting. Occasionally the giving exceeded the terms, and that is the signal to stop rather than to increase. If the terms were met exactly and nothing moved, record it as a real result, because that entry is worth more than any number of impressions.

Does a paid app help with keeping track of this?

The ritual method is free and the sources are public, so a paid tool is not buying access to hidden knowledge. What it usually buys is the tedious part: a dated log per cycle, the four terms kept together instead of scattered across notes, and a nudge when a review date arrives. Buy that if your logs are the weak link. Do not buy it expecting a stronger guarantee, because no arrangement in the sources offers one.
