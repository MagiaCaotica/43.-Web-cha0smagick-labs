INCIENSO

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 9 min read

Incense is the cheapest working tool in ceremonial practice and the most reliably skipped. A stick costs less than a coffee, burns for twenty minutes without supervision, and gives a working a shape that a diagram on paper never will. What follows is how to pick one, charge it, and read the result afterward without telling yourself a story that did not happen.

What Incense Actually Does in a Working

Smoke does three jobs in a ritual, and only one of them is mystical. The first is temporal. A stick that takes forty minutes to burn gives a working a defined length, which matters more than most beginners expect. Without a clock, a five-minute rite and a five-minute cup of tea feel identical from the inside. Incense puts an end on the working while you are still inside it.

The second is state. The smell arrives before the reasoning does, and olfactory memory is unusually durable and largely independent of language. You can forget the words of a working a year later and still recognise the room, and the hour, the moment you smelled it. Practitioners use that on purpose. One distinct scent per class of work means the scent itself becomes the index.

The third is the one people argue about. Incense carries an intention the way a signature carries a name. It does not amplify anything on its own, and it will not rescue a working built on a vague aim. What it does is mark the working as deliberate. The flame, the timing, the spoken words, the visible snuffing of the ember: those four actions in sequence are what make the hour different from the hour before it.

Where the Correspondence Tables Came From

Planets, metals, colours, numbers, days of the week: these groupings are the organising principle of Western ceremonial magic, and they are old. They arrive in printed form in the Three Books of Occult Philosophy, published in 1533, which was the first great printed synthesis of planetary, angelic and demonic hierarchies. Anyone working today is using a system that has been copied, corrected and expanded for nearly five centuries.

The system earns its keep as a memory device. When you sit down to build a working for a Mars question, the tables hand you a colour, a metal, a number and a day without you having to invent them. That is the real function. Correspondences are a prompt that stops you working in a vacuum.

Plants sit inside the same frame. Frankincense, myrrh, dragon's blood, copal, and cannabis all appear in religious and magical working material with documented use across Jewish, Christian, Hindu and folk practice. Cannabis in particular has a long record as an incense, and its legal status differs sharply between one jurisdiction and the next. Check the law where you live before anything else about the substance.

Choosing a Resin, a Powder, or a Stick

Three formats, three different behaviours. A stick smoulders quietly for thirty to sixty minutes and asks nothing of you. A powder takes a flame, flares hard, and burns out in under a minute. A resin needs a small charcoal disc and gives you the most control over intensity, because the amount is yours to decide.

For a short working on one question, powder suits the shape: the intent is stated, the material burns, the ember is snuffed, and you are done before the smoke thins. For a long evening with several items, a stick is easier and gives the room a steady background. For anything where you want to build and release gradually, resin on charcoal is the format built for it.

Cost matters less than people assume. A cheap supermarket stick burns adequately, and the difference between a ten-dollar box and a three-dollar box is usually scent rather than function. Where quality does show is in resins, where adulterated material smells thin and gives you a short, unsatisfying burn.

The Charge Sequence, Step by Step

Step five is the part that gets skipped, and it is the part that makes the structure. Putting out a flame you lit yourself is a physical mark that something was opened and something was closed. Skipping it leaves the working without its second half, and the second half is where the change is supposed to take hold.

  1. Write the intent as one sentence, in plain language, on paper. If you cannot write it in a sentence, the working is not ready.
  2. Light the incense or the charcoal. Wait for a steady flame rather than a flare.
  3. State the intent aloud, once, in the first person and the present tense.
  4. Pass the smoking material over the written sentence three times, left to right.
  5. Blow out or snuff the flame. Watch the ember go out. This is the release.
  6. Sit with the smoke until the room has changed, then stop.

Why People Reach for a Match Instead of a Candle

A candle is the more common choice and it is the weaker one for short workings. A candle flame is steady, bright and easy to ignore. It burns for hours whether or not you are paying attention, so nothing about it marks a beginning and an end.

A match gives you a small flame you can control, and it goes out when you want it to. When you strike it, hold it to the incense, then blow it out deliberately, you have performed a complete miniature version of the charge-then-release structure in about four seconds. That symmetry is why experienced practitioners reach for a match without thinking.

A candle earns its place in a different job. When you are working on several items, or the working is meant to sit overnight, or you want the material present for hours rather than minutes, a candle does the job better. Pick the tool that matches the length of the hour.

Keeping the Correspondences Useful Instead of Decorative

Most correspondence work in books is decorative. It lists Mars as red, iron, number five, Tuesday, and leaves it there. Listing is not using. The test is simple: can you say which planetary hour you are working in and why you chose it? Can you say which material you put on the charcoal as a result?

If the answer is no, the table is wallpaper. Push it one step further. Decide the hour first, then let the table choose the colour of the cloth, the metal of the ring on your hand, and the number of times you pass the smoke. One decision upstream, three decisions downstream, and the ritual suddenly has a spine.

The same move works for elements. A working about endings wants a smoke that moves air, not a heavy resin that sits. A working about clarity wants something sharp and thin. The correspondence is the argument for why this material, tonight, for this.

Diagnosing a Flat Result Without Fooling Yourself

Write down what you expect before you start. Not the desired outcome, which is too generous, but the specific physical sign you would accept as evidence. Heat in the hands. A room that suddenly feels smaller. A small external coincidence within the hour. Write it on the same sheet as the intent.

Then check the sheet afterwards instead of checking your memory. Memory is a poor instrument here, because hope edits it in real time. A sheet of paper does not edit.

Three repetitions with varied conditions is a fair trial. Change the hour, change the material, change the phrasing of the intent, and run it three times. If all three produce nothing you can name, the working is not being received. That is a useful result. It tells you the problem is the aim, the hour or your own attention, not the incense, and it tells you that before you spend another month on the same phrasing.

What to Do When the First Charge Is Weak

Do not escalate potency. The instinct after a flat result is to reach for a stronger material, and that is exactly backwards. A stronger smoke makes the ritual feel more serious without making it land.

Four changes are worth trying before anything else. Re-charge the same material, because a second charge on the same aim is a different act from a first one. Change the hour, because the table is an argument and you may have picked the wrong column. Simplify the intent to one concrete sentence and cut it in half again. And give the work a written anchor, a piece of paper you touch at the start and end.

If all four fail, stop. A working that has failed four different ways is telling you something specific, and the sensible response is to change the aim rather than the method.

Keeping a Working Supply Kit Small

A ballpoint pen, a sheet of paper, matches, one box of sticks and an hour when nobody wants anything from you. That is a complete kit. Everything else is optional and most of it is decoration bought to make the shelf look serious.

The hour is the part people cannot buy. A working interrupted at the door is not a working, and no amount of equipment compensates for being reachable. If you can protect twenty uninterrupted minutes, you can do this work. If you cannot, the equipment is not the bottleneck.

Keep a log. One line per working: date, hour, material, intent in five words, and the sign you actually saw. A year of that log is worth more than a shelf of resins, because it is the only record that shows you which changes your practice and which ones you only felt.

Where This Sits in a Longer Practice

Incense is a support structure, not a practice on its own. It gives a working a length, a scent and a closing action. It does not give the working an aim, and it will not supply the one thing that makes a working worth doing, which is a reason you actually care about the outcome.

Used that way it ages well. The log thickens, the materials get cheaper as you learn which ones you reach for, and the ritual shrinks from an event into a habit. That is the direction you want. A practice you look forward to survives longer than a practice you can only approach with preparation.

The other half of a longer practice is the record, and the record is what turns a repeated gesture into something that can be examined. Write the hour down. Write the sign down. In a year you will have data where most people have impressions, and data is what lets you change your mind when you need to.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do the planetary correspondences matter mechanically?

No. A table linking Mars to a colour, a metal and a weekday does not make anything happen. What it does is hand you a consistent set of choices so that a working has a structure instead of an improvisation, and so that you can repeat it and compare the results. Treat the tables as a decision-making tool, not a mechanism, and stop worrying about whether the number five matters.

Do I need expensive ritual tools to do this properly?

No. A pen, a sheet of paper, a box of matches and twenty uninterrupted minutes will carry you further than any copper incense burner. Equipment improves the aesthetic of the working and has no effect on its result. The one thing worth spending money on is a private hour, and that is not something a shop sells. Protect the time first and buy the resin later.

Why does everyone say to use a match instead of a candle?

Because a match gives you a small flame you can put out on purpose, which is a complete miniature version of the charge-then-release structure in about four seconds. A candle burns for hours whether or not you are paying attention, so nothing about it marks a beginning and an end. When a working is long or left overnight, a candle is the better tool, so the rule is not absolute.

How many times can I re-charge the same stick of incense?

Three, and rarely more than that. Each charge is a distinct act, so three charges make a short sequence rather than a repetition. After that the stick is spent and a fresh one gives you a clearer result, because a nearly finished stick burns thin and the thinness can be mistaken for a weak working. Write the number of charges on the wrapper if you lose track.

Is it safe to burn resin or incense indoors?

Ventilate properly and keep the charcoal dish on a heatproof surface with air moving under it, because resin smoulders for a long time without any visible flame. Keep every burning item away from curtains, paper, pets and children, and never leave resin unattended. If you have asthma or a smoke sensitivity, choose a stick or a powder instead and work near an open window.
