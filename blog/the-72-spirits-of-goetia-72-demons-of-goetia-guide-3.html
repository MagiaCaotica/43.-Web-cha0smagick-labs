The 72 Spirits of Goetia

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 9 min read

This is a field report rather than a reference, and the difference matters. Over four weeks I worked with three goetic spirits chosen by their stated domains rather than by reputation, kept a written record of every sitting including the ones that produced nothing useful, and tried to stay honest about which parts of the experience were structural and which were expectation. The corpus is fascinating and the modern presentation of it is mostly marketing, so the only way to say anything worth hearing is to show the workings with their failures still in. Three spirits, three domains, twenty-eight days, forty-one recorded observations, and one conclusion that surprised me. Read it as a description of what tends to happen, not as a claim that anything supernatural occurred.

What this series of workings set out to check

The question I wanted to answer was narrow: does the classical structure of the material produce better results than the popular presentation of the same material. Popular versions sell the spirits as terrors who must be propitiated. Classical versions describe officials with administrative domains. If the structure is doing real work, the classical reading should be the more functional one to work with.

I also wanted to avoid the trap that makes this field unwritable, which is treating every outcome as a hit. So I fixed the terms in advance, wrote down what each working was supposed to be for, and logged anything that happened without editing the entry afterwards. A record you improve as you go is a record of your memory, not of the work.

The rules I fixed before starting

Four rules, all written down on day one. Choose by stated domain, never by reputation or by which spirit sounds most impressive. Keep each working to a single stated purpose. Never extend a working past its original window because it is nearly producing something. And stop the whole series immediately if it started interfering with sleep, work, or relationships.

The last rule is the one people skip and the one that matters. The material carries a real risk of becoming absorbing, not because the spirits are dangerous but because the ambiguity of any sitting is infinitely expandable. Short daily practice beats escalating intensity, and that principle held up under the exact conditions it was written for.

Choosing by domain rather than reputation

The goetic material assigns each spirit a domain, and those assignments are the most functional part of the corpus. They are drawn from a scheme in which these beings were administrative or disciplinary officials, not torturers of the innocent. Nine of them are Presidents, thirty-six are Dukes, and thirty are Kings, arranged in small houses of two, three, or four.

I picked three that both the classical text and the popular version agree are about learning, communication, and finding what has been lost. Choosing this way removed almost all the friction that comes from selecting a famous name and then discovering you have no use for its domain. Nobody is going to be impressed by a working whose purpose you could not state in one sentence.

  1. Read the stated domain, not the reputation attached to the name.
  2. Write the purpose as one sentence before you begin.
  3. Reject any spirit whose domain does not touch a real task in your week.
  4. Note the rank, since the classical text treats it as meaningful.

The first working, and what actually happened

The first sitting was the least dramatic and by far the most useful. I reduced a sentence about finding a document I had misfiled, constructed a seal, sat with it briefly, dismissed it, and then went and looked in the place I would not have looked. The document was there. The document was there because I had not looked in that drawer in four months, and the working gave me a reason to look at it now.

That is the whole mechanism, visible in the open. Nothing supernatural occurred. What occurred is that a vague intention to sort the filing became a specific intention to check one drawer, and the specific intention survived until I acted on it. I logged it as a hit with a note that the causal path ran entirely through my own behaviour, because that note is what makes the entry trustworthy six weeks later.

The second working, where the seal did most of the work

The second sitting was aimed at communication and produced a change I still cannot fully account for. A conversation I had been avoiding happened, unprompted, because the working had forced me to write down exactly what I wanted to say and how I wanted to say it. Once it was written, avoiding it became harder than saying it.

The seal itself was not the active element here. Sitting down to write the statement was. This is consistent with the rest of the series and with what the classical sources actually describe, which is a domain of activity rather than a personality to be negotiated with. I would not describe this as the spirit doing anything. I would describe it as specification doing something, which is less romantic and considerably more portable.

The third working, which produced nothing I could use

The third was a miss, and the miss was more informative than either hit. I aimed it at a problem where I had no clear next action available, only a general dissatisfaction. The sitting went fine. Fourteen days later nothing had changed and nothing had become clearer.

Reading back through my own entries, the pattern was obvious. The two workings that produced movement both named a specific thing I could do that week. The one that produced nothing named a state I wanted to be in. Encoding a state gives you a symbol and no action, so the practice had nothing to attach to. That is a repeatable finding, and it is one I would not have reached by reasoning alone.

Where the failures clustered

Three failure modes showed up, and none of them was supernatural. The first was vague framing, which produces a symbol without an action. The second was domain shopping, the habit of switching spirits when a working stalls, which guarantees a miss and destroys the record. The third was late editing, going back to an entry days later to add a detail that flatteringly explains the outcome.

All three are ordinary errors in ordinary self-measurement. That is the most defensible finding in the series. The mechanics of this material are not the difficult part. The difficult part is being an honest observer of your own behaviour, and that difficulty is shared with every other tradition that asks you to test claims you would like to be true.

  • Vague framing, meaning the intent named a feeling rather than a task.
  • Domain shopping, meaning the spirit was changed after a disappointing result.
  • Late editing, meaning entries were revised once the outcome was known.
  • Window stretching, meaning a working was quietly extended past its date.

What the app was and was not good for

I used a reference app for the classical text and it earned its place in one specific way. Having the seal, the stated domain, and the rank on a single screen removed the opportunity to drift, which was the failure mode that had already cost me one working. The constraint was useful in the same way a narrow menu is useful.

It was not good at interpretation, and no reference work could be. The domains are stated in terms broad enough that almost any intention can be bent to fit, and a tool that makes that bending easy is a tool that will help you talk yourself into a match. The function of the classical text is to narrow the fitting, not to widen it, and the reading is the part worth doing by hand.

The moment the classical framing started to matter

Around the third week something shifted, and it was not a change in what I experienced. It was a change in how I read the entries. Once I stopped treating the spirits as agents with wills and started treating the domains as categories of activity that had been misread for centuries, the material became usable in a way it had not been before.

The popular presentation pushed these beings out of their classical context, where they were administrative or disciplinary officials rather than torturers of the innocent. That framing is the reason so much modern material is unusable. Once you restore the office, you get something you can actually act on, and the fear-based framing that sells the subject becomes unnecessary.

What I would and would not repeat

I would repeat the structure: one purpose, one spirit chosen by domain, a fixed window, a written record, no changes made after the outcome is known. That structure produced two usable results out of three, which is a far better rate than I get from unstructured intention-setting and costs almost nothing.

I would not repeat the ambition. I began expecting the classical framing to unlock something, and it did not. It unlocked better method, which is less satisfying and more valuable. The expectation was the least disciplined part of the whole exercise, and it is the part the tradition most encourages without ever saying so.

The finding I did not expect

The unexpected result was that the record mattered more than the ritual. Nothing in the series produced an effect I could not also produce by writing the purpose down and doing the thing. What the record did, and what the ritual did not, was make that visible to me at the time, which changed how often I actually did the thing.

This is not a debunking. It is a relocation. The goetic corpus is a very good catalogue of domains, a very old filing system, and a poor source of instructions. Used as a filing system it earns its place. Used as a set of instructions it produces exactly as many unsatisfying sittings as the popular material promises it should.

What the record has to contain to be worth anything

For a series this small, six fields are enough: the date, the stated purpose in one sentence, the spirit and its stated domain, what you did during the sitting, what happened inside the window, and what you concluded at the end. Nothing else is needed and nothing else will be read.

The sixth field is the one that gets skipped and the only one that makes the other five worth keeping. A conclusion written at the end, while you still remember roughly what you were hoping for, is honest enough to be corrected later. A conclusion written a month after is a story. Keep the field small and fill it in on the day, every time, even when the entry is dull and especially when the entry is disappointing.

  1. Date, written as a full date rather than a weekday.
  2. Purpose, as one imperative sentence naming an action.
  3. Spirit and stated domain, so the choice is auditable later.
  4. What happened, and the conclusion, both recorded on the day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which demon in the list is associated with money?

Three names come up and they are not interchangeable. Mammon is the king most directly associated with wealth, Clauneck is a duke whose stated claim is granting riches, and Paimon is associated with dignities, which is rank and standing rather than cash. If you are choosing by domain, take the two the text names for riches and leave the third out of a first series.

Who are the nine Presidents in the classical listing?

The nine Presidents are Bael, Agares, Vassago, Samigina, Marbas, Valefor, Aamon, Belphegor and Saleos. The rank is not decorative in the original scheme, where these were senior figures among administrative beings. Note that popular lists frequently disagree with printed editions on the count and the grouping, so work from a specific text rather than from a summary.

Are the Goetia spirits demons in any real sense?

Not in the way the popular material implies. These names reach the grimoire through the seventeenth-century conjuration text and the Lemuria, with much of the older name material drawn from Jewish and Christian demonological sources such as the Book of Enoch. In the classical scheme they held office with assigned domains. Calling them demons describes where the material came from, not what they are or what they want.

Where does the money go in a paid version of this?

Into a better reference. A purchased grimoire app is worth having when it puts the classical text, the stated domains, and the seal on one screen without a connection, because that removes the chance of drifting into a tradition you did not choose. It does not do anything a printed book will not, and the interpretive work stays yours in either case.

What should I do if a working starts affecting my sleep?

Stop, and treat that as a real signal rather than a sign that something is happening. This material is most likely to become absorbing through ambiguity, not through any agency of the beings involved. Reduce the intensity, shorten the window, and return to a short daily practice rather than escalating. If sleep does not recover, the practice needs to stop and the underlying worry addressed properly.
