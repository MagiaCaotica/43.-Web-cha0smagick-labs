The Mechanism: Direction as Decision Filter

The Law of Direction: When you have clear direction, most decisions make themselves. Without direction, every choice is a dilemma — you deliberate, weigh pros/cons, oscillate, fatigue. With direction, choices are evaluated against a single criterion: Does this move me toward or away from my direction?

The Mechanism: Direction = Decision Filter. The brain processes ~35,000 decisions/day. Without a filter, each decision consumes PFC resources (willpower). With a direction filter, decisions matching the direction = automatic yes (basal ganglia automation). Decisions contrary = automatic no (no deliberation needed). Neutral decisions = evaluated against values hierarchy. The direction filter offloads 80%+ of decisions from PFC to basal ganglia automation.

The Direction Equation: Decision Speed = Direction Clarity × Values Alignment. High clarity + high alignment = sub-second decisions. Low clarity = deliberation paralysis. The Direction Framework builds both clarity and alignment systematically.

The Direction Equation: Decision = Filter(Direction, Values). Same inputs + clear filter = fast decision. Same inputs + no filter = deliberation paralysis. Direction is the filter; Values are the tiebreaker.

Phase 1: The Purpose Audit — 10 Core Statements

You cannot have direction without purpose. This audit takes 45 minutes and produces your 10 core purpose statements.

Step 1: The Raw Generation (15 minutes)

Set a timer. Write 20 statements answering: "What am I here to do?" No editing. No judgment. Just generate.

Step 2: The Resonance Rating (10 minutes)

Rate each statement 1-10 for visceral resonance. Not intellectual agreement — visceral resonance. The feeling in your chest/gut when you read it.

  • 1-3: Discard (not yours, borrowed, should)
  • 4-6: Archive (peripheral, supportive)
  • 7-8: Keep (aligned, true)
  • 9-10: CORE (visceral resonance, non-negotiable)

Step 3: The Core 10 Selection (10 minutes)

Select exactly 10 statements rated 9-10. If fewer than 10, keep the highest 8-9s. If more than 10, force-rank and cut.

PURPOSE AUDIT TEMPLATE: ======================== CORE PURPOSE STATEMENTS (Rate 1-10, keep 9-10 only): 1. _____________________________________________________ | ___ 2. _____________________________________________________ | ___ 3. _____________________________________________________ | ___ 4. _____________________________________________________ | ___ 5. _____________________________________________________ | ___ 6. _____________________________________________________ | ___ 7. _____________________________________________________ | ___ 8. _____________________________________________________ | ___ 9. _____________________________________________________ | ___ 10. _____________________________________________________ | ___ ARCHIVED (7-8): _____________________________________________ DISCARDED (1-6): ____________________________________________

Step 4: The Purpose Synthesis (5 minutes)

Synthesize your 10 core statements into 3 purpose pillars. These are your identity-level anchors.

PURPOSE PILLARS: ================ PILLAR 1 (Identity): I am someone who ___________________________________ PILLAR 2 (Contribution): I create _______________________________________ PILLAR 3 (Legacy): I leave _____________________________________________

Phase 2: The Values Hierarchy — 7 Decision Criteria

Purpose gives direction. Values give the decision criteria. This hierarchy becomes your decision engine.

Step 1: The Values Generation (10 minutes)

List 20 values. Not "what I should value" — what you ACTUALLY optimize for. Look at your calendar, bank statement, browser history. What do your actions reveal you value?

Step 2: The Forced Ranking (15 minutes)

Force-rank all 20. No ties. This is painful but necessary. The top 7 become your decision criteria. The top 3 become your automatic veto/approval triggers.

VALUES HIERARCHY TEMPLATE: ========================== 1. _____________________________________________________ (VETO TRIGGER) 2. _____________________________________________________ (VETO TRIGGER) 3. _____________________________________________________ (VETO TRIGGER) 4. _____________________________________________________ 5. _____________________________________________________ 6. _____________________________________________________ 7. _____________________________________________________ 8-20: ___________________________________________________

The Decision Engine

Your top 3 values = automatic filters:

  • Decision violates Value #1 → Automatic NO (no deliberation)
  • Decision violates Value #2 → Automatic NO (no deliberation)
  • Decision violates Value #3 → Automatic NO (no deliberation)
  • Decision aligns with Values 1-3 → Automatic YES
  • Decision neutral on Values 1-3 → Evaluate against Values 4-7
  • Decision aligns with Values 4-7 → YES
  • Decision violates Values 4-7 → EVALUATE (rare)
The Values Engine: Top 3 = Veto power. Top 7 = Decision criteria. The hierarchy IS the decision engine. No deliberation needed when values speak.

Phase 3: The Direction Statement — One Sentence Compass

Purpose (Why) + Values (How) = Direction (Where). The Direction Statement synthesizes both into a single navigational sentence.

The Direction Statement Formula

DIRECTION STATEMENT FORMULA: ============================= "My direction is [CORE PURPOSE], guided by [VALUE 1], [VALUE 2], [VALUE 3], toward [SPECIFIC 12-MONTH HORIZON]." EXAMPLE: "My direction is building autonomous value-creation systems, guided by autonomy, leverage, and compounding, toward a $500k/year portfolio requiring <10 hrs/week maintenance by December 2026." RULES: - One sentence. Memorizable. Sayable in one breath. - Core Purpose = from Purpose Pillars - Values = Top 3 from hierarchy - Horizon = Specific, measurable, 12-month, within your influence - NO outcome guarantees. Direction = vector, not destination.

The Direction Statement as Daily Compass

Memorize it. Say it each morning. Use it as the filter for every decision:

  • Morning: Recite direction statement. Sets the day's filter.
  • Decision point: "Does this move me toward my direction?" → Filter applied.
  • Evening: "Did today's actions serve my direction?" → Audit loop.

Direction is a hypothesis, not a vow. The quarterly audit detects when the hypothesis is falsified.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Direction Framework simplify decisions?

The Direction Framework creates a single filter: 'Does this move me toward my direction?' Decisions matching the direction = automatic yes. Decisions contrary = automatic no. Decisions neutral = evaluate against values hierarchy. This eliminates decision fatigue by replacing deliberation with filtering.

How often should I audit my direction?

Weekly micro-audit (5 min): Is today aligned? Monthly review (30 min): Is the direction still resonant? Quarterly deep audit (2 hours): Does the direction still serve my evolving purpose? The loop compounds — weekly prevents drift, monthly corrects course, quarterly evolves direction.

What if my direction changes?

Direction is a hypothesis, not a vow. The quarterly audit exists precisely to detect when the hypothesis is falsified. If data contradicts direction → update direction. The framework is designed for evolution, not rigidity. The only failure is not auditing.

How specific should the direction be?

Specific enough to filter decisions, flexible enough to survive contact with reality. 'Build a $10M business' = too outcome-dependent. 'Build systems that generate value for [specific audience]' = direction. Direction filters decisions; goals measure progress along the direction.

What's the difference between purpose, direction, and goals?

Purpose = Why (identity level, stable). Direction = Where (vector, evolves quarterly). Goals = What (milestones, measurable, serve direction). Purpose anchors direction; direction guides goals; goals measure progress. The framework aligns all three.
