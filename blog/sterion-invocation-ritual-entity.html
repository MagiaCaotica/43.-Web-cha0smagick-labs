STERION

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 9 min read

Sterion is a name that appears in the later goetic literature, in the register of small, sharply described figures rather than the broad sovereign entries. That is exactly why it is worth a single-entity study. A broad entry can be worked from reputation and produces nothing you can evaluate. A narrow one has to be worked from its stated office, and the process of doing that teaches the method far better than any general guide. What follows takes one name and works it all the way through: reading the entry, understanding what the seal is doing, what the classical vocabulary was protecting, how a modern approach relaxes the framework without abandoning the structure, and what a working actually looks like when it is specified in enough detail to repeat and log. Written for someone who would rather understand one entry properly than browse seventy-two.

Reading a single entry before working with it

A goetic entry is short and every clause in it is doing work. Rank, visible form, office, and the list of permitted actions are four separate claims, and the differences between them matter. Rank is standing. Form is a description intended to be drawn. Office is what the figure is for. Permitted actions are the boundary of the request.

Read the entry as a specification rather than as a biography. There is no backstory in it, and the urge to supply one is where most of the imaginative material about these figures comes from. Nothing in the entry supports a personality, and inventing one gives you a figure you will project onto in a way the text never licensed.

Then check it against a second source. The entries vary between printings, sometimes in the stated form and sometimes in the permitted actions, and the variation is informative. Where two versions disagree, the earlier one is generally the closer to the compiled material, and the later ones tend to expand.

The distinction the vocabulary was built to protect

In classical ceremonial usage, evocation means calling a spirit so that it appears, and invocation means inviting its influence to operate through the operator. The distinction is between a presence and a function, and most modern usage collapses it into a single undifferentiated act of calling.

This matters more than it sounds. Evocation implies apparatus, a circle, a medium, a visible appearance, and a demand on your own state that is why practitioners who are not prepared for it should not attempt it. Invocation implies none of that. You are working with a pattern, and the pattern operates through you.

The second is the one that suits almost all modern practice, including everything in this piece, and it is also the one that carries much less risk. If you find yourself building apparatus for a contact you did not want, you have drifted into the first tradition without deciding to.

What the classical catalogue gives you that a name does not

The catalogue is the most structured entity list in the Western grimoire tradition, and its structure is the useful part rather than the contents. A ranked, described, bounded collection of figures is a specification format. It tells you what to record, what to ask for, and what you are not asking for.

That format is what makes a working auditable. Because the entry names an office, you can check afterwards whether the contact you recorded matches the office. A vague figure gives you nothing to check against, and an experience with nothing to check against cannot accumulate into knowledge.

The weakness of the format is that it invites over-reading. When every figure has a stated form, it becomes easy to read the form as a description of a being rather than as a template for a drawing. Keep the distinction visible in your notes and the material stays usable.

What the modern reading gives up, and what it keeps

Modern practice treats these figures as independent pattern fragments, and the value of that framing is that it does not require a theology. A pattern can be invoked without a claim about its ontology. You can work with a named figure, get useful results, and never once assert that anything external answered.

That is the position that makes this material safe to use without a licence, and it is worth being explicit that it is a choice rather than a proof. The classical framework makes the same material harder and more dangerous, and buys nothing that the modern framing cannot supply for the kind of work most people are actually doing.

The most useful working definition is Carroll's, that these figures are best taken to be facets of the practitioner's own mind. On that view, working with an entry is a way of accessing a mode of your own attention that you do not have ordinary access to, and the result is a capacity rather than a contact. It also neutralises most of the risk without requiring you to become a sceptic about anything.

Picking by stated office, never by fame

With a single entry, the selection question is narrower but the same principle applies. Write your question as one sentence with a named outcome and a timescale, then check whether the entry's stated office contains that situation. If it does not, no amount of persuasion will make the working do what you want.

Reputation is the worst selector in the list and this is the clearest case of that, because a single narrow entry is most likely to be known for reasons unrelated to the its office. If you recognise the name and cannot say what the description says, that is the answer.

If your question genuinely does not fit, the honest response is to say so and choose a different tool, not a different entity. Most disappointing first workings in this material are selection failures recorded as working failures.

The seal, and what it is actually doing

The seal binds a name to a single occasion. Without it, invoking a figure is just using a word, and a word is used for a hundred things. The seal makes the contact specific, which is what makes it possible to judge afterwards whether anything happened.

Construct it the same way every time, from the same materials, in the same order. A seal you improvise per working makes each working unique, and uniqueness is the opposite of what you want from a comparison. Keep the drawing somewhere private and out of circulation, for the mundane reason that an object you would not show anyone is a commitment.

The classical texts also specify the hours and the material, and the specification is worth following once so that you know what it is doing, then dropping where it is pure form. Form can be learned and then set aside. The binding function cannot be improvised.

  • One sheet, one instrument, the same order of strokes, every time.
  • The question written beside the seal so the binding is visible in the record.
  • Stored somewhere private, and not photographed or shared.

The working, specified in parts

Write the contact down before you begin. One paragraph, saying what you are asking for, what you are offering, how long you intend to be in contact, and what ends it. This is not ceremony. It is the document you will read in the log afterwards, and a working without written terms produces an experience you cannot evaluate.

Then do the three parts in order. Open by stating the terms back, so that any failure to meet them is visible. Make the call, naming the office and repeating your sentence as logged rather than as improved. Close by ending the contact explicitly and putting the seal away, not beside you.

The closing is the part people skip and the part that causes the difficulty. Leaving a contact nominally open is the documented route to a working that seemed fine and then was not. End it the same way you would end a phone call, deliberately, and note the time.

The record, and the three lines that matter

Three lines in the log carry most of the value: the question as written, the time the contact opened and closed, and one sentence describing what you noticed before you interpreted anything. Everything else can be added later if you remember it. These three cannot be reconstructed later at all.

Write the interpretation on a separate line, and only after the description. This single formatting habit is what makes a goetic log readable a year later, because it keeps what happened separate from what you thought it meant. Most of the noise in this genre comes from those two things being written as one.

Then fix the review date. A specific number of weeks, decided now, at which you will read the entry and ask whether the question got answered. The date is the part that turns a series of experiences into a practice, and it is the part that is always left open.

Failure modes, in the order they arrive

The first is the ambiguous question. It cannot be written down, so no entry can be judged to have worked. The second is the wrong entry, chosen for recognition rather than for its stated office. The third is the improved wording at the point of the call, when the request drifts away from the logged sentence.

The fourth is skipping the close. The fifth is filtering the record, keeping the strange entry and discarding the dull one, which over a year leaves you with a diary of the unusual and no information about the rate. All five are ordinary and all five are visible in a log that was kept properly.

The sixth is escalation, which is the only one with real cost. A disappointing result tempts a bigger request, a longer contact, a more elaborate seal. Reduce intensity instead. A shorter daily practice is almost always the better response, and the temptation to escalate is the point at which this material starts costing more than it returns.

One first working, specified

Pick something with no person in it. A document you need to find, a habit you want to establish, a question about a skill you are trying to learn. Nothing about another human being, nothing with a deadline attached to somebody else, nothing irreversible.

Run it once, on a dull day, at a modest hour, with the written terms and the seal already prepared. Expect nothing in particular and log whatever happens, including the sentence that says nothing happened. Set the review date at the end rather than the beginning, so the closing is unambiguous.

Then repeat it on a second cycle with one thing changed, and only one. Change the question, or the time, or the entry. Changing all three tells you nothing, because you will have four possible explanations and no way to separate them. Two cycles is the minimum that produces a comparison, and most people find it more useful than twenty improvised ones.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Sterion claim to be able to do?

Read the entry rather than the reputation. What matters is the stated office and the list of permitted actions, and those are the parts of the text that make a working with this entry auditable. If your question is not contained in the office wording, no result is possible and the problem is selection rather than the figure.

What is the difference between invoking and evoking?

In classical usage, evocation calls a spirit so that it appears, which implies apparatus, a visible form and a demand on your own state. Invocation invites its influence to operate through you. The second suits almost all modern practice, carries much less risk, and is what everything in this piece uses.

Is working with a named figure risky in practice?

The risks are ordinary rather than supernatural. Interference with sleep, work or relationships, escalation after a disappointing result, and letting a contact stay nominally open. All three are managed by short daily practice, a written question, and a deliberate close, and none of them require a sceptic to avoid them.

How many workings should I run before deciding anything?

Two, with one variable changed between them, is the minimum that produces a comparison you can read. Most disappointing first workings are spent learning the sequence, so treat the first as apprenticeship rather than as evidence. If two consecutive cycles produce nothing describable, audit the selection before you audit the entry.

What am I paying for in an investigation app for this?

The material itself is centuries old and costs nothing to read. What a paid tool buys is the evidence discipline the method depends on: sealed entries with the entry text beside the reading you took from it, a timestamped log that cannot be quietly revised later, the review date that holds you to a stopping rule you set in advance, and a saved history for comparing the second cycle against the first.
