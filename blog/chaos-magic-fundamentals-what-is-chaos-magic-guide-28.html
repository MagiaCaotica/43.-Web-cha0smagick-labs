Chaos Magic Fundamentals

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 27, 2026 ⬢ 9 min read

The chaos magic scene has no certification, and the reason is structural rather than an oversight. A movement that dissolved doctrine did not leave behind a body of claims that can be tested, so there is nothing on which an exam could be based. What it left instead is permission, and permission plus confidence plus a low entry price is a very good environment for people who will say anything. This is not a criticism of the method, which is genuinely good, and it is not a claim that the people involved are dishonest. It is a description of a gap, and the gap is closable with three questions that anyone can ask.

The structural problem, stated in one line

The movement emerged in the early 1970s, and the conventional starting point is the founding of an order in 1976. The two documents everybody actually reads appeared in the same period. Both of them insisted on experiment over doctrine, and the name was a deliberate double gesture, playing on the word chaos while signalling an affiliation with a much older motif.

The contribution that has lasted is methodological rather than metaphysical, and it is the reason the field has been unusually open to honest failure. It made it acceptable to test a claim rather than inherit it. That is a real gain and it is the one worth defending. The cost is that when there is no inherited doctrine, there is also no shared standard of mastery, and a field with no standard of mastery will produce confident teachers whether or not anyone has earned the confidence.

No doctrine, and no falsifiable claim of mastery

Here is the mechanism in full. A claim of mastery has to name something the master can do that a beginner cannot. Without a doctrine, there is no such thing to name. The most that can be claimed is that the person has done it longer, or more intensely, or with more lineages behind them, and those are not claims about skill. They are claims about volume.

Volume does not scale into competence on its own. Some of the people with the longest histories in this field have nothing to show beyond accumulated practice, and some of the most technically sharp people in it arrived recently and hold no lineage at all. Both are consistent with the structure. The absence of a testable claim of mastery is not a scandal in this field. It is the direct consequence of the design decision that made the field valuable in the first place.

The three tests that actually work

If there is no certification, there are substitutes, and three of them are cheap to apply. Ask about money. Ask about failure modes. Ask about a record. Each of these produces very different answers from a practitioner and from a person selling a product, and each of them can be asked in a single conversation without any confrontational framing at all.

The reason these three work and others do not is that they ask for specific information rather than for self-description. Claims about lineage, tradition, or authority are all self-reports and therefore uninformative. Money, failure modes, and records are all external, and the external is where a claim can actually be checked. You do not need to be hostile. You just need to ask the questions that have answers with the wrong shape in them.

The test about money

Ask what the person charges and what they promise in exchange, then check whether the two are the same kind of thing. Payment for instruction is ordinary. Payment for a guarantee of an outcome is a different transaction, and it is the one worth walking away from, because a guarantee requires the seller to have more control over the outcome than anyone can have.

The arrangement that should end the conversation is money in exchange for a claimed result. Everything else has defensible versions. A subscription for lessons is defensible even if the lessons are mediocre. A course is defensible. Personalised attention at a high hourly rate is defensible and you may still decline it. What is not defensible is a price attached to an outcome, because the person charging it either believes they control the outcome or knows they do not, and in both cases the buyer is being sold something that will not arrive.

The test about failure modes

Ask how they know when something has failed, and listen to whether the answer contains a category of results that would count as failure. A practitioner who has done this work for any length of time has a list, and the list is specific. The aim did not happen. The timing was wrong. The follow-through did not occur. The person found a better method. Those are failures with causes, and they are the mark of someone who has looked.

The absence of that list is the finding, and it is worth more than any credential. When every outcome can be read as partial success, the practice cannot fail and therefore cannot teach, and a person who cannot describe a failure has not been doing this long enough to have one. This is the single most useful question in the field and it takes about forty seconds to ask.

The test about a record

Ask to see something written down from before a result was known. Not a success story, not a retrospective account, not a chart produced afterwards. Something with a date on it, made before the outcome existed, that anyone could read now and say whether it was borne out.

This is a hard question and almost nobody passes it cleanly, which is worth saying in advance so that you do not treat one unprepared person as evidence. A person with a notebook from last year is unusual and should be treated as notable. A person who describes their process carefully but has nothing written down has still taught you something, and what they have taught you is that their conclusions are reconstructions. Both answers are informative and one of them is better.

The specific arrangements that recur

A pattern shows up repeatedly and it is worth being able to name it. The offer is access, initiation, or transmission that is nominally scarce and actually available on payment. The scarcity is manufactured by describing an ordinary introduction as a rite. The promised benefit is a transformation rather than a skill, which is the tell, because transformations cannot be assessed and skills can.

The second recurring arrangement is the totalising system. Everything the student has done before is reframed as a preliminary stage of this one thing, which converts any prior scepticism into a stage of the seller's own method. The third is the isolation move, where doubting is characterised as a low level of development rather than as a reasonable response to a claim. None of these requires a false statement to work, which is what makes them durable.

Certification, and why it is unavailable

There is no certifying body in this field and there is not going to be one, for structural reasons rather than institutional failure. A certification requires a body of agreed knowledge, an agreed standard of competence, and a mechanism for disqualifying people. This field deliberately declined the first of those, and the other two cannot be built out of nothing.

This is not a defect to be fixed by good organisation, and it is worth resisting the urge to propose one. A certification for a practice whose founding documents insist that claims should be tested rather than inherited would be a contradiction, and the people best placed to build one are the people whose commitment to the method rules it out. The absence is the price of the thing that is worth having, and the honest response is to use local tests rather than to hope for an institution.

What lineage claims are worth

Lineage claims are almost worthless as evidence of competence and are not entirely worthless as information. They tell you where someone came from and whom they consider their teachers, and that is genuinely useful context because different teachers in this field really do teach different things. What they do not tell you is whether the person can do anything.

The specific problem is that lineage is trivially transferable. Anyone can be given a lineage, and in a field with no central authority, nothing prevents it. So the claim carries almost no verifying weight, and the people who make it most emphatically are frequently the ones with the least verifiable record. Treat lineage as a way of knowing what school someone is in, which is interesting, and not as a way of knowing whether to pay them.

The one question that ends most of it

Here is the question. What result are you able to point at, and what would have happened if you had not done the work? Asked plainly, this is a request for a counterfactual, and the counterfactual is the thing nobody can supply. If they answer with a number, ask where the number came from before and after. If they answer with a feeling, ask what else was happening that year.

This works because it does not accuse anybody of anything. It is a normal question to ask, it has an objective answer available to anyone doing real work, and the person who cannot answer it usually does not realise that the question is technical rather than hostile. Used this way, a handful of these conversations will do more to protect you than any amount of reading about warning signs.

Where genuine authority does exist

It exists locally, it is small, and it looks nothing like what people expect. The people worth learning from in this field are usually the ones with modest aims, dated records, a stated failure list, and no interest in being thought of as masters. Some of them teach and some do not, and the ones who do not are frequently the better teachers, because a person with nothing to sell has no reason to overstate.

Authority of this kind is verifiable in a way that lineage is not. You can check the dates. You can ask about the failures. You can see whether the aims have stayed small. And when the answers hold up over several conversations, that is as much authority as this field can support, which is not much and is not supposed to be. The field's best quality is that it does not let anyone claim more than the evidence carries, and the people worth working with respect that.

The position to hold

Take the method seriously and the titles not at all. The methodological contribution of this tradition is real: test your claims, keep a record, let a bad month count, keep the aims small enough to check. That is a good practice and it applies to the teacher as much as to the student, which is the whole argument.

On the question of what to pay for and who to believe, use the three local tests and refuse anything priced on outcome. On the question of authority, look for dated records and named failures rather than transmissions and initiations. And on the question of your own conclusions, keep them small. A method that dissolves doctrine also dissolves the person who claims to know more than the evidence supports, and that is not a temporary inconvenience. It is the design working as intended.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I tell a genuine practitioner from a charlatan in this field?

Use the three tests. Ask what they charge and whether the price is attached to a result rather than to instruction. Ask how they know something failed, and listen for a specific list of failure modes. Ask for something written down from before a result was known. None of these is hostile, all three have answers with the right or wrong shape, and the third is the most decisive when it is available.

Is there any certification or formal authority in chaos magic?

No, and there is not going to be one. A certification needs a body of agreed knowledge, which this field deliberately declined, and from that the other requirements cannot be built. Anyone proposing a certifying body is proposing something that contradicts the founding method. Use local tests with specific individuals rather than waiting for an institution that will not arrive.

Does the lack of doctrine mean the field is nonsense?

No, and the causal story runs the other way. The same decision that left no doctrine is what made the field unusually honest about failure and unusually open to testing claims. What it also did was remove any testable claim of mastery, which is why confident teachers proliferate. Both of those are consequences of the same design and you have to take the second along with the first.

What about lineage and initiations, are those worth nothing?

Worth something, but not as evidence of competence. Lineage tells you which school somebody came from and that is real information, because teachers in this field genuinely differ. It carries no verifying weight because it is trivially transferable and there is no central authority to check it. Treat it as a way of knowing what someone believes and not as a reason to pay them.

How should I price spending on courses or coaching?

Instruction is a defensible purchase and a guarantee is not. A course, a subscription for lessons, and hourly time with an experienced practitioner are all ordinary transactions. What to decline is any arrangement priced on the outcome, since that requires the seller to control something nobody controls. Beyond that, use the failure-modes question as your main filter, because it costs nothing and sorts well.
