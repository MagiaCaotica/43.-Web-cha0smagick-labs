Why South America Appears Obsessed With Witchcraft

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 10 min read

The observer's claim is that the region is unusually preoccupied with magic. The more useful and much better supported claim is that South American magic is unusually visible, and that the visibility is a property of the archive rather than of the population. A colonial apparatus recorded it, a scholarly apparatus preserved it, and a set of practices that keep written liturgy and formal roles has been continuously documented for a very long time. An outsider sampling a large consistent corpus and concluding that the continent has a temperament is a mistake worth examining, because the same corpus contains a working idea that most of this site would benefit from adopting.

The word doing the work is the problem

Obsessed is an attribution. It takes a quantity of visible practice and converts it into a characteristic of the people doing it, which is the same move that made European observers describe African and American practice as superstition and then treat the description as a finding about the people rather than about the record.

The documentary trail is unusually good, and that is the whole explanation for the impression. Nineteenth-century travellers, missionaries and colonial officials wrote down what they saw because their job was to report on populations they had been sent to understand. A century of anthropology and folklore scholarship followed, in several languages, and the Brazilian and broader Latin American ritual record is now among the most thoroughly documented non-European systems in existence.

Which means an outsider reading this material is sampling a documentary tradition, not a population. The interest belongs to the record-keeping. Nothing follows from the record about how much attention the average person in any city is giving to magic, and anyone who tells you otherwise is reading an archive as a demographic.

The comparison that settles it: no one claims Europe was obsessed with alchemy on the strength of the libraries. The libraries are large there too. Alchemy was ordinary and unremarkable, and it produced a large archive because the institutions were literate and interested in cataloguing.

The one idea in this corpus that most practitioners should steal

Afro-Brazilian practice centres on a concept usually translated as work, appearing in the Portuguese as trabalho or occasionally as procurar. The point is not a translation problem. It is a different default assumption about what magic is.

A great deal of Western occult material treats magic as an experience to be had, a state to be entered, a sensation of contact. The South American corpus treats it as labour. You do it, on a schedule, for a purpose, on behalf of someone, and the value of the act is partly that it is work rather than experience. There is no premium on the intensity of what you feel.

This maps almost exactly onto the discipline the rest of this site keeps recommending for completely different reasons. A working that happens on a day you are tired is work. A working that produces a reviewable count is work. A working you only do when you feel ready is a practice whose frequency is controlled by your mood, which is a design flaw.

The Brazilian framing supplies the reason: you are not seeking a state, you are performing a task, and tasks have schedules. That is a better foundation for a practice than intensity, and it is not new. It is just not the part that travels well in translation, because a reader looking for techniques will find none and conclude the system is empty.

Urban systems with roles, courts, and an entry protocol

Umbanda and candomblé are not loose folk practice. They are organised, urban, and role-structured, with a house or temple that has a formal name, a leader, a calendar, and a protocol for entry.

That protocol is the part that surprises people coming from chaos magick. There is a process. There are conditions of entry. There is someone who has the standing to authorise you and who can also tell you that you are not ready, and that refusal is not a discouragement, it is the system working.

The calendar in the seven-by-seven pattern is documented and consistent, and it produces a frequency of contact that has nothing to do with enthusiasm. You are in contact on the schedule because the schedule says so. That single structural decision is the difference between a practice and a hobby, and it is the difference this site spends a lot of effort re-deriving in the absence of an institution.

The blending with Catholic saints is usually cited as evidence of corruption. It is better read as an adaptation with a long history, and the read that matters is functional: a system that absorbed a majority religion's calendar and figures kept its own structure while gaining reach. Whether that is compromise or strategy is a question about a specific community, not about the system.

What chaos magick gave up to become cheap, and what that cost

Spare and Carroll both removed initiatory structure deliberately. Carroll refused it explicitly, on the reasonable ground that an initiatory structure reproduces the gatekeeping that the practice exists to dismantle, and that is a coherent argument that has held up for fifty years.

The South American example makes the cost visible in a way the usual internal debate does not. Those systems have something chaos magick does not: institutional memory. When a beginner does something wrong, somebody with standing says so, and the correction has already been made once by somebody else who made the same mistake. The knowledge is not in a book that might not contain your question. It is in a room.

What fills that role in chaos magick is the log, and the log works, but it is a one-person instrument. It cannot tell you that your method is wrong, only that it produced nothing. It catches sloppiness and it does not catch bad method, because bad method looks exactly like a method that has not run long enough.

This is the real tradeoff and it is not resolved anywhere in the material. A cheap practice with no gatekeeping buys access and pays with a slower feedback loop. That is a good trade for most people and a bad one for anyone whose main failure mode is doing something wrong repeatedly and not finding out.

Both can be had. The Southern systems have roles and also have practice you can begin. What they do not have is a hundred-page booklet and a sigil generator.

What the corpus does not license

There is no single South American witchcraft. The record contains Indigenous nations' practices, Afro-Brazilian religions, European-derived folk magic carried in the colonial period, and a large amount of modern syncretic material, and they have different origins, histories and relationships to each other. Treating them as one tradition is the same error as treating all of European magic as one tradition.

The specific practices that circulate internationally, the ones with the recognisable imagery and the strong folklore, are usually a minority strand rendered highly visible. What gets shown is not a fair sample of what a large number of people in Brazil or Argentina or Colombia do.

The lineage claims deserve the same scepticism applied to any other tradition's. Continuity is often asserted and thinly documented, particularly across the Atlantic crossing, where a great deal was reconstructed, adapted and invented, and where the invention is legitimate practice rather than a defect.

The safe claim is the one about the archive: this region has an unusually well-documented and unusually legible set of ritual systems, which makes it a better place to study the structure of practice than most. That claim survives scrutiny. The claim about a temperament does not, and the second is the one usually made.

What an outsider gets wrong about the audience

The popular image is the candle, the smoke, the raised hand, the invoked name. That is the part of the record that photographs well, travels to other countries with a book or a video, and reproduces reliably in a new setting.

What the corpus is actually full of is administrative. Scheduling, roles, who may ask for what, who may be asked, what a request costs, what a person is obliged to do afterwards, who is responsible for a person who cannot be responsible for themselves. This is boring and it is the load-bearing part.

The gap between those two things explains a great deal of the disappointment foreigners report. The visible material is the part that travelled, and the visible material is not the part that does the work. Nobody goes home disappointed that the candle did not work, because the candle was never the mechanism.

If you are working inside the line of practice this site covers, the honest translation is to stop looking for the exotic and look at the administration. What schedule, what role, what cost, what is the review, and who has standing to say you have it wrong.

Where a newcomer can and cannot start

They can start. Every one of these systems has an entry route, and the entry route is a real and documented process rather than a rumour, which is more than most initiatory traditions will tell you.

They should start with the responsible parts and not the dramatic ones. Learn the schedule, learn the roles, learn what is asked of you and what you are not permitted to ask for, and treat the dramatic material as something you will be told about when somebody with standing decides you are ready. That order is not a modern ethical preference imposed from outside. It is how the systems themselves are organised.

The particular thing to resist is the shop window. There is a market of imported imagery, and it is not what the record contains. Nothing in the documented systems is served by a pentagram on a laptop.

The realistic timeline is long and the entry cost is low. Nobody can be talked into a day of practice and a certificate, and any offer of that in this area is selling something the systems do not contain.

Five ways this reading goes wrong

Reading the correction as dismissal. The claim that a temperament explanation is weak is not a claim that the practices are uninteresting. They are the best-documented ritual systems available for structural study and most of this site's discipline was independently arrived at in places that had no access to them.

Importing the romantic version. The exotic reading is a twentieth-century product and it distorts the material as thoroughly as the superstition reading did.

Flattening multiple nations and multiple traditions into one thing. Indigenous nations' practices, Afro-Brazilian religions and European-derived folk magic are distinct, and treating them as one tradition repeats the error being corrected.

Assuming the lack of initiation means lack of standards. Some of these systems are extremely strict and the standard is enforced socially rather than by certificate, which is harder to inspect from outside and not softer.

Reading the outsider's framing and keeping it. A description produced for the benefit of somebody else's curiosity is a description, not a finding, and the person who most needs to notice that is an outsider who has adopted it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is South America really obsessed with witchcraft?

No, and the question contains its own answer. What is unusual about the region is not interest but documentation. Colonial officials and missionaries wrote extensive records because reporting on populations was their task, and a century of scholarly work followed, so the archive is unusually large and unusually consistent. An outsider sampling it is sampling a documentary tradition and reading it as a population characteristic. The same reasoning would produce a claim that Europe was obsessed with alchemy, on the strength of a large body of writing by people who were not especially interested in it.

What is the most useful idea in Latin American folk magic?

Treating magic as work rather than as an experience. Afro-Brazilian practice centres on a concept usually translated as trabalho, and the default assumption underneath it is that you perform a task on a schedule for a purpose, not that you seek a state. That single shift removes the premium on intensity, which is the flaw in a lot of Western material, and it makes the frequency of practice a property of the design rather than of your mood. It is also the closest existing parallel to the review-date discipline used in chaos magick, arrived at in a completely separate context.

Can a foreigner join an Umbanda or Candomblé community?

Every one of these systems has a documented entry route, and it is a real process involving a request, conditions, and somebody with standing who can authorise or refuse you. What you should not do is start with the dramatic material, because the systems themselves sequence it that way: learn the schedule, the roles, what is asked of you and what you are not permitted to ask for, and treat the rest as something you are told about when someone decides you are ready. That ordering is internal to these traditions rather than a modern imposition. The realistic timeline is long and the entry cost is low, and anyone offering a certificate and a day of practice is selling something the systems do not contain.

Why do these traditions include Catholic saints and figures?

Because a system that absorbed the majority religion's calendar and figures kept its own structure while gaining reach, which is a much better description than the corruption story usually told about syncretism. The interesting question is not whether the mixture is pure, because almost no living tradition is pure, but whether the combination did functional work. In this case it plainly did. The broader lesson for anyone building a practice is that adapting a majority framework to reach more people and keeping your own internal logic is a legitimate strategy rather than a compromise to be apologised for.

Are there good apps or books for exploring this?

For the mechanics that this site covers, the free tools and the existing posts are the practical route, and a subscription is the wrong shape for exploratory work because it turns a research question into a recurring decision. If you want a serious introduction to the documentary side rather than the imagery, the study of the record is where the real material is, and a single good scholarly book beats a long app subscription. If you want to work with a system from this material, the commitment is to a community and a schedule rather than to a product, and no product substitutes for it.
