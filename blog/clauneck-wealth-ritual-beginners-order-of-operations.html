Clauneck Wealth Work for Beginners: The Right Order of Operations

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 14 min read

Clauneck is the most-used financial name in modern goetic practice and the least accurately described. The Ars Goetia gives him arts, sciences, hidden things, invisibility and boldness. It gives him no line about money. That gap is why beginner wealth work built on him so often stalls, and why the first useful thing to do with Clauneck has almost nothing to do with getting rich. This is a sequencing guide: the order that works, the parts to leave alone, and the sentence you have to write before you draw anything.

Start by deleting the word infinite from your goal

The phrase doing the most damage in beginner wealth work is infinite. It sounds like ambition and functions as a spell against measurement. Infinite wealth cannot be confirmed, because no account balance confirms it. Nothing you could observe would distinguish a successful infinite-wealth ritual from an unsuccessful one, which means the ritual cannot teach you anything, including how to do it better next time.

Substitute a target with three properties: a number, a date, and a behaviour you control. A client who owes you two thousand, a specific month, and a request sent in writing on a Tuesday. That is a worse sentence than infinite wealth and a far better target, because at the review date you will know whether the number moved and whether the request went out. The ritual has somewhere to fail.

This is the whole difference between a wealth working that compounds and one that feels productive. Infinite is a mood. Two thousand by the fifteenth is a commitment, and commitments are the only thing a sigil can amplify.

What Clauneck actually grants, according to the source

Before deciding what to ask him for, it is worth being precise, because most of the modern wealth material about Clauneck is cultural adoption rather than traditional authority. In the Ars Goetia he is a President who appears as a beautiful woman, commands thirty legions, teaches the arts and sciences and all crafts, discovers hidden and secret things and goods taken by others, can make a person invisible, is excellent at inducing the love of women, and inspires men and women to boldness. He can also make a person turn aside from a woman.

Not one clause concerns coin. Mammon is the spirit assigned to usury and wealth in the same book. Clauneck was a crafts and discovery spirit, and in a culture that turned every competent specialist into a businessman, he acquired a financial following. Nothing wrong with that adoption, but you should adopt it consciously. A spirit of skill read as a spirit of money is a second translation stacked on top of the first, and translations stacked on translations lose accuracy fast.

The useful reading is the opposite of the obvious one. A spirit who discovers hidden things and goods others have taken is a spirit of due process. That is a better fit for most beginners' actual financial problem than abundance is, because most people reading this are not short of opportunities. They are short of a way to find the opportunity that already exists and to move on the one that is already owed to them.

The three weeks, in order

A workable beginner ramp runs about three weeks and produces one small, finished, logged result. Not a system. One result, written down, with a date on it.

Week one is literacy and record-keeping. Draw three sigils, charge them by a method you can repeat, and write down the method. The method matters more than the sigil. If your only record is the fact that you did a ritual, you have no way to learn anything. If your record includes the time, the method, the state you were in and what you did in the following week, you have a dataset that will outlive your enthusiasm.

Week two is one small verifiable working. Small means the result is observable within a fortnight. Something with a name attached. A reply that arrives, an appointment that gets booked, a figure that gets corrected on an invoice, a question that gets answered. The sigil is charged once at the start and not recharged. Most beginners recharge compulsively, which converts a single act into a series of anxious restarts and destroys the causal story.

Week three is where the financial framing goes, and only if week two produced a result. Not before. A financial working built on an unverified method has nothing to amplify, so you end up attributing every coincidence in the month to the sigil. That is how beginners end up certain they are manifesting and equally unable to change anything.

The order matters more than the content. Literacy, then a result, then a larger target. Reversed, the larger target eats the method.

What to skip entirely in the first ninety days

Four things that look like progress and reliably function as avoidance.

Building a servitor. This is the most common displacement. A servitor is a real technique with real applications, and it is also a way to avoid a direct request you are uncomfortable making. If the actual thing you need is a conversation with a person, no entity will make that conversation easier and the servitor work will absorb the attention you would otherwise have spent rehearsing it. Leave it. There is a detailed guide to the craft when you are ready.

Grimoire shopping. A shelf of books read in fragments produces a practice made of quotations rather than a method. One technique executed forty times teaches you more than forty techniques read once. If you buy, buy one thing and work it.

Planetary hour scheduling at the start. Hour work requires a house, a chart, a book of correspondences and an hour worth of uninterrupted time. All four are barriers, and the first three are administrative. It is a fine technique. It is a poor second move.

Long-duration ritual structures. Forty-day workings, no-sex-no-money ascetic periods, multi-week commitment contracts. These are advanced tools with advanced failure modes. The first time you meet a difficult month, a forty-day structure gives you a rule to keep or break, and most beginners break it and conclude the magic failed. Short cycles that finish cleanly teach you how to complete things, which is the actual skill.

The cost of all four skipped items is real money. A beginner stack of a book, a sigil app and a moon phase tool is enough for months. The most common financial mistake in this corner of the practice is spending the operating budget on apparatus and then having nothing left for the thing the apparatus was for.

How to work with a President rather than command one

Clauneck's rank is President, which means the working is a request to an entity with its own agenda and its own court, not a construction you assemble from parts. The grammar changes accordingly. You are not building a tool. You are asking someone who already does this to do a specific thing, for a reason, with a stated willingness to be told no.

That last clause is the part beginners leave out and it is the part that makes the working coherent. An unrefusable request has no answer, and a request with no possible no is not a request. Writing the refusal condition before you start is what turns an invocation into a transaction, and it is also what lets you notice a genuine no, which is often more useful than a yes you were going to argue with.

Give the reason for the ask. Presidents in this material respond to a stated purpose, and an ask with no purpose gives the court nothing to weigh. This costs nothing to write and it changes the character of the request from demand to petition.

Ask once, in writing, then stop. The thirty legions are described as inspiring boldness, and boldness is his own word for the gift. Persistence is not the technique here. Boldness is.

The order to ask for things in

If you are going to work with him, sequence the requests by how well they match his stated office. This is not a ranking of power. It is a ranking of fit, and fit is what makes a request land.

First, the hidden things. Asking to be shown what exists rather than asked to be given what does not. In practice this looks like research, a referral, a lead that turns out to be real, a document you did not know you were entitled to. It is the most faithful use of his description and the easiest to verify, because the result is an object you can hold.

Second, boldness. This is a cheap ask and frequently more valuable than the money ask, because most people who stall on wealth are stalled on the conversation that precedes it. A sigil charged before a difficult call, once, without recharging, is a reasonable use of his office.

Third, the correction of an imbalance. Goods taken by others. An invoice that should have been paid, a scope that was quietly reduced, a payment that was agreed and never arrived. This is the clause that most closely maps onto a real beginner's financial situation, and it is a much better first financial target than a total.

Last, and only if the first three have worked and been recorded: anything requiring actual money to appear. This is where the tradition is thinnest and where the temptation to improvise is strongest. It is not forbidden. It is simply the request most likely to be a projection wearing a spirit's name, and you will not be able to tell the difference while you are still hoping.

A working sheet you can actually fill in

Six lines, filled before the sigil is drawn. The point is that every line has a value, so the working has a shape.

The ask, in one sentence, with a number and a date in it. The reason for the ask, in one sentence. The refusal condition, in one sentence, meaning the specific outcome that would count as told-no. What you will do in the seven days after, written as an action with a time on it. What you will record, being specific about the three things you will write down and when. The review date.

Six lines is a low bar and it is above the bar most people clear. The average wealth working has no review date, which means it can never be counted, which means it can never be improved, which means it is not a working. It is a mood with a sigil attached.

The review date is the part to protect. Everything else in this framework exists to make that date meaningful. Set it before you start, put it somewhere you will see it, and decide now what result ends the practice. A practice that can only be continued is not a practice.

Where the money framing usually breaks

Three predictable failure points, all of them honest and all of them avoidable with a little structure.

The spending substitution. A working aimed at a balance is often a working aimed at income, and income reliably becomes spending. The two look identical from inside the practice and they resolve differently. Settle it with two numbers, the balance at the start and the balance at the review date, and a third, the total spent in between. An increase in balance alongside an equal increase in spending is a working that did nothing about money, however much it felt like it did.

The attribution drift. Two months after a charged sigil, a good thing arrives. Was it the sigil? Unknowable. The way out is volume: many small workings with dates, so that you have a series rather than an anecdote. A series can be examined. An anecdote can only be believed or doubted.

The escalation. Each round needs a bigger request, because the last one was too small to feel like anything. This is the most reliable way to spend a lot of money for no return, and it is usually driven by a definition of success set at the start that no achievable result could have satisfied. Fix the target or stop.

None of these are arguments against the practice. They are arguments for keeping records, which is cheap and dull and the entire difference between a practice that improves and one that accumulates rituals.

Three signs you are ready to move up

You are ready for a bigger target when all three are true.

You have a completed log, not an intention to keep one. Several entries, with dates and outcomes, including at least one that did not go your way. The failure entries matter more than the successes, because they are the only part of the record that can teach you.

You can state the method without notes, and you can say what you would change. Not the ritual. The sequence, the timing, the target. If your answer is that you would work it harder, you are not ready.

The small working produced a result you could have produced otherwise, and you cannot tell which was which. This one is uncomfortable, and it is the honest position. If you are certain a tiny sigil changed a conversation, you are likely reading meaning into ordinary outcomes, and a larger target will amplify that error rather than correct it.

What a first ninety days should cost

Less than most people assume, and worth saying plainly because apparatus spending is the most common way beginners lose money on a practice whose whole subject is not losing money.

A sigil tool is the only genuinely useful purchase at the start, and a browser-based generator is enough. The drawing method is not where results live, and a better-looking sigil is not a more effective one. This site has a free generator, and a dedicated app if you want sigil storage, versioning and charge logs in one place.

A moon phase reference matters more than it looks, because timing is the cheapest variable to control and the easiest to feel good about adjusting. Free web tools cover it.

Books: one, and preferably the one that gives you a method rather than a cosmology. Our own volume on servitor construction is the obvious choice if that is the craft you are heading toward, though for a first ninety days it is honestly a step ahead of where you are.

An ESP or focus training app earns its place only if you intend to use the log. The training games are a legitimate way to sharpen attention before a charge, and useless as a substitute for it. There is one for Android.

Total: comfortably under the cost of a single impulse purchase, plus a notebook. A paper log in a physical book is better than an app for this specific purpose, because the friction of writing discourages the lazy entries that ruin a dataset.

If the total you are about to spend exceeds that, the difference is almost certainly going toward tools for a practice you have not started.

The ethics of a financial working

Three constraints, none of them negotiable, all of them structural rather than moral.

An ask aimed at money owed to you, money undercharged, or an opportunity you can describe accurately is a different act from an ask aimed at someone else's decision. The second one is the mechanism by which practitioners collect debt, break relationships and end up isolated. Decide before you start which of the two you are doing, and write it down, because the distinction is invisible in the moment.

Do not use boldness to override someone's informed no. His description says he inspires boldness. It does not say he removes consequences, and no entry in the Ars Goetia describes overriding a decision someone has already made. Reading a stronger version than the source supports is how a practice gets a reputation.

The refusal condition is an ethical commitment as much as a practical one. If the condition you wrote before starting is met, you act on the answer. Practices that treat a no as a technicality are practices that will eventually ask you for something you are not willing to give.

The version worth building toward

What you are actually assembling is a small, dated, written record of intent plus a repeatable method plus an honest review. Three components, all cheap, all learnable, none of which require an unusual belief to use well.

The belief part is genuinely optional and worth examining separately, because beginners tend to bundle it with the craft and then get stuck on the belief. A sigil drawn to encode a specific action, charged once, followed by a specific action on a specific day, is a coherent unit of practice whether or not you think a spirit intervened. Most of the reliable effect sits in the encoding and the follow-through, and those are yours.

Start with the number and the date. Charge once. Do the thing. Write down what happened. That is the whole method, and it works in the first week, and everything else is a refinement on it. Clauneck's thirty legions are not going to arrive and fix a practice that skipped the part where you send the email.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a Clauneck wealth ritual take to work?

The honest answer is that the timing is not the part you control. A sigil drawn and charged in ten minutes can produce an observable result within a fortnight, and a two-hour working can produce nothing for a year, because the mechanism is the action you take afterwards and not the drawing. Judge the practice on the rate at which completed logged workings produce their stated results, measured over at least a dozen entries. A single unset working tells you nothing about speed.

Do I need a grimoire or a specific book to work with Clauneck?

You need a usable text of the Ars Goetia and one reliable method for drawing and charging sigils. That is genuinely all, and it is why the beginner-friendly advice is to spend the first month on one technique rather than on a library. Most of what circulates as Clauneck financial material is modern devotional writing layered on a two-sentence entry, so a modern source that presents it as tradition is worth reading with a specific question: which sentences are quoted and which are the author's own construction.

Should I make a sigil for every money problem I have?

No, and the multiplication is the reason it stops working. Each unsigned intention dilutes the charge rather than multiplying it, and thirty unresolved financial situations is a worry that has been given a ritual shape and therefore feels like progress. Pick the single line item with the largest gap between what is owed and what has been received, work it to completion with a review date, and only then start a second. Volume comes after accuracy, not before it.

What if I want to work with a spirit associated with money directly?

Then name that spirit in the source and work with what the source says, which is the approach the Clauneck articles here take. Mammon is the entry the Ars Goetia assigns to wealth and usury, and working him honestly means working his actual clause rather than borrowing Clauneck's skill clause and calling it financial. A guide to writing that kind of pact correctly, with the four-part test and a safe protocol, covers the structure these workings share.

Is it worth paying an app or a book for this?

Worth paying for convenience and for storage, not for permission. What separates a working that improves from one that does not is a written log, and a free notebook does that as well as anything. A sigil tool saves time and prevents you from charging the same drawing twice, which is a real and common error. Beyond that, the honest answer is that no product substitutes for the six-line working sheet, and anyone selling one as a shortcut is selling you the part you already have for money.
