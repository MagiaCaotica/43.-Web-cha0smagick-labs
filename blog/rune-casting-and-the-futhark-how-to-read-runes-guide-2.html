Rune Casting and the Futhark: A Casting Method That Works

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 6 min read

Runes are the most compact divination system in common use, and that compactness is their main advantage over tarot. Twenty-four characters, three at a time, and a spread short enough to hold in your head. You can cast a full reading in under a minute without a deck, a table or a candle, which makes them the most practical system for a small daily practice. The compactness is also the difficulty. With tarot, a card is a picture and the interpretation has somewhere to start. A rune is a character, and the interpretation has to come from you, which means the system teaches you less per cast and asks more of you per cast. People who get good at runes are people who have logged several hundred of them. Two historical points matter for practice. First, the runes were an alphabet. They were used for writing, naming and binding, and the mystical reading is a modern elaboration layered on a working script. Second, the Elder Futhark is the wrong set for most Nordic beginners, who encounter the sixteen-rune Younger Futhark first and are then told the wrong one is the real one. Neither is wrong, but they are different tools for different jobs.

Elder Futhark and Its Three Aettir

The Elder Futhark comprises twenty-four characters in three groups of eight, called aettir, and the structure survives into modern practice because it is genuinely useful. Three rows of eight gives you a natural way to organise a large alphabet into a set you can hold in memory.

The aettir are ordered by shared properties rather than alphabetically. The first row, the Freyr row, is concerned with people and their circumstances. The middle row, the Hagal row, is about hardship and difficulty. The last row, the Týr row, runs from war and death through to art and time.

The structure means a three-rune draw has a default structure. Three from the first row is about people, three from the middle is about difficulty, three from the last is about outcomes. You do not have to use that, but having it available removes most of the decision-making from interpretation.

A Casting Method You Can Repeat

The cleanest beginner method is a three-rune draw from a shuffled deck face down, taken left to right, without looking. It takes seconds, it produces the same distribution every time, and it has the enormous advantage of not involving a bag you have to remember to shake differently every time.

A single-rune draw is the daily practice. It is a one-word answer, and the discipline is writing the interpretation in one word before turning to a reference. That constraint is what builds fluency, and it is much faster than looking up meanings.

Three runes is the standard small reading, and there is a convention worth adopting: the first is the situation as it stands, the second is the force acting on it, the third is where it heads. Reverse positions, if you use them, indicate blockage, and treating every reversed rune as a caution is a good default for a first year.

Why Bindrunes Are the Real Ancestor

Bindrunes are characters combined into a single compound glyph, and they are attested in the historical record from the migration period. Three or four runes merged into one mark, used for names and short phrases where the writer wanted a monogram.

That is structurally identical to modern sigilisation: reducing a statement to its constituent letters, then merging them into a minimal glyph nobody else can read. The mechanism Austin Osman Spare described in 1904 is a direct inheritance, and the continuity is well documented in the sigilisation literature.

The lineage matters for two reasons. It shows that sigils are not an invention of one early twentieth-century occultist, and it suggests that the compression principle is doing the same work in both cases, namely converting a statement into something that is hard to read accidentally and easy to recognise deliberately.

Elder or Younger Futhark

The Younger Futhark has sixteen characters, and the Anglo-Saxon futhorc added several more for sounds the original did not cover. All three are real, and they came from different regions and dates, not from a single progressive sequence.

The Elder Futhark is the standard choice for divination because twenty-four characters give a wider interpretive range and because the majority of modern rune materials use it. If your interest is historical, Younger Futhark is the better fit for Viking-Age material, and Anglo-Saxon runes are the right choice for Old English texts.

Learn one set first. Mixing sets mid-beginning produces confusion about the meanings, since the translations overlap heavily but not exactly, and the resulting muddle is the reason people conclude runes are arbitrary.

Do You Need a Staff

No. A staff is an aesthetic and a handling aid, and a beautiful one is a legitimate reason to own one. Neither function affects what the cast means.

What matters is that the casting process is identical each time. A bag shaken the same number of times, a deck cut the same way, stones drawn from a bowl without looking. Ritual consistency in the method is what removes the chance element cleanly, and everything beyond that is decoration you are free to enjoy.

Holding a staff, or shaking a bag, or turning over three cards: any of these work. What does not work is changing method whenever the answer is inconvenient, because then you have no baseline to compare against.

Interpreting With a Small Alphabet

Twenty-four characters sounds limiting until you realise the interpretive move is not lookup, it is association. Take the rune's core sense and let it run: fire, journey, harvest, doorway, inheritance, loss.

A short written meaning helps more than a paragraph. Core sense, one image, one application. Three lines in a reference is enough to work from, and a long description actively hurts because it gives you a finished interpretation you will then try to fit the situation into.

This is why rune reading improves so fast for people who log. Three hundred logged casts is a large personal dataset, and at that point you recognise the situation pattern faster than you recognise the rune.

Journaling the Sequence

Log the date, the question, the runes drawn, and your interpretation in one sentence, plus what you did. That last field is what converts a reading record into an audit.

Read the log at three months looking for the runes you keep drawing and the question types you keep asking. In most logs there is one rune that appears four or five times, and it is nearly always the one describing the person's dominant coping strategy.

Second pattern worth looking for: whether runes drawn before a decision differ from those drawn after. This is not a test of validity. It is a way of noticing whether you actually use the reading, which is the variable you control.

What Runes Will Not Tell You

They will not tell you what another person is thinking, they will not predict an outcome, and they will not resolve an ethical question for you. This is not a limitation of the medium, it is a limitation of short answers to long questions.

The word rune in Old Norse appears in both a writing context and an explicitly mystical one, but the modern esoteric literature reads considerably more into the historical sources than the sources themselves state. That gap is worth knowing about, because filling it is the first step toward treating the reading as more authoritative than it is.

What runes do well is short situational judgements that you are already well placed to make. Used that way they are a fast, honest thinking tool. Used as a sensor they will let you down, and they will do it in a way that costs you confidence in the method.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a rune staff to cast runes?

No. Casting from a bag, drawing from a shuffled deck, or reading from stones on a cloth all produce equivalent results. A staff is an aesthetic and a handling aid, and the only real requirement is that you use the same method each time so the chance element is handled consistently.

Are runes an alphabet or a divination system?

Historically both. The same characters served writing, naming and binding before they served anything mystical, and the divination use is a later elaboration. That does not make the practice invalid, it just means the interpretive material you find today was added rather than inherited.

Which runes should I learn first?

The twenty-four rune Elder Futhark, in aettir order. The Anglo-Saxon futhorc and the sixteen-rune Younger Futhark are real sets worth learning later for historical work, but mixing them in the first months creates confusion because the translations overlap without matching exactly.

How many runes should I draw for a reading?

One for a daily word, three for a standard spread with situation, force and direction. More than three is where the reading stops being usable, because a longer cast needs vocabulary you have not built yet and the later runes tend to get interpreted as filler.

Is a paid rune deck or app worth it over drawing on paper?

The drawing is free and the interpretation is the whole skill, so a paid deck only improves the aesthetic. Where payment can earn its place is the log, since a searchable history of past casts is genuinely hard to maintain by hand, and that archive is what teaches you your own patterns.
