The 72 Spirits of Goetia

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 7 min read

The Goetia material is the most commercially packaged corner of Western esotericism, and it is also one of the few places where the gap between what a text says and what a video says is large enough to measure. The corpus itself is not guilty of most of what is attributed to it. It is a bureaucratic inventory, mostly dry, describing officials with assigned jurisdictions in a scheme borrowed from older demonological material. What turns it into horror cinema is a modern layer of interpretation that has almost nothing to do with the source. This piece is written for someone who has watched a good deal of the popular content and wants to know which parts survive contact with the text. It names the limits of the corpus plainly, including the claims it cannot support, and it treats the tradition as a working practice rather than as an object of belief.

What the corpus actually contains

Seventy-two named entities sorted into three ranks: nine Presidents, then thirty-six Dukes and thirty Kings, the lot sorted into small sets of two to four names apiece. Each entry gives a rank, a name, a stated domain, and a description of what the entity is said to do. That is the whole of it. The classical descriptions are administrative to the point of boredom, closer to a municipal register than to a demonology.

There is no narrative, no theology of horror, and no account of what any of it means. The horror is imported by the reader and by everyone who has retold the material since. A great many people who can recite the number of ranks correctly have never read an entry all the way through, which is how a list of officials became a list of monsters.

The two older channels it arrived through

The names enter the European grimoire lineage through two older channels, a seventeenth-century conjuration text and the Lemuria. Much of the name material is older still and comes from Jewish and Christian demonological writing, with the Pseudepigrapha Book of Enoch among the sources usually cited.

The point of the provenance is not to debunk anything. It is to establish that this is a compiled taxonomy with a long transmission history, which is a different kind of object from an anthology of actual beings. Whether the entities behind the names exist is a question the text does not address and cannot be made to address, no matter how the question is phrased.

The classical context the retelling removed

In the older scheme these beings held office. A President teaches the liberal arts, a Duke governs a duchy, a King commands an army or a court. Several descriptions read like a magistrate or a master of a guild, which is exactly what makes them useful as working material and useless as horror.

That context is not decoration to be restored for accuracy. It changes what the practice can be for. A spirit with a stated jurisdiction gives you a structure to work within, and a spirit without one gives you an occasion to improvise. The first is a method. The second is a mood.

  1. Office rather than appetite is what the descriptions state.
  2. A jurisdiction gives the working its shape.
  3. A name without a domain can only be improvised around.
  4. Improvisation is where the frightening material comes from.

The modern brand, as a separate thing

Video essays, commentary channels, the apps, the grimoire products: that layer is a twenty-first century phenomenon sitting on top of much older material, and it is worth studying as a phenomenon in its own right. Something that started as a footnote in a large grimoire became a self-contained entertainment genre with its own iconography.

The genre has its own logic. Entities are ranked by recognisability rather than by fit, dramatic scenes are supplied where the text gives none, and the selection of a spirit is usually made on the basis of a name or a film rather than a stated domain. That process is not fraudulent. It is a cultural recycling operation, and it is worth watching yourself inside it.

The counterargument the enthusiasts raise

The strongest case for the modern reading is that a description of authority is still a description of power, and that the intervening centuries of clerical editing are a reliable way to obscure the original force of the material. On that view, recovering the older scheme is not a demystification but a restoration.

It is a coherent position and it can be held without embarrassment. What it does not do is change what the entries say, and it remains an interpretive layer rather than a finding. Nobody has produced a mechanism by which removing the Christian demonological framing makes the entities more responsive rather than less.

Where the practice actually breaks down

The common failure is selection by fame. People pick the most recognisable names and then discover that the entity's stated domain has nothing to do with their question, and they stretch the description until it appears to fit. The log records the question and not the mismatch.

The second failure is escalation, and it is the one with consequences. A person who has had an unremarkable result will often try a larger entity, then a more dramatic framing, then a stronger invocation, and the sequence escalates without any new information arriving. That pattern is better explained by frustration than by a changing response on the other side, and it is the most common reason this material ends up costing more than it should.

What the corpus refuses to support

It does not support a controller. Nothing in the entries describes a being that influences a person against their will, and the one passage routinely cited in modern retellings is a bound condition rather than a power of coercion.

It does not support compulsion, transformation, or the acquisition of abilities. It does not support the idea that any entry grants a permanent faculty, and the entries that read most extravagantly in translation are usually about knowledge, discovery and the removal of obstacles. Anyone offering you those things on the strength of a seventy-two-spirit list is selling something the list does not contain.

  • No entry describes controlling a person against their will.
  • No entry grants a permanent ability or a transformation.
  • No entry describes compulsion as a technique.
  • The strongest claims in translation concern knowledge and discovery.

Choosing by jurisdiction

If you want to work with this material, the useful question is what an entry says it does, not how well you know its name. The list is arranged by jurisdiction, which means there is almost always an entry whose stated domain is closer to your question than the famous one you were going to pick.

Write the question down first. Then find the three entries whose stated domain touches it and read their full descriptions rather than the summaries. In most cases one of the three will be obviously better suited than any headline name, and the choice will be defensible in your own log months later when you have forgotten what you were thinking.

What working with one looks like in practice

Decide the question. Choose the entry by domain. State the terms, including what you will do and what you will not do, and set an end date. Construct the seal if you work that way. Perform the sequence, close it, and write the entry the same day.

Keep the duration short. Keep the framing administrative rather than dramatic, because the text you are working from is administrative and importing drama into it is the single biggest difference between a working practice and a performance. And close the book afterwards, because leaving the material open is how a one-off question becomes a condition.

  1. Write the question before opening the book.
  2. Select on the stated domain, not on the name.
  3. Set terms and an end date before you begin.
  4. Write the entry the same day, misses included.

The safety question, answered plainly

The physical risks here are negligible and should not be inflated. Nothing in the practice requires anything unsafe, and the genuine danger is entirely in how attention gets organised. The real hazards are fixation, the erosion of sleep, and the slow reorganisation of a life around a practice that has stopped producing information.

Those are manageable and the management is structural rather than moral. Fix a review date. Write everything down. If a practice is interfering with work, sleep or relationships, reduce its intensity rather than intensifying it in response, and if reducing it feels impossible, that is the finding you were looking for.

Whether any of it is worth doing

For most people the value is not in the entities, and the honest version of what this practice is for is a structured way of making a decision and putting it in writing. That is a real use and it survives every debunking the material has received.

The genre is also worth reading as a cultural document, because watching a footnote become a television property tells you a great deal about how symbols move and why people invest them with authority. Neither reading requires belief, and both are more interesting than the thing the videos claim is happening.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the corpus genuinely refuse to support?

Any claim of compulsion, transformation, permanent ability, or control over a person against their will. Nothing in the seventy-two entries describes a being that overrides a human being's stated preferences, and the passages modern retellings lean on are bound conditions rather than powers of coercion. The strongest claims in the text concern knowledge, discovery and the removal of obstacles.

Which spirit does the text associate with wealth?

A royal one rather than a demonic one. The King of wealth is named in the entries, and a duke is described as granting riches. Worth noticing that both sit in the ranks of officialdom, which tells you something about how the older scheme understood money. What none of them does is create anything, and any working promising otherwise is promising something the text does not say.

Are the spirits demons in any real sense?

That question cannot be answered from the source because the source does not discuss ontology. What it gives you is a taxonomy with a transmission history: names drawn from older demonological writing, arranged by rank and jurisdiction, in a compiled list rather than an anthology of beings. Whether anything answers to those names is a separate question, and the text neither supports nor forbids it.

Is a paid app worth having for this?

As a reference, occasionally, and for the same reason a printed grimoire is worth having: finding a jurisdiction quickly rather than reading the list repeatedly to find it. It is not worth paying for as guidance, because the guidance in this category is where the dramatic framing gets added, and that framing is the thing the classical text does not contain.

How do I know when I have gone too far?

When the practice has started reorganising your life rather than sitting inside it. Sleep disruption, secrecy about what you are doing, checking results more than once a day, and the sense that a question has become a condition are all early signals. None of them require a crisis, and the response to all of them is to reduce the frequency and write the whole thing down again.
