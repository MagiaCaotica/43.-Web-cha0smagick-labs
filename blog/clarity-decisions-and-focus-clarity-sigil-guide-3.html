Clarity, Decisions and Focus

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 27, 2026 ⬢ 10 min read

There is a problem with clarity workings that nobody in the literature wants to name, and it is this: clarity is a noun, and a noun cannot fail. You can work for a week and feel nothing, and nothing has falsified anything, because clarity was never specified in a way that could have been contradicted. That makes the whole genre unusually safe to practise and unusually difficult to learn from. The way out is not scepticism. It is substitution. Replace the noun with a criterion that some future version of you would be able to mark as true or false, and the practice immediately becomes the same kind of thing as every other working in this tradition: a commitment with a test attached. This page takes the four claims that hold up, shows where each one leaks when applied to clarity, and builds the substitution.

The word that cannot fail

Write clarity in one line and try to state what would count as having got it. Most practitioners cannot, and the reason is structural rather than personal: clarity is defined as an absence of a specific confusion, and absences are hard to date. Compare it with the noun attention, which is at least continuous and observable, or with knowledge, which can be tested by asking a stranger. Clarity is the residue left when two other conditions are satisfied, so aiming at it directly aims at a result rather than a cause.

The consequence for practice is that a clarity working is structurally protected from correction. You can believe in it for years and the belief will never be touched by a bad week, because the bad week is not a measurement of anything. A tradition that prides itself on testing claims has produced a genre where testing is impossible, and that is worth naming before anything else.

A sigil for clarity, and what it commits you to

Attentional control is among the better-studied cognitive faculties, and there are training methods for it that replicate across labs. That is the strongest thing to be said for a clarity working, and it is also the answer to the falsifiability problem. A sigil aimed at clarity works best when it commits you to a specific known method rather than to a state: a fixed sitting schedule, a task with a defined start and end, a rule about what happens when you notice you have drifted.

In practice this means the sigil is a commitment device aimed at a method rather than an invocation aimed at a condition. That is not a demotion. It is the same structure as every other well-built working in this tradition, where the glyph is a compressed instruction you have already decided to follow, and the question worth asking is always which part of the outcome was already in the plan.

Why paralysis is usually a conflict and not a shortage

Decision paralysis is very often reported as an information deficit, and the diagnosis is usually wrong. What people describe as not having enough information is more commonly two criteria in conflict: one that optimises for safety and one that optimises for growth, both of which are legitimate, both of which are fully satisfied by the same evidence, and which cannot both be maximised. More data does not help, because the data was never the binding constraint. The conflict is.

Forcing the aim into an imperative sentence is a surprisingly effective way to detect this, because the imperative requires a verb with an object and no qualifying clause. If the sentence you produce needs the word or in it, you have found the conflict in your own handwriting. This is the practical reason the exercise is worth doing before the charge rather than after, and it costs nothing to try three versions of the sentence and notice that one of them keeps needing a subordinate clause.

The imperative sentence as an instrument

The compression from sentence to glyph is well documented in this tradition and the mechanism of the sentence is the part that gets skipped. Writing the aim as an imperative forces a specific structure: an agent, a verb, an object, and a time. Most of the work in a clarity working happens at this stage and not later, because an aim written as a wish cannot be charged, and an aim that cannot be charged produces nothing to attach a practice to.

There is a second effect worth keeping. The imperative drops the hedges, and the hedges are usually where the doubt lives. When a person writes that they would like to be able to decide more easily, they have written a description of a difficulty. When they write that they will decide by Friday on the criteria they listed, they have written an instruction, and the psychological distance between those two sentences is larger than the distance between charging the glyph and not charging it.

Projective surfaces and the limits of self-report

The Rorschach and the other projective tests were built on a specific observation: some material is accessible to a person but not to their own reporting about themselves, and a stimulus with no obvious structure allows it to surface. The same logic sits behind using a card deck as a thinking tool rather than a predictive one, and it is genuinely useful for a decision you have already circled several times without resolving.

Its limits are equally real, and they are the limits of the method rather than of the technique. A projective surface produces material, not information, and it will produce material with no special claim on being true about the future. Used properly it is a generator of options you had not considered, which is a different and more modest function than divination and a perfectly good one.

The old discipline hiding inside the word

Gnosis in this tradition means a moment of sustained contact with an experience, as opposed to an abstract idea about it, and the word carries far more history than the modern movement. That older meaning is a description of a skill rather than an attainment, and the skill is trainable: the capacity to stay with something for long enough that it stops being a category and starts being a particular thing. Most of what people want when they ask for clarity is this, phrased as a wish about the world.

Which is why the contemplative route and the sigil route are not competing. A sitting practice that trains sustained contact with one thing, twenty minutes a day, is doing the same work as a clarity working done properly, and it will produce it more reliably. The sigil's job is to make the sitting happen on the days you would not have chosen to sit.

The test that makes the aim falsifiable

Take the aim and add a clause that some future version of you could rule on. Not a feeling to be assessed, and not a general sense of improvement, but something with a date and a criterion: how many decisions you made using the written criteria, how often you noticed yourself circling, whether you could state your reasoning for a decision within a minute of making it. Any of these can be false, which is the entire point.

Write the criterion down before the charge and do not revise it afterwards. Revising a criterion after a disappointing month is the mechanism by which a clarity working becomes permanently unfalsifiable, and it usually feels like intellectual honesty rather than like moving the goalposts. The test of whether you have done it honestly is simple: could the person who is most disappointed have been given the criterion in advance and held you to it?

A working you could fail

Here is a complete construction that satisfies all of this. The aim is that by the end of the month you will have made three decisions that you recorded at the time, using criteria you wrote down beforehand, and you will be able to explain each one in a sentence. That can fail. It can fail because you made no decisions, because you made them without writing, or because when you look back the reasoning is not recoverable, and each of those failures is informative.

The charge is unremarkable, and that is the correct feel for this genre: a short fixed sequence, a glyph you no longer need to look at afterwards, and a written follow-through that occupies most of the month. A clarity working with a large ritual apparatus attached to it almost always turns the apparatus into the point, and then you have a practice of maintaining apparatus rather than a practice of deciding.

Where clarity workings go vague

The drift is extremely regular. The first aim is specific and checkable. By the third charge it has become broader, because each disappointing week makes the target feel insufficient, and broadening the target is a reliable way to make next month feel like progress. By the fifth the aim might be to be at peace with uncertainty, which cannot fail and therefore cannot be practised.

The most reliable detector is the subordinate clause. Re-read the current version of the aim and count how many times you need a hedge, a qualifier, or a phrase like as appropriate. Every hedge is a place where the aim has been quietly widened to protect it from a result, and an aim that needs hedges is not a difficult aim, it is an absent one.

The record that keeps the aim honest

Three columns and one of them is the hard one. Write the date, the criterion as it stood that day, and then what you would have done anyway had there been no working at all. That third column is the control, and it is uncomfortable precisely because it is the honest baseline. Most months will show that your decision-making improved for reasons that have nothing to do with the charge, and recording that is what keeps the whole exercise from becoming a self-reinforcing story.

The reason to keep this log even when it deflates you is that it is transferable. The pattern in the third column, the situations where you already had the capacity and simply did not use it, is a description of your own decision-making that is worth more than the sigil. You can use it on questions that have nothing to do with this tradition at all, which is more than can be said for most of what gets built in the first months.

A month of reading calm as evidence

The most common false positive in this genre is calm. Feeling settled for a week after a demanding decision is excellent, and it is not evidence that the working did anything, because the demand has passed. Anyone who has ever had a good stretch of life without performing a ritual has had the experience that the tradition is built to explain, and this is the cheapest possible way to manufacture confirmation.

The countermeasure is to check the timing. If the calm appeared on a week with no external cause, write down what was different, because that is a real observation. If it appeared the day after a difficult conversation resolved, the calm belongs to the conversation. The distinction takes ten seconds at the time and is very difficult to reconstruct a month later.

Replacing the noun with a criterion

The whole critique reduces to a single edit. Wherever the working says clarity, put a testable thing in its place: a number of decisions recorded, a duration of uninterrupted attention, a count of times you returned after drifting, a single sentence explaining a choice you made. Each of these can be false, each of them is connected to something people actually mean by clarity, and each of them can be checked by someone who is not you.

This substitution is also the more respectful option. A working that asks for a measurable behaviour is one you would be happy to explain to somebody who does not believe any of this, because the explanation does not require them to accept a premise. A working that asks for clarity asks a question that can only be answered by the practitioner, and asking such questions repeatedly is how a practice becomes insulated from its own results.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is a sigil for clarity hard to test?

Because clarity is a noun describing the absence of a specific confusion, and absences are hard to date. Unlike attention or knowledge, it cannot be marked true or false, so a disappointing month falsifies nothing. The fix is substitution: replace the noun with a criterion some future version of you could rule on, such as a number of decisions recorded using written criteria.

Is decision paralysis caused by a lack of information?

Usually not. It is more often a conflict between two legitimate criteria, one optimising for safety and another for growth, both satisfied by the same evidence and not jointly maximisable. Since the evidence was never the binding constraint, collecting more of it does not help. Writing the aim as an imperative exposes the conflict, because the sentence will keep needing the word or.

What does gnosis mean in this context?

A moment of sustained, non-abstract contact with an experience. It is a description of a trainable skill rather than an attainment, and it is far older than the modern movement. Most requests for clarity are really requests for this capacity, phrased as a wish about the world, which is why a sitting practice of twenty minutes a day often produces more than the sigil does.

Why are projective tests and card decks useful for decisions?

They were built on the observation that some material is accessible to a person but not to their own reporting, and a stimulus with little obvious structure lets it surface. Used as generators of options you had not considered, that is a modest and useful function. It is not divination, and the material produced has no special claim to be true about the future.

How do I stop my clarity aim from drifting?

Re-read the current version of the aim and count how many hedges, qualifiers or phrases like as appropriate it needs. Each hedge is a place where the aim has been widened to protect it from a result. Also write the falsifiable criterion down before the charge and refuse to revise it afterwards, since revising a criterion after a bad month is the standard route back to unfalsifiability.
