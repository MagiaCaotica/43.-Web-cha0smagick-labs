Pacts with Mammon: A Complete Guide to Goetic Wealth Rituals

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 26, 2026 ⬢ 16 min read

Mammon is named twice in the New Testament, both times as the thing you cannot serve alongside God, and both times the Greek translators reached for an Aramaic word that simply means wealth. That is the whole of the traditional case against him, and almost everything else attached to the name was added later by people who were not trying to sell you anything. Once you separate the Aramaic term from the medieval demon, what is left is a remarkably clean tool for a specific problem: the gap between knowing you should act and acting.

What the word Mammon actually means

The name reaches English through two Gospel passages. Matthew 6:24 reads "you cannot serve both God and Mammon." Luke 16:19-31 tells the parable of the rich man and Lazarus, and in the Syriac Peshitta the word used for the rich man's accumulated wealth is the same Aramaic term, mamōnā. The Greek Septuagint transliterated it rather than translating it, which is why the word survived in English unchanged.

The Aramaic sits on the root meaning to entrust, to acquire, to hold in trust. It does not mean greed as a sin, and it does not mean money as a thing. It means the accumulation itself — the pile you have built and now look at. The parable makes the distinction brutally clear. The rich man's problem was never that he had money. It was that he used the money to buy distance from a beggar at his gate, and that the distance was the entire content of his relationship to the suffering in front of him.

The prohibition is about lending, not about having

This is the part that gets lost in every sensational retelling. Alongside the Gospel sayings sit two Old Testament passages that explain what kind of mammon the tradition is actually objecting to. Leviticus 25:35-37 forbids lending to a poor neighbour at interest. Deuteronomy 23:19-20 forbids taking interest from your own countrymen at all.

Read together, these are not a prohibition on prosperity. They are a prohibition on usury — on extracting rent from someone in a weaker position than yourself. The historical setting makes that plain: these are agricultural societies where a failed harvest puts a household three months from starvation, and a high-interest loan against a harvest is a mechanism for permanent land loss.

Carry that distinction into occult practice and a lot of inherited taboos dissolve. "Do not make a pact for money" is not a ban on wealth. It is a ban on using occult power to extract from the vulnerable, which is a rule about means, not about ends. A ritual performed to help you get a job you will show up to is not the same act as a ritual performed to bankrupt a competitor.

Mammon in the Goetia, and the edition problem

If you go looking for Mammon in the Lesser Key of Solomon you will hit a wall of edition variance. The Ars Goetia, a seventeenth-century addition to the original Grimorium, lists Mammon among the seventy-two, generally as a king who instructs in treasure and the arts of coin. The numbering shifts between printings, and a good number of modern websites copy a number from a source that copied it from a source that never opened the text.

What matters for practice is the description, not the position. Three traits are stable across the tradition: he is associated with wealth rather than with its destruction, he grants rather than commands, and there is no recorded shrine, no temple, and no geographic territory in his name. That last detail is a real tell. Spirits with territory have shrines. Spirits with grants have only instructions.

The medieval Christian iconography did not help. Early European representations drew him as a sow with a woman's face, which came from reading the parable as a portrait of gluttony rather than as a warning about detachment. The pig is an artist's solution to an ambiguous text, and it did more to shape the modern image of Mammon than any grimoire did.

Baphomet is not Mammon, and the confusion has a cause

A large share of the material written about money rituals is actually about Baphomet, and the two get merged constantly. They are not the same entity and they did not come from the same source. Baphomet as most people picture it — a goat-headed figure with a human body, arms raised, seated — was drawn in 1856 by the French occultist Éliphas Lévi as a frontispiece illustration. Lévi built it as a deliberate synthesis of the horned god Pan and the horned goddess Diana of Ephesus, meant to be a symbol of the reconciliation of opposites rather than a portrait of a devil.

Baphomet's famous torch and the 666 on the forehead were added by Éliphe Lévi's disciple Louis Lavallée, and the whole image was popularised in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries by a fringe of ceremonial magicians. Lévi himself said the figure was an allegorical image and that the name was to be pronounced Bayphomet. Nothing about it appears in any medieval or early modern source.

Mammon, by contrast, is a Biblical word with a documented history. If a piece of writing about your money ritual keeps producing a goat, the source has drifted, and the ritual you end up performing will be shaped by that drift.

The Golden Dome connection: Typhon

The most useful thing that happened to Mammon in the esoteric literature was not Christian at all. In the Golden Dawn system, the destructive aspect of the earth element is personified as Typhon, and Crowley names Typhon explicitly as the higher type of earth — the element considered in its active, corroding, mineral form. Typhon is chthonic, hidden, associated with rot, mineral wealth buried underground, and the appetite that never feels satisfied by what it has taken.

Crowley drew the connection out in Book 4, where he treats Typhon as the elder and harder form of the earth, distinct from the younger and more tractable form. The Golden Dawn's ritual vocabulary maps Typhon's qualities onto the human vice of greed in a way that reads very differently from the Gospel parable. The parable says: wealth used to avoid your neighbour. Typhon says: wealth that has stopped behaving like wealth and started behaving like a force that owns you.

For practice, Typhon is the more workable figure. He is a natural process, not a personality. Nobody was ever Typhonically abducted. You can work with a natural process, because it will still be running when you have finished the ceremony. Working with a personality means you have to negotiate, and negotiation implies it has a position.

Why modern chaos magick mostly abandoned pacts

Chaos magick is unusually honest about its own history, and the abandonment of pacts is the most useful thing in it. Austin Osman Spare's god-forms, published in Liber ABA in 1909, were a deliberate replacement for the binding ritual. You do not summon a god and bargain with it; you construct a temporary identity, inhabit it, and act from inside it, then let it dissolve. Peter Carroll's "greeks" in Liber Null pushed the same idea toward abstraction — a greek is an idealized figure you borrow, not a being you owe.

The reason is structural rather than moral. A pact is a promise, and a promise creates an obligation. Obligation creates a stake. Stake creates attachment. Attachment is the precise mechanism that makes a wealth pact leak power back into the fear it was meant to end, because the moment the outcome matters to you, you have reintroduced exactly the anxiety the working was designed to dissolve.

So the contemporary position is not that pacts are forbidden. It is that a pact is the wrong tool for a beginner and the wrong tool for most outcomes, and that the honest replacement is gnosis plus a written, dated, measurable commitment. A wealth pact is worth doing if and only if you can state in one sentence what you are actually committing to, and that sentence must contain a number and a date.

The one-sentence test before you start

Before any of this becomes a ritual, write one sentence with four parts in it. What number. By what date. What specific action you take, repeated how often. And what you do about it if the date arrives and the number has not moved.

If you cannot write the fourth part, stop. The fourth part is the whole protection. Rituals fail backwards: the failure is not that the money did not arrive, it is that nothing was specified, so nothing was attempted, and the absence of effort is reinterpreted after a month as evidence about the universe rather than evidence about the sentence. The rewritten interpretation is what people come back with, and it is always a version of a message about their own worth.

A working that passes this test is boring. It is short, it is dated, it is measurable, and it costs almost nothing. That is the point. The ceremony exists to compress the interval between deciding and doing. If your ritual is more expensive than the action, the ceremony has become the action and you have changed jobs.

A protocol that stays inside safe limits

The structure below is deliberately unglamorous. It is a Spare-style construction wearing the vocabulary of a pact, because that is what the workable version looks like in practice.

  1. Write the four-part sentence from the previous section on plain paper. Sign it. Do not sign it in blood and do not use a name that is not yours; the signature is a commitment to yourself, and blood is only a way of raising the emotional cost, not of raising the accuracy.
  2. Fold the paper once. Draw a sigil over the fold that encodes the sentence, not the outcome. The difference matters: an outcome-sigil encodes a result you do not have, and encoding a result is how you accidentally build a servitor that does nothing but sit there. A sentence-sigil encodes a set of instructions, and instructions are executable.
  3. Touch it to a candle flame without holding it in the flame. The heat is the activation and the near-miss is the point — you are marking a boundary, and the ritual has to be able to end.
  4. Say the sentence once, out loud, in your own voice. Not in a ceremonial register. The voice you use when you are telling a colleague what you are going to do by Thursday.
  5. Begin the action on the same day, within a quarter of an hour. This is the load-bearing step and it is the one everyone skips. The sigil is a marker that the action has started, not a substitute for it. A sigil charged on Monday with no action on Monday is a receipt for a purchase you never made.
  6. Repeat the marking daily for the full window you wrote down. Nothing more. No escalation, no new sigils, no second spirit, no expanded scope.
  7. On the date you wrote, read the four-part sentence out loud and read the number. Either outcome is data.

The seven-day version if you are not ready for the window

Some situations do not support a thirty-day window. A rent deadline, a mortgage adjustment, a decision that cannot wait. In those cases the structure compresses, and the compression is safe as long as the fourth part survives.

Shorten the sentence to the number and the date. Take the action the same day, within the quarter hour. Do not run the ritual more than once. Do not add anything to it later, and specifically do not respond to a disappointing result by escalating — a second spirit, a bigger sigil, a bloodier signature. Escalation after disappointment is the most common way a wealth working turns into a disaster, because the disappointment is being treated as a signal to increase effort rather than as a signal to inspect the sentence.

What actually changes: the mechanism, not the demon

Strip out the supernatural and three real mechanisms remain, and all three are well documented outside of magic.

The first is expectancy restructuring. A written, signed, dated commitment produces a measurable change in what you notice. You start seeing the openings that were always there and were never salient, because you are scanning for them. Nothing about the world changed. The sampling did.

The second is implementation intent. A plan that exists only as an intention is a plan with no trigger, and intentions decay fast. Writing the action into a physical object converts it from a preference into a scheduled item, and scheduled items are executed at a much higher rate than preferences. This is the most reliable effect in the whole structure and it has nothing supernatural in it whatsoever.

The third is the compression of the decision. A commitment made once, in advance, with the reasoning already done, removes the decision from the moment of action. People who are good at taking action are not usually more decisive at the moment of action; they have already decided, usually in private, days earlier.

Any practitioner who tells you the money appeared because a demon granted it has misdescribed their own process, and usually has not noticed the real cause, which means the technique will not work again for them either.

What failure looks like, and what to do about it

The failure modes of a wealth working are not mysterious and they are not karmic. They are four specific, recognisable patterns.

  • Obsession. The ritual becomes the focus and the action does not happen, or happens in fragments around it. This is the most common outcome by a wide margin, and it is not a spiritual failure. The ritual supplied emotional certainty and certainty is often worth more to the nervous system than the money. Fix: keep the daily marking to seven minutes, cap the total ritual time, and put the action first in the day so the ritual is a reward for having acted rather than a substitute for acting.
  • No movement on the number by the date. The number did not change, which means either the action was not specific enough to move it, or the action was not taken at the frequency you wrote. Fix: rewrite the sentence with a smaller number and a shorter window. A number that is too large is indistinguishable from a number that is impossible, and you cannot tell them apart from inside.
  • The guilt spiral. Shame, self-doubt, a conviction that a lack of money is a moral verdict. This is the usury prohibition being internalised as a personal failing, and it arrives with remarkable consistency within two weeks. Fix: the prohibition was never about your income. It was about extracting rent from someone worse off than you. If your action was ordinary work, ordinary selling, or ordinary asking, no such rule was engaged.
  • Escalation. The disappointment is interpreted as insufficient effort, so the working gets bigger. A second spirit is consulted, a larger sum is promised, the signature gets more expensive. Fix: stop. An escalation that begins with a disappointing result is being driven by the disappointment, not by information.

The real signs that a working is going well

Almost nobody checks the right indicators, so the obvious ones get ignored. Watch these, and watch them instead of the bank balance.

You start making the call you were avoiding. Not because you feel brave, but because the ritual has already made the decision and the remaining friction is administrative.

You notice openings you previously would have skipped. This is the expectancy mechanism becoming visible from the inside, and it is a real change even though nothing outside you has changed.

The catastrophising stops. This one is worth more than the money. Financial anxiety is a chronic prediction of future loss, and a working that has actually been carried out and actually followed through is hard to sustain catastrophising on top of. That reduction in dread is often the first durable result people notice, and it tends to show up before the number moves.

You are more willing to tell someone what you are trying to do. The shame falls away once there is a written sentence with your own signature on it, because a written sentence is a plan rather than a confession.

Objections, answered directly

"Isn't this entirely placebo?" Placebo is a real mechanism with real effect sizes, and calling it placebo does not make the outcome disappear. The brain executing a written commitment is a different machine from the brain mustering about one. Nothing in this structure depends on a demon arriving.

"Is calling it Mammon deceptive?" No, provided you know what you are doing, and this article is the reason you know. You are naming a traditional personification of accumulation so that the working has a handle to grip. Nothing is being contacted. If you wanted a contact, you would be doing a different operation with a different risk profile.

"Does this violate Christian teaching?" The prohibition is usury. It is a rule about lending at interest to someone with less, in an agricultural economy, written in a context of catastrophic harvest failure. It is not a prohibition on having money, and the parable's sin is the use of money to avoid a neighbour, not the possession of it.

"What about real demonic pacts?" There is no evidence for them, and the framing has a structural problem independent of the evidence. A binding contract is only ever as enforceable as the counterparty's interest in honouring it. Whatever you would be binding yourself to would gain nothing by honouring the agreement, which means the agreement has no counterparty. A pact with no counterparty is a promise to yourself with extra steps and a costume.

"Should I consult a teacher?" Only if you already know what you want, and only if the teacher will write down your four-part sentence. Any teacher who will not accept a specific, dated, measurable commitment is selling you a relationship rather than a technique.

The discipline that outlasts the ritual

Mammon is a name for accumulation, and accumulation is the thing the working is actually about. The strongest version of this practice is the one that stops being a ritual and becomes a rule.

Write the sentence. Name the number and the date. Take the action on the day, in the quarter hour, before anything else. Do not increase the ceremony when results disappoint. Do not consult anything larger the next time. Record the number on the date, whatever it says, and keep the record — a kept record is what turns three years of intermittent anxiety into a pattern you can actually see.

That rule, followed for a year, will outperform any pact in the grimoire, and it will cost you nothing. Everything else in this structure exists to get you to start the rule. Once the rule exists, the rest is optional, and the optional part is the part that can go wrong.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a Mammon pact satanic?

No. It has nothing to do with Satan, and the two get linked through a chain of nineteenth-century illustration work rather than any historical source. Mammon is a Biblical Aramaic term for accumulated wealth, attested in the Gospels and used in the Peshitta. What you are doing in a wealth working is a written, sigilised, dated commitment. No entity is contacted, invoked, or given anything. If a source tells you the opposite, that source has a different business model.

Do I have to sacrifice something to Mammon?

No. A working that requires a sacrifice is asking for money you do not have in a form you cannot spare, and the request will escalate until the refusal tells the practitioner what the technique was actually for. In this structure the only commitment is a specific action, repeated on a schedule you chose in advance, with a specific measurable outcome and a specific date. That is the whole obligation. If anyone tells you the price is higher than your written sentence, the price is the sale.

How long before I should expect a change?

Judge it by the number you wrote, on the date you wrote, and not before. Judging early is the standard failure, because an early judgement gets filled in by the anxiety the working was meant to reduce, and the anxiety is very convincing when you are looking straight at it. Track the shorter indicators first: whether you are making the call you were avoiding, whether you notice openings, whether the catastrophising has stopped. Those usually move inside two weeks. The number moves whenever the action moves it, which may be after the date, and that is also data.

Do I need a spirit, a sigil, or any occult material at all?

The sigil is the part with real function, and its function is mechanical: it is a compressed, single-glance version of the written instruction, and putting it somewhere you will see it converts an intention into a scheduled item. That conversion is the entire effect. A candle is convenient for the activation moment and does no work beyond that. A spirit is not required, and adding one is the most reliable way to make the working depend on your own interpretation of what a spirit wanted, which is the last thing you want when the aim was to reduce interpretation.

Can I work with Typhon instead?

Yes, and many practitioners find it cleaner. Typhon in the Golden Dawn system is the destructive, chthonic aspect of earth, which Crowley described as the elder form of that element. As a natural process rather than a personality, Typhon requires no negotiation and no counterparty, and he keeps operating after the ceremony ends, which is exactly what you want from a working about accumulation. The disadvantage is that Typhon is a harder figure to work with, because the destructive face of earth is not interested in your intentions. He is interested in what actually decays, so the working is better aimed at things that need to be broken down than at things that need to be built.
