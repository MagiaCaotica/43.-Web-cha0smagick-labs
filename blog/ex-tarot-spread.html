Why Ask the Tarot About an Ex?

After a breakup, the hardest question to answer is also the one that keeps you up at night: what does my ex think of me? Do they miss you? Are they seeing someone else? Is there a real chance of reconciliation, or are you holding onto something that is already gone?

Your friends will tell you to move on. Your brain will replay every memory on a loop. But the tarot cuts through the noise with a different kind of clarity: it reflects the emotional currents underneath the surface — the thoughts and feelings your ex may not even admit to themselves.

The five-card spread below is the most reliable layout for ex-related questions because it separates what you want to hear from what is actually happening. Each position answers one specific question, and together they form a complete picture of the situation.

How the 5-Card Ex Spread Works

This spread answers five precise questions in order:

  1. Their thoughts right now — what is actually on their mind about you.
  2. Their feelings toward you — the emotional truth, not the story they tell others.
  3. What they are not telling you — hidden motives, fears, or unspoken words.
  4. The obstacle between you — the real reason you are apart.
  5. The likely outcome — where this situation is heading over the next 3 months.

Shuffle with your left hand (the receptive hand) while holding the question in your mind. Cut the deck into three piles, then draw one card for each position from top to bottom. Do not look at them all at once — reveal one at a time and journal the interpretation before moving to the next.

Position-by-Position Interpretation

Here is how to read each position with the Rider-Waite deck:

  • Position 1 — Their thoughts: Wands mean they think about the passion and intensity of the relationship. Cups mean nostalgia and missing you. Swords mean they are overthinking, possibly justifying the breakup. Pentacles mean they are focused on practical matters (work, money, stability) more than on you.
  • Position 2 — Their feelings: Reversed cards here often indicate suppressed feelings — they feel something but refuse to act on it. The Two of Cups suggests genuine lingering affection; the Four of Swords suggests emotional exhaustion and distance.
  • Position 3 — What they hide: The Moon reversed can mean they are hiding doubt about the decision. The Page of Swords reversed often points to new gossip or someone else influencing them. The Hermit reversed may mean they are avoiding the real reason.
  • Position 4 — The obstacle: This is the card that tells the truth about why you broke up. If the obstacle is a Major Arcana card like the Tower, the rupture was fundamental. If it is a court card, a third person (family, friend, new partner) is involved.
  • Position 5 — The outcome: Cups and Wands in this position suggest the door is not closed. Swords and Pentacles suggest closure — the lesson is complete. Reversed Major Arcana cards usually mean the chapter is over.

Common Outcomes and What They Mean

Certain cards appear over and over in ex readings. Here is what they usually signal:

  • Two of Cups: There is still a connection worth exploring. Expect contact within weeks.
  • Death: Transformation, not literal death. The old relationship must end for something new — with them or without them.
  • Three of Swords reversed: The pain is healing on both sides. Reconciliation becomes possible once the hurt clears.
  • The Lovers reversed: A mismatch of values. The relationship cannot return to how it was — but a different kind of connection may be possible.
  • Ten of Cups: A strong indicator of reunion in the right conditions, especially with Cups surrounding it.

Red Flags to Watch For

The tarot is honest, and sometimes the answer is not what you want. Watch for these signals that the door is closed:

  • The Five of Cups repeatedly appearing — you are mourning a version of them that no longer exists.
  • Two or more Swords in positions 2 and 5 — the mind has already moved on even if the heart has not.
  • The Nine of Swords in the outcome position — the relationship causes more anxiety than joy; healing requires distance.
  • Court cards reversed in positions 3 and 4 — someone else is influencing the situation, and not in your favor.

Remember: a negative reading is not a curse. It is information. The tarot shows you where you stand so you can stop wasting energy on a closed door and redirect it toward what is actually possible.
