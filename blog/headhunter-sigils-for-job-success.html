Headhunter

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 27, 2026 ⬢ 8 min read

Career outcomes are a strange target for this kind of practice, and the strangeness is instructive. Unlike a sigil for money, a sigil for a job does not point at something you control. It points at a chain of other people's decisions, most of whom will never know you exist. That makes career work a poor target for external causation and a good target for the thing the practice actually does well, which is convert a vague intention into a specific behaviour you repeat under low motivation. This guide covers the servitor-for-a-task pattern, why implementation intentions map so precisely onto sigil commitment, how to aim a study working at a schedule rather than at memory, and where the consent line sits when clients and employers are involved.

A target you can only half control

Every hiring decision, every contract, and every client enquiry passes through somebody else's head. That is the structural difference between a career working and a money working, and it should be settled before the sigil is drawn rather than discovered afterwards in a disappointing quarter.

The consequence is that the controllable portion has to be specified precisely. Applications sent, conversations had, portfolio pieces published, follow-ups made, referrals asked for. These are yours. The interview outcome is not. A career working that does not name the controllable portion is a wish, and a wish produces a charge followed by a fortnight of waiting.

The servitor for one repeated task

Assigning a persistent thoughtform to one repetitive job is among the most frequently documented practices in modern chaos magic, and the reason it recurs is that it works within the same limits as everything else here. A thoughtform attached to a specific recurring task makes the task more likely to be done without negotiation.

The design constraints are tight. One task, not a category. A trigger, not an intention. You want a servitor that handles client follow-ups on Tuesday mornings, not one that brings you abundance. Vague assignments produce vague behaviour, and vague behaviour is indistinguishable from not having done the practice at all.

The if-then shape that behavioural work supports

Implementation intentions, phrased as if situation X arises then I will do Y, are among the most replicated findings in behavioural psychology. The mechanism appears to be that specifying the trigger removes the need to make a decision in the moment, when motivation is least reliable.

This is exactly the structure a sigil provides. The glyph stands for the if, the ritual of charging stands for the then, and the fortnight of ordinary work is where the plan either gets executed or does not. Practitioners who find sigils more effective when they follow the charge with a written if-then statement are not adding an unrelated technique. They are filling in the half of the mechanism the glyph left out.

Choosing a target that is actually yours

Write out the goal, then go through it clause by clause and mark who decides each part. Sentences that resolve to another person's decision are out of scope. Sentences that resolve to you are in. What survives that filter is usually a short list, and it is often shorter than the practitioner expected.

For a freelance career it might be three published pieces and six conversations with past clients. For someone in permanent employment it might be the internal project, the professional registration, the conference talk. Both are workable. Aiming at the promotion itself is not, because the promotion is somebody else's signature on somebody else's schedule.

Drafting the working, sentence first

Draft in the imperative, with a number and a deadline. I will send eight applications before the fifteenth. I will ask three former colleagues for a referral this week. I will publish the piece on Thursday. Specificity at this stage is not mysticism, it is contract drafting.

Then reduce to a glyph, charge, and write the trigger conditions down separately. The sigil carries the intention, the trigger list carries the execution, and keeping them apart means you can revise the triggers when the plan changes without invalidating the charge. Practitioners who fuse the two rebuild everything every time the week goes sideways.

Acquiring clients, and where the ethics sit

Client acquisition is the case where the consent question gets sharp, and it deserves a clear rule. A sigil aimed at your own follow-up rate, your own visibility, or your own portfolio is aimed at you. A sigil aimed at a named client's decision to hire you is aimed at them, and they have not agreed to it.

The distinction is not always obvious, because career outcomes are downstream of other people. The working test is whether the named party would describe the attempt as acceptable if you told them about it. If they would, the aim is arguably defensible as a way of organising your own behaviour. If the honest answer is that they would be uncomfortable, keep the name out of the sigil and put the effort into the portfolio instead.

Study aims, and the wrong kind of goal

A sigil aimed at better memory performs worse than one aimed at a specific study schedule, and the reason is that memory is an outcome while a schedule is a behaviour. Distributed practice and retrieval practice are among the most reliable findings in the whole of the learning sciences, and neither of them requires occult involvement to work.

So aim at the schedule. I will sit for three blocks of forty minutes on Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday. I will test myself on Sunday rather than rereading. The sigil is not doing anything for your recall. It is making a commitment that you will probably break on the day you least want to, which is exactly the day a fixed trigger helps.

Calendar, timer, and the hours worth protecting

Two instruments earn their place here. A calendar with the study or application blocks written in before the week starts, and a timer for the blocks themselves. Everything else in the career working is optional ceremony and ceremony is fine as long as it does not replace the block.

The block has to be defended like a meeting, which means it goes in the calendar before optional work claims the time. Career practice consistently fails at this specific point. People protect their morning because mornings are habitually protected, and they do not protect Thursday afternoon because Thursday afternoon is where everything else goes.

The follow-through that makes the glyph true

The fortnight after the charge is the working. A glyph with nothing behind it is a drawing. This is the least interesting part of the practice to write about and it is the part that determines whether any of the material was worth the time.

Keep a one-line-per-day log of the specific actions, because the diagnosis depends on it. Ten applications and no replies is a targeting or CV problem. Thirty conversations and no second call is a pitch problem. Nothing sent at all is a commitment problem. Without the log you have the outcome and no way to act on it.

Signals that beat a good month

A single good month tells you very little, because hiring and contract cycles are noisy and outcome is dominated by factors you did not control. The signals worth trusting are process signals that you can attribute: the application rate held, the follow-ups went out, the piece got published, the conversation count rose.

The other signal worth trusting is a change in the quality of conversations rather than the quantity. Two referrals that produce real discussions beat twenty cold applications, and a practice that produces the first is working even when the quarter is quiet. Log that separately, because it is the leading indicator and the bank balance is the lagging one.

What to do with a no

A rejection is data with a specific shape, and treating it as a verdict on the working is how people end up abandoning a method that was functioning. The question worth asking is which part of the chain produced the no. Timing, screener, pitch, fit, or budget are five different problems with five different fixes.

If the ledger shows the actions were executed and the outcomes were poor, the working did its job and the strategy needs work. If the ledger is sparse, the sigil has been carrying a load it was never designed for. Diagnose the chain, not the spell, and rewrite the target clause that was pointing at somebody else's decision.

Keeping a servitor from becoming a nag

A task-servitor that keeps firing when the task is already done becomes a source of low-grade friction, and people respond to friction by abandoning the practice. Give it an end condition. This quarter, this client, this study block. When the condition is met, retire it deliberately and note the retirement.

The same applies to the record. Career practice generates a great deal of information about other people, and a log full of named prospects that never converted is a small liability if it is ever seen. Keep the counts, drop the names once a thread is closed, and the record stays useful without becoming a dossier on people who did not know they were in it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a sigil help me get a job?

The part of the process you control is real and the part you do not control is not yours to influence. A working aimed at your application rate, your published work, or your follow-up habits is a commitment device and it functions. A working aimed at a specific employer's decision is an attempt to act on somebody else's mind, and there is no evidence that a glyph does that.

What is a servitor for a task, in practice?

A thoughtform assigned to one specific recurring job, with a trigger attached. Something like handling client follow-ups every Tuesday morning, or reviewing flashcard material after lunch. The design rules are one task, one trigger, and a stated end condition. Assignments framed as broad categories produce no observable behaviour change and are indistinguishable from not doing the practice.

Should my study sigil aim at memory or at a schedule?

At a schedule. Memory is an outcome that depends on the schedule, and distributed practice with retrieval is what actually moves it. A sigil for better memory gives you nothing to do on a Tuesday. A sigil for three forty-minute blocks on Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday gives you a commitment you can execute or fail to execute, which is the level at which the practice operates.

How do I know whether the working failed or the market did?

Look at the process log rather than the outcome. If the actions were executed at the specified rate, the working functioned and the strategy or the market is the variable to examine. If the actions were not executed, the working is irrelevant to the result. Keeping the one-line-per-day action log is the only reliable way to tell these two situations apart after the fact.

Is it unethical to sigil for a specific client or employer?

It is an attempt to influence a decision you do not control, made by someone who has not agreed to it, which makes it ethically fraught in this tradition's own terms. Aiming at your own visibility, your own follow-up rate, or your own portfolio avoids the problem entirely and is usually more effective, because the controllable portion is where the actual leverage is.
