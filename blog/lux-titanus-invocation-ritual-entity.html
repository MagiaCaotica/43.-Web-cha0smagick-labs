LUX TITANUS

By Frater Alek0s ⬢ September 27, 2026 ⬢ 9 min read

This is a record of a first working with LUX TITANUS written for its failures rather than its successes, because the failures are what a beginner can actually use. Nothing dramatic happened. The office was too wide, the first charge produced nothing, the second charge produced a coincidence that could not honestly be counted, and the only real change was in how the notebook was written. That is what entity work mostly consists of, and the practitioner who expects a dramatic first month tends to abandon the practice before the boring part starts. The four ideas underneath are standard: evocation asks for appearance, invocation asks for influence through the operator, and most modern usage collapses the distinction. The Ars Goetia supplies rank, form, office and powers for each of seventy-two spirits, which is why it remains the most structured entity catalogue in the field. Modern chaos practice treats those entities as independent pattern fragments. Carroll's claim was that they are facets of the practitioner rather than separate beings, and that last position is what makes a field-notes approach possible, because it removes the question of whether a disappointing result was a refusal.

What happened in the first week

The charge was made on a Thursday, in the evening, in a room with the light off. The office as first written was a single sentence, which was already a mistake, and the sentence was about turning difficult work into flow. The sigil was drawn twice and charged five times, and the whole thing took about forty minutes including the notes.

The first week produced nothing that survived the notebook. There was a period of slightly elevated work on the Friday, which could have been sleep, could have been the effect of having written something down, and was certainly not distinguishable from either. Writing it in the log as a yes would have been the first lie of the record, so it was written as a blank and left there.

The paperwork, and why it matters

The charge page had four lines: the name, the office sentence, the date, the count. That is the minimum and it is more than most first workings record. The value of it appeared two months later, when the question of whether to continue could not be answered from memory and could be answered from the page in about a minute.

Memory is the wrong instrument for this because memory reconstructs. Reconstructed accounts of the early weeks drift toward a narrative in which something worked, and the drift is not deliberate. A four-line page cannot drift, which is why the habit is worth the small tedium of keeping it. The date matters most, because without it you cannot tell a two-month gap from a two-week one.

The charge that produced nothing

Two weeks later, a second charge with a revised sigil. The revision was cosmetic, which is worth naming as a mistake: nothing about the office had changed, so the new sigil encoded the same vague target with different line crossings, and the result was the same absence. The charge itself was done well. Attention was on it. The count was kept.

This is the ordinary shape of failure in entity work and it is almost never about the quality of the ritual. It is about the target. Flow is not an action you take, so there was nothing for a commitment mechanism to attach to, and a commitment mechanism with nothing to hold produces exactly this: a clean, well-attended, result-free session.

The office that had to be rewritten

The rewrite happened in a single sitting at the end of month one, and it followed a simple rule. The new sentence had to begin with a verb naming something physically done at a desk, and it had to be completable in under ten minutes. The version that finally went on the page was: open the hardest file, write one paragraph, stop.

The change is large and it is the whole of the learning here. The original office asked for a state, which is something you either have or do not. The revision asks for an action, which is something you can fail to do, and failing to do it produces information. A working that cannot fail is a working that cannot be evaluated, and an unevaluable working is indistinguishable from a ritual with no content in it.

Two things that felt like contact

Two events in the second month were logged as felt contact, and both are ambiguous. One was a sentence arriving during an unrelated conversation that turned out to be usable, which is a common experience and has an ordinary explanation in salience rather than in anything else. The other was a dream with a structure that matched the office, which is the kind of event that people report often and that no procedure has ever made more likely than chance.

Both were written down with the word ambiguous beside them, which is the most useful annotation available. The failure mode in entity practice is not reporting ambiguous things as confirmations, it is refusing to write them down at all because they seem too subjective to record. Both errors produce a record that cannot be audited. The annotation keeps them in the log without counting them.

Sleep, and the honest confusion about it

The most reliably reported effect across the whole period was a change in sleep, and it is the one that prompted the most doubt. Sleep better on the nights following a charge is extremely common as a reported experience and there is no reason to think it is anything other than expectation plus the fact that an evening was spent sitting still before bed.

It was logged anyway, every night, as a one to five. Keeping the log was worth more than any interpretation of it, because the numbers eventually showed the change stopped after the charges stopped, which is the single most useful piece of information in the record. A practice that helps you sleep only when you are doing it is a practice you have to keep doing, and that is a different proposition from one that has altered something.

A failure worth writing down

The most instructive entry in the notebook was a week of perfect execution with no result at all. Every planned session happened. The log was complete. Nothing in the world changed in a way that could be identified, and the only difference from a successful month was that the aim had drifted back to a state rather than an action somewhere around week five.

Drift is the characteristic failure and it does not announce itself. It feels like persistence, and the difference between persistence and drift is only visible afterwards in the log, where the aims across weeks are written in the same hand and plainly not the same aim. Write the aim at the top of every log page. The repetition is what makes the drift legible.

Narrowing the working after month two

The narrowing was done by subtraction rather than by inspiration. The name stayed. The dramatic form was dropped, since nothing had ever depended on it. The evocation-versus-invocation question was settled in favour of invocation on the grounds that it produced less to interpret. And the office was reduced to the single desk action, unchanged for two further months.

Reducing to one action for two months feels excessive and is the point. A narrower target is easier to fail, which means the log fills with real information rather than impressions. Everything that got cut was also cut from the sigil, since a sigil for three aims is a sigil for the one you care about least, and keeping the unused parts in the notebook is what made the cuts reversible rather than regrettable.

The second charge, smaller

The second real charge used a smaller sigil, a count of three rather than five, and no additions. It was done immediately after writing the office on a fresh page, in daylight, with no ritual apparatus at all, because none of the apparatus had ever been doing anything and carrying it was costing attention that the desk action needed.

The smaller charge felt like less and cost a quarter of the time. Nothing in the record indicates that the first was more effective, which is a useful negative result: the record now contains a comparison, and the comparison says the expensive version was not better. Nobody would have learned that without having run both.

What the field notes cannot settle

Three questions stayed open and will probably stay open. Whether the desk action happened more often because of the working or because the log made it visible, which is unanswerable without a control condition nobody has. Whether the entity is anything at all, which the pattern-fragment view makes irrelevant to the practice. And whether the effect, if there is one, is distinguishable from the general effect of writing a specific sentence down repeatedly.

That third question is the one that matters and it is the one most practitioners skip. The honest position is that a sigil is a compression device and a repeated sentence is a commitment device, and both of those have well-understood effects that require no external agency. That position does not make the practice pointless. It makes the parts of it that are worth keeping identifiable.

Grounding, and why it came first

Grounding is usually presented as something to do afterwards, after an intense working, to come back down. It is more useful at the start. A few minutes of naming the room, the hour, the physical facts of where you are, and what you will do next, before the charge, is what keeps a working from bleeding into the rest of the day.

The field notes make the case themselves. Every session that skipped it produced notes that were longer than the session, which is a sign the state lasted too long. Every session that included it produced four lines. The practical effect is that the practice stayed small, and a practice that stays small is one you can still be doing in five years, which is the only durability criterion that has ever mattered here.

The decision to keep going anyway

The decision was made from the log rather than from the feeling, and the reasoning was narrow. The practice cost about fifteen minutes a week. It produced one measurable change in behaviour and one plausible-sounding ambiguity. Nothing in it was coercive, and the aims all sat inside the practitioner. On those grounds it continued.

That is the whole of the justification, and it is a weaker justification than most practitioners offer. It is also the one that survives contact with someone else, which matters, because a practice defended on grounds that only make sense from inside the state will eventually be defended harder than the evidence supports. LUX TITANUS remains in the notebook, charged twice a month, with the office sentence copied at the top of every page.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a disappointing first working evidence the entity refused?

On the pattern-fragment view that most modern practice uses, there is no entity to refuse, so a disappointing result is a technical problem with the aim rather than a message. That view is useful precisely because it gives you something to change. The first rewrite is usually to convert a state you want into an action you take, because only the second can fail informatively.

Should I record ambiguous experiences like dreams or coincidences?

Yes, annotated as ambiguous. The failure is not reporting something as a confirmation, it is refusing to write it down because it seems too subjective. Annotated entries keep the material in the record without letting it accumulate into a story, and the annotation is what stops a later reader, including you, from upgrading it into evidence.

How much does ritual apparatus matter?

Less than the cost of carrying it suggests. In this record the apparatus never did anything measurable, and the sessions with no apparatus at all were shorter and produced cleaner notes. A timer and a separate notebook earn their place because they change what you record. A talisman, a specific moon phase and a candle do not, unless you want them for reasons you would be honest about.

What if the practice only helps while I keep doing it?

Then it is a practice you have to keep doing, which is not a failure but it is a different proposition from having altered something. The useful diagnostic is to check whether an effect persists through a deliberate gap. A change in sleep that stops when the charges stop was never an alteration; it was a condition of the routine, and knowing which one you have tells you whether to keep going.

How do I stop the aim drifting back to a state?

Write the aim at the top of every log page, in the same words each time. Drift does not announce itself and feels like persistence while it happens. The repetition makes it legible afterwards, when the pages can be read against each other, and a log with that repetition is the only practical instrument for detecting it before month four.
