The three levels: how to read any sentence in this book

Every statement about how a servitor is made, kept, or ended appears under one of three labels. This is the fastest way to use the volume: find the claim you care about, read its label, and you know how much weight it can carry.

1. Operational claim

A directive. Do this, in this order, for this result. These are the sentences you can follow directly and they make up the working core of the book â€” the creation procedure, the maintenance schedule, the closing sequence, the banishing protocol.

An operation claim is something like: sequence the activation so that the charge is built before the name is fixed, not after. You can act on that this evening. Whether the universe takes it up is a separate question, and the book files that separately.

2. Empirical claim

A statement about outcomes, with the evidence it rests on and the limits of that evidence attached. The book is explicit when the evidence is thin, anecdotal, or absent, and it does not dress a report of one practitioner's experience as though it were a finding.

An empirical claim is something like: operators report that servitors with an unresolved loop persist longer than clean ones â€” and here is what the sample actually supports. Read the strength of the evidence, because the book states it.

3. Interpretive claim

A reading, an interpretation, a symbolic account. This is where most grimoires put their best prose, and it is also the part that cannot be settled by evidence because it was never an empirical claim to begin with. The book marks it so you never mistake a beautiful reading for a demonstrated result.

An interpretive claim is something like: the number of legs on the constructed figure encodes the number of responsibilities the servitor will accept. Enjoy it. Use it if it works. Do not cite it.

Why this matters more than it sounds: most servitor literature is written entirely at level three and presented as level one. A book that labels its own claims lets you build the practice without inheriting the confusion.
